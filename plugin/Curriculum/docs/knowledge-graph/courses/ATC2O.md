# Dance (ATC2O)

**Dance (ATC2O)** is a Grade 10 open course in [The Arts](../subjects/the-arts.md).

This course emphasizes the development of students’ technique and creative skills relating to the elements of dance and the tools of composition in a variety of performance situations. Students will identify responsible personal and interpersonal practices related to dance processes and production, and will apply technologies and techniques throughout the process of creation to develop artistic scope in the dance arts.

## Before you take this course

This course has no formal prerequisites.

## Where this course leads

Completing this course opens the door to [Dance (ATC3M)](ATC3M.md).

## What students learn

The learning is organized into 3 strands: Creating, Presenting, and Performing, Reflecting, Responding, and Analysing, and Foundations.

### A. Creating, Presenting, and Performing

*By the end of this course, students will:*

**A1 The Creative Process.** use the creative process, the elements of dance, and a variety of sources to develop [movement vocabulary](../glossary/movement-vocabulary.md)

- use the elements of dance to create and perform a variety of movement [phrases](../glossary/phrase-e35737c7.md) inspired by sources (e.g., select key words, images, and messages from the poem “My Name Is Gossip” and interpret them through a variety of movement phrases; create a short dance composition based on a personal, social, or environmental issue)
- create and perform phrases that manipulate three or more elements of dance (e.g., with a partner, create movement phrases in three-quarter [time](../glossary/time-06dc6394.md), juxtaposing symmetrical and asymmetrical shapes; repeat their phrases, adding an element of [energy](../glossary/energy-7b16847d.md) \[fast, slow, light, or strained\] and focusing on the relationship between the two movers)
- use the elements of dance to create and perform movement vocabulary through [guided improvisation](../glossary/guided-improvisation-cb59114f.md) (e.g., use [flocking](../glossary/flocking.md) to explore slow-motion movement, changes of [levels](../glossary/level-c6cc7954.md), and changes of direction/facings)
- develop multiple solutions to movement problems following specific guidelines for performance (e.g., vary a sixteen-bar movement phrase by exploring it first as a stationary phrase and then as a locomotor phrase; introduce two [pathway](../glossary/pathway-3202031d.md) changes to help emphasize or reconfigure particular movements within their phrases)

<sub>The source curriculum adds 4 teacher prompts, examples, and instructional notes for these expectations.</sub>

**A2 Choreography and Composition.** combine the elements of dance in a variety of ways in composing individual and [ensemble](../glossary/ensemble.md) dance creations

- use a variety of [choreographic forms](../glossary/choreographic-form-c7a44190.md), structures, and techniques to connect a series of movement phrases (e.g., rearrange a phrase learned in class and perform it in a tight clump in unison; [retrograde](../glossary/retrograde.md) a second phrase and perform it as a [canon](../glossary/canon.md), then return to the first phrase and repeat to reveal an [ABA](../glossary/aba.md) structure)
- construct a dance composition inspired by a source (e.g., create a dance composition inspired by a two- or three-dimensional art work, or a musical excerpt or poem)
- use a variety of compositional approaches to extend their ability to express ideas through dance (e.g., use approaches such as guided improvisation, responding to the [stimulus](../glossary/stimulus.md) of a poem or a social or environmental issue, or varying and repeating phrases in ABA form)

<sub>The source curriculum adds 3 teacher prompts, examples, and instructional notes for these expectations.</sub>

**A3 Dance Techniques.** demonstrate an understanding of the dance techniques and movement vocabularies of a variety of dance forms from around the world

- apply a [kinesthetic awareness](../glossary/kinesthetic-awareness.md) of the vocabularies within a variety of dance forms from around the world (e.g., explore and practise basic movements from ballet, modern dance, Afro-Brazilian dance such as samba, and modern/jazz dance \[as conceived by dance creators such as Gus Giordiano, Reginald Ray Savage, Wade Robson, Eva Von Gencsy, Vicki Adam-Willis\])
- accurately reproduce a given spatial [pattern](../glossary/pattern.md) or pathway (e.g., perform a tendu exercise en croix with a partner and provide feedback to help the partner make corrections; repeat the exercise noting improvements)
- arrange and present extended sequences using the dance vocabulary and techniques of an identified dance form (e.g., rearrange and extend a ballet centre combination by adding familiar stationary and travelling ballet steps)

<sub>The source curriculum adds 3 teacher prompts, examples, and instructional notes for these expectations.</sub>

**A4 Performance.** apply dance presentation skills in a variety of contexts and performances

- revise and refine movement to enhance dance performance and interpretation (e.g., revisit already created phrases to clarify the movement, energy, pathways, formations, and counts and timing)
- use the tools of stagecraft in dance performances, including performances at alternative venues (e.g., suggest solutions for staging problems presented by an alternative or unconventional performance environment, such as an outdoor site)
- apply an understanding of the importance of stage presence in rehearsal and performance (e.g., maintain focus and alertness throughout their performance)

<sub>The source curriculum adds 3 teacher prompts, examples, and instructional notes for these expectations.</sub>


*Transferable skills developed in this strand: Critical Thinking & Problem Solving, Innovation, Creativity & Entrepreneurship, Communication, Global Citizenship and Sustainability, Digital Literacy.*

### B. Reflecting, Responding, and Analysing

*By the end of this course, students will:*

**B1 The Critical Analysis Process.** use the critical analysis process to reflect on and evaluate their own and others’ dance works and activities

- use the critical analysis process to identify and compare a wide variety of dance forms (e.g., compare features of the dance forms experienced in the Grade 10 dance program)
- analyse dance works in terms of both their content and their [fluency](../glossary/fluency-93f37146.md), artistry, or expressiveness (e.g., describe the individual movement combinations in a dance piece and determine whether they have been put together effectively to create a successful work)

<sub>The source curriculum adds 2 teacher prompts, examples, and instructional notes for these expectations.</sub>

**B2 Dance and Society.** demonstrate an understanding of how societies present and past use or have used dance, and of how creating and viewing dance can benefit individuals, groups, and communities

- describe the characteristics and function of a world dance form (e.g., an oppression dance such as gumboot)
- explain how dance exploration can contribute to personal growth and self-understanding (e.g., how they have benefited from listening to feedback or trying to respond to constructive criticism)
- identify and describe ways in which different types of dance reflect the cultures that produced them (e.g., Kathak expresses devotion to Hindu gods; the hoop dance reflects Aboriginal beliefs about how all living things on the earth grow, change, and are connected)
- demonstrate an understanding of how dance exploration and presentations can contribute to the school and the broader community (e.g., use visual, oral, or written means to describe school, community, and professional dance productions they have attended or presented, and explain how they and others benefited from the   experience)

<sub>The source curriculum adds 4 teacher prompts, examples, and instructional notes for these expectations.</sub>

**B3 Connections Beyond the Classroom.** demonstrate an understanding of the purpose and possibilities of continuing engagement in dance arts

- identify physical, intellectual, and artistic skills that are developed through dance and explain how they can be applied to a variety of careers (e.g., with a partner, research and report on possible summer volunteer or employment opportunities where their learning in dance could be helpful)
- identify and describe ways in which dance arts could play a greater role in their community (e.g., identify groups in the community who might benefit from dance activities but who are not currently served by dance organizations)
- develop and maintain a traditional or digital portfolio to document and analyse their dance-related learning in a variety of ways (e.g., through photographs, ticket stubs, programs, videos, recorded or written personal reflections), and use it to analyse their strengths and areas that need improvement

*Transferable skills developed in this strand: Critical Thinking & Problem Solving, Self-Directed Learning, Communication, Global Citizenship and Sustainability, Innovation, Creativity & Entrepreneurship.*

### C. Foundations

*By the end of this course, students will:*

**C1 Physiology and Terminology.** demonstrate an understanding of, and use correct terminology when referring to, the physiology of movement as it relates to dance

- demonstrate an understanding of the importance of a positive body image and a healthy lifestyle to their learning in dance (e.g., explain how self-esteem and positive nutritional choices can enhance their learning and development in dance; create a [collage](../glossary/collage-c7d1ddb0.md) of magazine images that represent stereotypes of beauty)
- demonstrate an understanding of basic muscular anatomy as it relates to movement (e.g., outline the  characteristics and functions of cardiac muscle, smooth muscle, and skeletal muscle)
- demonstrate, and describe using correct terminology, the movement repertoire of a variety of dance forms from around the world (e.g., movements associated with breakdance, such as uprock, [freeze](../glossary/freeze-47c5db25.md), rollback, six-step, zulu spins)

<sub>The source curriculum adds 3 teacher prompts, examples, and instructional notes for these expectations.</sub>

**C2 Contexts and Influences.** demonstrate an understanding of the social, cultural, and historical origins and development of dance forms, including their influence on each other and on society

- identify and describe the contributions of some dance pioneers to particular dance forms (e.g., Martha Graham’s influence on modern dance; Loie Fuller’s contribution to modern dance and dance on film; Luigi’s contribution to jazz dance)
- identify and describe ways in which dance addresses social questions of local and/or global interest (e.g., explain how the choreography of a dance work on a social justice or environmental theme helps communicate the intended message)
- identify and describe similarities and differences in some dance genres from around the world, and illustrate them through performance (e.g., compare the use of ritual as a key element in two or more dance genres; use a concept attainment chart to compare the key features of various dance genres)

<sub>The source curriculum adds 3 teacher prompts, examples, and instructional notes for these expectations.</sub>

**C3 Responsible Practices.** demonstrate an understanding of safe, ethical, and responsible personal and interpersonal practices in dance activities

- identify and follow responsible practices in dance activities (e.g., explain the need for and demonstrate discipline in daily dance practice; identify and carry out the responsibilities of assigned roles; respect the boundaries between their own and others’ roles)
- demonstrate problem-solving skills during rehearsal and performance (e.g., find ways to work constructively with others; explore a variety of possibilities to find solutions to artistic problems)
- identify and follow safe and ethical practices in dance activities in both classroom and performance settings (e.g., maintain appropriate distances; use equipment safely; refrain from displays of temperament and respond constructively to “acting out” by others; develop a class definition of choreographic property; identify  and  acknowledge copyrighted material)

<sub>The source curriculum adds 3 teacher prompts, examples, and instructional notes for these expectations.</sub>


*Transferable skills developed in this strand: Critical Thinking & Problem Solving, Communication, Self-Directed Learning, Global Citizenship and Sustainability, Innovation, Creativity & Entrepreneurship.*
