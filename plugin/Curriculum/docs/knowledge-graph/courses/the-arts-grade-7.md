# The Arts (Grade 7)

**The Arts** is the Grade 7 The Arts program in [The Arts](../subjects/the-arts.md).

Here you will find an overview of student development and learning in the arts at the intermediate level, as well as the expectations and learning for the four areas of study for Grade 7.

## Before you take this course

This course has no formal prerequisites.

## What students learn

The learning is organized into 4 strands: Dance, Drama, Music, and Visual Arts.

### A. Dance

*By the end of Grade 7, students will:*

**A1 Creating and Presenting.** apply the creative process to the composition of a variety of [dance pieces](../glossary/dance-piece.md), using the [elements of dance](../glossary/elements-of-dance.md) to communicate feelings and ideas

- create dance pieces to represent or respond to specific rhythms and pieces of music (e.g., use the [body](../glossary/body.md), body parts, and the floor \[stamping, stepping, body slapping\] to replicate the rhythms in the music; transform a music imaging exercise into a dance interpretation)
- use dance as a language to communicate ideas from their own writing or media works (e.g., create a dance piece inspired by a student authored poem about relationships with the natural world or by a student media work about divorce or loss)
- use [theme and variations](../glossary/theme-and-variation.md) in a variety of ways when creating dance pieces (e.g., create a simple movement [phrase](../glossary/phrase.md) \[theme\] and then repeat it in modified form \[variation\] using choreographic [manipulations](../glossary/manipulations.md) \[retrograding the original phrase, facing another dancer, adding more dancers\])
- use the elements of dance and [choreographic forms](../glossary/choreographic-form.md) (e.g., [pattern forms](../glossary/pattern-form.md), [narrative forms](../glossary/narrative-form.md)) to communicate a variety of themes or moods (e.g., use entrances or exits to communicate beginnings or endings; use a recurring sequence of movements to signal a particular mood or character; use [canon](../glossary/canon.md) form for emphasis)

<sub>The source curriculum adds 4 teacher prompts, examples, and instructional notes for these expectations.</sub>

**A2 Reflecting, Responding, and Analysing.** apply the critical analysis process to communicate their feelings, ideas, and understandings in response to a variety of dance pieces and experiences

- construct personal interpretations of the messages in their own and others’ dance pieces, including messages about issues relevant to their community and/or the world (e.g., dance pieces on topics such as urban sprawl, land claims, poverty, homophobia, homelessness), and communicate their responses in a variety of ways (e.g., through writing, class discussion, oral reports, song, drama, visual art)
- analyse, using dance vocabulary, their own and others’ dance pieces to identify the elements of dance and the choreographic forms used in them and explain how they help communicate meaning (e.g., use of crouching shapes low to the ground and [bound energy](../glossary/bound-movement.md) communicates the idea of confined space; use of [site-specific](../glossary/site-specific.md) locations \[outdoor playground\] to structure a dance communicates the idea of connection to the environment)
- identify and give examples of their strengths and areas for growth as dance creators, interpreters, and audience members (e.g., share with a partner what they did well during a performance, using dance vocabulary; use a concept map to explain their choice of dance movements)

<sub>The source curriculum adds 3 teacher prompts, examples, and instructional notes for these expectations.</sub>

**A3 Exploring Forms and Cultural Contexts.** demonstrate an understanding of a variety of dance forms, traditions, and styles from the past and present, and their sociocultural and historical contexts

- describe the evolution of dance and performance as different groups of people have responded to external factors such as migration, a new environment, and/or contact with other groups or cultures (e.g., the evolution of Maritime Acadian folk dances into Louisiana Cajun dances such as fais do do and the Mardi Gras dance Krewes; the origins and development of French and Scottish jigs; the evolution of the Métis jig out of imitations of wildlife movements \[prairie wild birds\] and the intricate footwork of Native dancing and European jigs)
- identify ways in which dance and its depictions in the media may influence a person’s character development and sense of identity (e.g., by influencing young people’s sense of themselves and their bodies; by providing dance role models who represent or promote particular lifestyles, values, and attitudes)

<sub>The source curriculum adds 2 teacher prompts, examples, and instructional notes for these expectations.</sub>


*Transferable skills developed in this strand: Critical Thinking & Problem Solving, Innovation, Creativity & Entrepreneurship, Communication, Self-Directed Learning, Global Citizenship and Sustainability.*

### B. Drama

*By the end of Grade 7, students will:*

**B1 Creating and Presenting.** apply the creative process to [process drama](../glossary/process-drama.md) and the development of [drama works](../glossary/dramatic-works.md), using the [elements](../glossary/elements-of-drama.md) and [conventions of drama](../glossary/conventions-of-drama.md) to communicate feelings, ideas, and multiple perspectives

- engage actively in [drama exploration](../glossary/dramatic-exploration.md) and [role play](../glossary/role-playing-role-play.md), with a focus on examining multiple perspectives related to current issues, themes, and relationships from a wide variety of sources and diverse communities (e.g., identify significant perspectives related to an issue such as peer pressure, treaty rights, or cultural identity, and assume roles to express the different perspectives; use [prepared improvisation](../glossary/prepared-improvisation.md) to communicate insights about life events and relationships; use [thought tracking](../glossary/thought-tracking.md) and symbolic [artefacts](../glossary/artefacts.md) to present a persona associated with a past historical event)
- demonstrate an understanding of the elements of drama by selecting and combining several elements and conventions to create dramatic effects (e.g., develop a drama presentation incorporating a series of [tableaux](../glossary/tableau.md), a group [soundscape](../glossary/soundscape-sound-collage.md), a movement piece, and a rap/song)
- plan and shape the direction of the drama by working with others, both in and [out of role](../glossary/out-of-role.md), to generate ideas and explore multiple perspectives (e.g., [In role](../glossary/in-role.md): use thought tracking or writing in role to explore the feelings and motivations of a character; introduce a new perspective during role play to foster a sense of [empathy](../glossary/empathy-empathize.md) with the character; Out of role: use a [place mat](../glossary/place-mat.md) activity to select ideas that group members agree upon; use [invented notation](../glossary/invented-notation.md) to explain the movement of the character)
- communicate feelings, thoughts, and abstract ideas through drama works, using audio, visual, and/or technological aids to heighten the dramatic experience (e.g., use music to create [mood](../glossary/mood.md); use video and drums/noisemakers to signal the climax; use a digital slide [presentation](../glossary/presentation.md) to create a backdrop of words or images; use costumes, props, fabric to establish character and/or setting)

<sub>The source curriculum adds 4 teacher prompts, examples, and instructional notes for these expectations.</sub>

**B2 Reflecting, Responding, and Analysing.** apply the critical analysis process to communicate feelings, ideas, and understandings in response to a variety of drama works and experiences

- construct personal interpretations of drama works, connecting drama issues and themes to their own and others’ ideas, feelings, and experiences (e.g., use a series of tableaux or [freeze-frame images](../glossary/freeze-frame-image.md) of key moments in a drama to show which moments had the greatest impact on them; write in role about an environmental issue, first from the point of view of an audience member and then from the point of view of an animal whose habitat is threatened)
- analyse and describe, using drama terminology, how drama elements are used to communicate meaning in a variety of drama works and [shared drama experiences](../glossary/shared-drama-experience.md) (e.g., compare and contrast how the director of a [play](../glossary/play.md) and the director of a film might use [body positioning](../glossary/body-position.md) and sound to communicate a character’s feelings to the audience)
- identify and give examples of their strengths, interests, and areas for improvement as drama creators, performers, and audience members (e.g., create a chart listing strengths and areas for improvement; highlight an area to work on in their next drama production; write a report on their learning in drama for a school newsletter)

<sub>The source curriculum adds 3 teacher prompts, examples, and instructional notes for these expectations.</sub>

**B3 Exploring Forms and Cultural Contexts.** demonstrate an understanding of a variety of drama and theatre forms, traditions, and [styles](../glossary/style-26d4a23f.md) from the past and present, and their sociocultural and historical contexts

- compare and contrast how social values are communicated in several different drama forms and/or styles of live theatre from different times and places (e.g., how views of colonist Aboriginal relationships differ in plays from earlier times versus contemporary plays; how themes of loyalty to family and/or country are treated in comic forms versus serious drama forms)
- identify and describe several ways in which drama and theatre (e.g., street festivals, film festivals, theatre festivals, local theatre groups) contribute to contemporary social, economic, and cultural life (e.g., attract tourists; provide jobs; provide entertainment; promote cultural understanding; raise people’s awareness of social issues)

<sub>The source curriculum adds 2 teacher prompts, examples, and instructional notes for these expectations.</sub>


*Transferable skills developed in this strand: Innovation, Creativity & Entrepreneurship, Communication, Global Citizenship and Sustainability, Critical Thinking & Problem Solving, Self-Directed Learning.*

### C. Music

*By the end of Grade 7, students will:*

**C1 Creating and Performing.** apply the creative process to create and [perform](../glossary/perform.md) music for a variety of purposes, using the [elements](../glossary/elements-of-music.md) and techniques of music

- sing and/or play, in tune, from musical [notation](../glossary/notation.md), [unison](../glossary/unison.md) music and music in two or more parts from diverse cultures, [styles](../glossary/style-43c6460b.md), and [historical periods](../glossary/historical-periods.md) (e.g., perform selections from a method book, student compositions, instrumental scores, [ensemble](../glossary/ensemble-7bbf9429.md) repertoire, African drum [rhythms](../glossary/rhythm.md), choral repertoire, jazz charts, spirituals, steel band music)
- apply the elements of music when singing and/or playing, composing, and arranging music, using them for specific effects and clear purposes (e.g., create a class [chant](../glossary/chant.md) or song to build community spirit; manipulate the rhythm or [dynamics](../glossary/dynamics.md) in a familiar piece to create an [accompaniment](../glossary/accompaniment.md) for a media presentation)
- create musical compositions in a variety of forms for specific purposes and audiences (e.g., use available instruments to create a composition in response to an object, a visual image, or a silent film; add rhythmic, melodic, or [chordal](../glossary/chord.md) accompaniment to a familiar song; [improvise](../glossary/improvise.md) rhythmic or melodic [phrases](../glossary/phrase-f64225dc.md) over a variety of ostinati; create compositions using found sounds or recycled materials)
- use the tools and techniques of [musicianship](../glossary/musicianship.md) in musical performances (e.g., apply markings for dynamics, tempo, phrasing, and [articulation](../glossary/articulation.md) when performing; use proper breath control throughout their singing range)
- demonstrate an understanding of standard and other musical notation through performance and composition (e.g., read and respond to [accidentals](../glossary/accidental.md), [repeat](../glossary/repeat.md) signs, various tempo markings; notate and perform a variety of [scales](../glossary/scale.md), including the [blues scale](../glossary/blues-scale.md); explain how some contemporary music, children’s songs, or Aboriginal singing, drumming, and dancing are transmitted through [oral](../glossary/aural-oral.md) tradition)

<sub>The source curriculum adds 5 teacher prompts, examples, and instructional notes for these expectations.</sub>

**C2 Reflecting, Responding, and Analysing.** apply the critical analysis process to communicate their feelings, ideas, and understandings in response to a variety of music and musical experiences

- express analytical, personal responses to musical performances in a variety of ways (e.g., represent musical scenes in Pictures at an Exhibition through art work or dramatization; record detailed analyses of music they have listened to in a log or reflection journal to explain why they enjoy it and how the elements of music are used)
- analyse, using musical terminology, ways in which the elements are used in the music that they perform, listen to, and create (e.g., compare the use of drums in different social and cultural contexts, such as Asian, Aboriginal, and African communities; listen to a Brazilian [folk song](../glossary/folk-song.md) or a current popular song, and describe how the use of the various elements affects their response to the music)
- identify and give examples of their strengths and areas for improvement as composers, musical performers, interpreters, and audience members (e.g., set a goal to improve their performance skills, reflect on how successful they were in attaining their goal, keep a practice journal, record and analyse their performances throughout the term)

<sub>The source curriculum adds 3 teacher prompts, examples, and instructional notes for these expectations.</sub>

**C3 Exploring Forms and Cultural Contexts.** demonstrate an understanding of a variety of musical genres and styles from the past and present, and their sociocultural and historical contexts

- analyse the influences of music and the media on the development of personal and cultural identity (e.g., describe how their personal musical preferences have been formed from listening to music readily available in the media; explain how cultural identity, including a sense of Aboriginal pride for Aboriginal students, can be reinforced by listening to music of their own culture)
- analyse some historical, cultural, and technological influences on style, genre, and innovation in music (e.g., the impact of the invention of the piano or the electric guitar)

<sub>The source curriculum adds 2 teacher prompts, examples, and instructional notes for these expectations.</sub>

### D. Visual Arts

*By the end of Grade 7, students will:*

**D1 Creating and Presenting.** apply the creative process to produce art works in a [variety](../glossary/variety.md) of traditional two- and three-[dimensional](../glossary/dimension.md) forms, as well as multimedia art works, that communicate feelings, ideas, and understandings, using elements, principles, and [techniques](../glossary/technique-397b78cc.md) of visual arts as well as [current media technologies](../glossary/current-media-technologies.md)

- create art works, using a variety of traditional forms and [current media technologies](../glossary/current-media-technologies.md), that express feelings, ideas, and issues, including opposing points of view (e.g., an acrylic [painting](../glossary/painting.md) that uses symbols to represent conflict and resolution; [performance art](../glossary/performance-art.md) or an [installation](../glossary/installation.md) that portrays both sides of the struggle between humankind and nature; a [mixed-media](../glossary/mixed-media-work.md) or digital [composition](../glossary/composition.md) of a personal mandala that shows both [unity](../glossary/unity.md) and opposing forces)
- demonstrate an understanding of [composition](../glossary/composition.md), using multiple [principles of design](../glossary/principles-of-design.md) and the “[rule of thirds](../glossary/rule-of-thirds.md)” to create [narrative art](../glossary/narrative-art.md) works or art works on a theme or topic (e.g., use [colour](../glossary/colour.md) \[[analogous](../glossary/analogous-colours.md), [monochromatic](../glossary/monochromatic-colour-scheme.md)\] to unify a montage of newspaper and magazine images and text on a social issue; use smooth, horizontal lines to give a feeling of [harmony](../glossary/harmony-b559425f.md) in a [drawing](../glossary/drawing.md); create a [landscape](../glossary/landscape.md) that shows unity, using [repetition](../glossary/repetition-3ba3d390.md) of [shapes](../glossary/shape-c23ea962.md), [values](../glossary/value.md), [textures](../glossary/texture-3e8c5463.md), and/or lines, a particular area of focus, and the rule of thirds)
- use [elements of design](../glossary/elements-of-design.md) in art works to communicate ideas, messages, and understandings for a specific audience and purpose (e.g., create [balance](../glossary/balance-4016e3b7.md) in [positive](../glossary/positive-space-shapes.md) and [negative space](../glossary/negative-space-shapes.md) in a personal [logo](../glossary/logo.md) design, using drawing or paper cut-outs of black-and-white shapes on a grey [background](../glossary/background.md); selectively manipulate the colour, values, and text in a digital composition to change the message of a print advertisement)
- use a variety of [materials](../glossary/materials.md), tools, techniques, and technologies to determine solutions to increasingly complex [design](../glossary/design.md) challenges (e.g., 

 drawing: make a [cubist](../glossary/cubist-technique.md) still life of objects with reflective or textured surfaces, using both [wet](../glossary/wet-and-dry-materials.md) \[e.g., [ink](../glossary/ink.md), [watercolour](../glossary/watercolour-paint.md) pencils\] and dry \[e.g., [conté](../glossary/conte-crayon.md), chalk\] materials to simulate highlights and transparency 
 mixed media: make a hand-made or altered book, using various materials and techniques to represent ideas about selected elements in dance, drama, music, and/or the visual arts
 painting: make a cityscape that will serve as a background in an animated short movie, using experimental watercolour techniques such as wet on wet or salt [resist](../glossary/resist.md)
 [printmaking](../glossary/printmaking.md): make a [collograph](../glossary/collograph.md) or [chine collé](../glossary/chine-colle.md) that communicates a personal experience through the use of shape and analogous colour
 [sculpture](../glossary/sculpture.md): make [clay](../glossary/hand-building-clay.md) or [papier mâché](../glossary/papier-mache.md) gargoyles or “[crossed creatures](../glossary/crossed-creatures.md)” that have exaggerated features, using open and closed forms
 technology: make a high-[contrast](../glossary/contrast-5fce85ca.md) [self-portrait](../glossary/self-portrait.md) or [caricature](../glossary/caricature.md) with software, using techniques such as blurring, cloning, [cropping](../glossary/cropping.md), distortion, [layering](../glossary/layering.md), rotation, and selection)

<sub>The source curriculum adds 4 teacher prompts, examples, and instructional notes for these expectations.</sub>

**D2 Reflecting, Responding, and Analysing.** apply the critical analysis process to communicate feelings, ideas, and understandings in response to a variety of art works and art experiences

- interpret a variety of art works and identify the feelings, issues, themes, and social concerns that they convey (e.g., compare the mood of two different works by two peers, such as Above the Gravel Pit by Emily Carr and Reflections, Bishop’s Pond by David Milne; categorize a variety of art works on the basis of the themes and issues that are explored by the artists)
- explain how the elements and principles of design are used in their own and others’ art work to communicate meaning or understanding (e.g., the use of [complementary colours](../glossary/complementary-colours.md) for shadow detail in a still life by Cézanne; the use of contrast to emphasize the features in a portrait; Brian Jungen’s use of positive and negative [space](../glossary/space-fd5bc538.md) and the colours in traditional First Nation art works to convey ideas about consumerism and culture in masks that he created out of brand-name running shoes)
- demonstrate an understanding of how to read and interpret signs, symbols, and [style](../glossary/style-78c0be0f.md) in art works (e.g., visual metaphors, such as a single tree, used to evoke loneliness in paintings by Group of Seven artists; objects used as symbols in Sadako and the Thousand Paper Cranes by Eleanor Coerr; messages conveyed by the use of traditional symbols in [contemporary art](../glossary/contemporary-art.md); an artist’s manipulation of the intended message of an advertisement by modifying symbols and elements of design in the imagery that is appropriated, or “borrowed”, from the original ad)
- identify and explain their strengths, their interests, and areas for improvement as creators, interpreters, and viewers of art (e.g., explain their preferences for selected works of art, using appropriate visual arts vocabulary; provide constructive feedback in a [critique](../glossary/critique.md) of their own work and the work of others; identify the strategies they used in planning, producing, and critiquing their own and others’ works of art)

<sub>The source curriculum adds 4 teacher prompts, examples, and instructional notes for these expectations.</sub>

**D3 Exploring Forms and Cultural Contexts.** demonstrate an understanding of a variety of art forms, styles, and techniques from the past and present, and their sociocultural and historical contexts

- identify and describe some of the ways in which visual art forms and styles reflect the beliefs and traditions of a variety of cultures and civilizations (e.g., art works created within a tradition for functional and aesthetic purposes; beliefs reflected in art works by artists working within an artistic movement in the past or present; the purposes of architecture, objects, and images in past and present cultures and the contexts in which they were made, viewed, and valued; art works that challenge, sustain, and reflect society’s beliefs and traditions)
- demonstrate an understanding of the function of visual and media arts in various contexts today and in the past, and of their influence on the development of personal and cultural identity (e.g., the function of traditional and contemporary styles of Aboriginal art in the development of cultural identity and revitalization; the contributions of people in various arts careers to community events, festivals, businesses, galleries, and museums; the significance of the art work of individuals and the arts of cultural groups in local and global contexts)

<sub>The source curriculum adds 2 teacher prompts, examples, and instructional notes for these expectations.</sub>


*Transferable skills developed in this strand: Critical Thinking & Problem Solving, Communication, Innovation, Creativity & Entrepreneurship, Self-Directed Learning, Global Citizenship and Sustainability.*
