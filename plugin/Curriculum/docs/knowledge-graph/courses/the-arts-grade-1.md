# The Arts (Grade 1)

**The Arts** is the Grade 1 The Arts program in [The Arts](../subjects/the-arts.md).

Here you will find an overview of student development and learning in the arts at the primary level, as well as the expectations and learning for the four areas of study for Grade 1.

## Before you take this course

This course has no formal prerequisites.

## What students learn

The learning is organized into 4 strands: Dance, Drama, Music, and Visual Arts.

### A. Dance

*By the end of Grade 1, students will:*

**A1 Creating and Presenting.** apply the creative process to the composition of simple dance [phrases](../glossary/phrase.md), using the [elements of dance](../glossary/elements-of-dance.md) to communicate feelings and ideas

- use movements that are part of their daily experience in a variety of ways in dance phrases (e.g., alter and exaggerate movements based on even rhythms such as walking, galloping, and swimming, and on uneven rhythms such as skipping and jumping; amplify and modify [percussive movements](../glossary/percussive-movement.md) such as the movement of a clock ticking or the [sustained](../glossary/sustained-movement.md) hold of a cat stretching)
- use dance as a language to express feelings and ideas suggested by songs, stories, and poems, with a focus on the element of [body](../glossary/body.md), particularly body [shapes](../glossary/shape.md) (e.g., use the entire body \[crouch, slump\] and body parts \[folded arms, bowed head\] to express an idea such as deep thought)
- create dance phrases using a variety of ways to connect movements (e.g., connect a melt and a spin using a [non-locomotor movement](../glossary/non-locomotor-movement.md); connect a walk and a skip \[[locomotor movements](../glossary/locomotor-movement.md)\] with a circle \[[pathway](../glossary/pathway.md)\])
- use varied and/or [contrasting](../glossary/contrast.md) body shapes to communicate different types of messages (e.g., a high level and open, expansive shape to show dominance; a closed huddled shape to show that you are holding a treasured or secret object)

<sub>The source curriculum adds 4 teacher prompts, examples, and instructional notes for these expectations.</sub>

**A2 Reflecting, Responding, and Analysing.** apply the critical analysis process to communicate their feelings, ideas, and understandings in response to a variety of [dance pieces](../glossary/dance-piece.md) and experiences

- describe differences they observe when various movements from daily life are used as the basis or [stimulus](../glossary/stimulus.md) for movements in a dance phrase (e.g., describe ways in which everyday actions and movements such as skipping on the playground, walking to school, brushing their teeth, or getting dressed are changed when they are used in a dance phrase)
- identify and describe how the element of body is used in contrasting ways to communicate ideas in their own and others’ dance phrases, with teacher support (e.g., standing versus kneeling [body bases](../glossary/body-base.md) can communicate differences in power; curved versus straight shapes can communicate contrasting emotions)
- identify and give examples of their strengths and areas for growth as dance creators and audience members (e.g., using connecting movements; working collaboratively in groups to create dance phrases incorporating everyone’s ideas; watching peer performances attentively and asking relevant questions)

<sub>The source curriculum adds 3 teacher prompts, examples, and instructional notes for these expectations.</sub>

**A3 Exploring Forms and Cultural Contexts.** demonstrate an understanding of a variety of [dance forms](../glossary/dance-form.md) and [styles](../glossary/style.md) from the past and present, and their social and/or community contexts

- describe, with teacher guidance, a variety of dances from different communities around the world that they have seen in the media, at live performances and social gatherings, or in the classroom (e.g., describe traditional dances they have seen to a partner \[Chinese ribbon dance, Highland fling, powwow [dance styles](../glossary/dance-style.md)\])
- identify and describe dance experiences in their own lives and communities (e.g., At home: dancing or moving to a favourite song/story; seeing dance on television or in a movie DVD; At school: playing at recess; In the community: dancing or observing dances at weddings, parties, cultural celebrations)

<sub>The source curriculum adds 2 teacher prompts, examples, and instructional notes for these expectations.</sub>

### B. Drama

*By the end of Grade 1, students will:*

**B1 Creating and Presenting.** apply the creative process to [dramatic play](../glossary/dramatic-play.md) and [process drama](../glossary/process-drama.md), using the [elements](../glossary/elements-of-drama.md) and [conventions of drama](../glossary/conventions-of-drama.md) to communicate feelings, ideas, and stories

- engage in dramatic play and [role play](../glossary/role-playing-role-play.md), with a focus on exploring a variety of sources from diverse communities, times, and places (e.g., retell and enact nursery and other childhood rhymes, stories, or narratives from picture books; use movement and [voice](../glossary/voice.md) to explore the thoughts of a familiar folk tale character in a variety of situations; use [guided imagery](../glossary/guided-imagery.md) and descriptive language to explore what a character might feel and experience in a story setting or picture; use [group role play](../glossary/group-role-play.md) to explore alternative endings to stories, fairy tales, and personal experiences; use role play or a [tableau](../glossary/tableau.md) at key moments in a story to help the [protagonist](../glossary/protagonist.md) solve a problem; interview a teacher in the role of a character from a story)
- demonstrate an understanding of the element of character by adopting thoughts, feelings, and [gestures](../glossary/gesture-8ae80d3b.md) relevant to the role being played (e.g., use facial expressions, body movement, and words to respond [in role](../glossary/in-role.md) to scenarios and questions; express different points of view after reading a picture book about issues of belonging and discrimination; work with a partner to create a short [scene](../glossary/scene.md) that shows the importance of acceptance, understanding, and inclusion)
- plan and shape dramatic play by building on the ideas of others, both in and [out of role](../glossary/out-of-role.md) (e.g., In role: add ideas to the dramatic play that reflect the knowledge and experience of the role that is being played \[such as a scientist mentioning an experiment or a journalist mentioning an interview\]; create and share scenes from their own experiences; Out of role: work in a group to plan and prepare a scene and ask follow-up questions such as “How could we make this clearer? What changes can we make to help our story be understood?”; describe the sort of person who might own a particular found object such as a bag or a coat)
- communicate feelings and ideas to a familiar audience (e.g., classmates) using a few simple visual or technological aids to support and enhance their drama work (e.g., use a sheer cloth moved quickly to represent water; use a rainstick or shaker to create a sense of mystery or magic; use a variety of classroom objects to create a play area for specific dramatic play experiences)

<sub>The source curriculum adds 4 teacher prompts, examples, and instructional notes for these expectations.</sub>

**B2 Reflecting, Responding, and Analysing.** apply the critical analysis process to communicate feelings, ideas, and understandings in response to a variety of [drama works](../glossary/dramatic-works.md) and experiences

- express feelings and ideas about a drama experience or performance in a variety of ways, making personal connections to the characters and themes in the story (e.g., in oral discussion, relate themes about family relationships or friendships to their own lives; after viewing a [play](../glossary/play.md) or clip of a movie dealing with family issues \[such as Cinderella or Princess Mononoke\], contribute to a class journal entry or draw a picture to show the feelings of one or more of the characters – a stepsister, Cinderella, the mice)
- demonstrate an understanding of how the element of character/role is used in [shared classroom drama experiences](../glossary/shared-drama-experience.md) and theatre to communicate meaning (e.g., to provide important information in a situation; to represent a particular perspective/point of view; to change the direction of the [plot](../glossary/plot.md); to symbolize an idea such as friendship)
- identify and give examples of their strengths, interests, and areas for improvement as drama participants and audience members (e.g., using personal experience and imagination to extend ideas in the drama; building on their own or others’ ideas)

<sub>The source curriculum adds 3 teacher prompts, examples, and instructional notes for these expectations.</sub>

**B3 Exploring Forms and Cultural Contexts.** demonstrate an understanding of a variety of drama and theatre forms and [styles](../glossary/style-26d4a23f.md) from the past and present, and their social and/or community contexts

- identify and describe drama and theatre forms, events, and activities that they experience in their home, school, and community (e.g., favourite television or computer programs and characters, imaginative play with action figures, dramatic play, attending plays and celebrations, interacting with picture books, [storytelling](../glossary/storytelling.md))
- demonstrate an awareness of a variety of roles, themes, and subjects in dramas and stories from different communities around the world (e.g., contribute to a class scrapbook about characters such as trolls/fairies, trickster themes in Nanabush stories \[from Native folklore\] and [Anansi stories](../glossary/anansi-stories-tales.md) \[from West African folklore\])

<sub>The source curriculum adds 2 teacher prompts, examples, and instructional notes for these expectations.</sub>

### C. Music

*By the end of Grade 1, students will:*

**C1 Creating and Performing.** apply the creative process to create and [perform](../glossary/perform.md) music for a variety of purposes, using the [elements](../glossary/elements-of-music.md) and techniques of music

- sing songs in [unison](../glossary/unison.md) and play simple [accompaniments](../glossary/accompaniment.md) for music from a wide variety of diverse cultures, styles, and [historical periods](../glossary/historical-periods.md) (e.g., play a simple rhythmic [ostinato](../glossary/ostinato.md) on a drum or tambourine to accompany singing; match [pitches](../glossary/pitch.md) in [echo singing](../glossary/echo-singing.md))
- apply the elements of music when singing, playing, and moving (e.g., [duration](../glossary/duration-9ff8b381.md): while singing a familiar song, clap the [rhythm](../glossary/rhythm.md) while others pat the [beat](../glossary/beat.md), and on a signal switch roles)
- create compositions for a specific purpose and a familiar audience (e.g., use the notes “mi”, “so”, and “la” to create a melodic [phrase](../glossary/phrase-f64225dc.md) that answers a sung question; use [rhythm](../glossary/rhythm.md) instruments, [body percussion](../glossary/body-percussion.md), or everyday objects to create an accompaniment to a story or song; use short rhythmic phrases in improvised answers to clapped questions)
- use the tools and techniques of musicianship in musical performances (e.g., sing with relaxed but straight posture and controlled breathing; rehearse music to perform with others)
- demonstrate understanding that sounds can be represented by [symbols](../glossary/symbols.md) (e.g., show rhythm and beat with [manipulatives](../glossary/manipulatives.md) such as math cubes or Popsicle sticks; use devised, or invented, forms of musical [notation](../glossary/notation.md), or simple forms of [standard](../glossary/standard-notation.md) musical notation)

<sub>The source curriculum adds 5 teacher prompts, examples, and instructional notes for these expectations.</sub>

**C2 Reflecting, Responding, and Analysing.** apply the critical analysis process to communicate their feelings, ideas, and understandings in response to a variety of music and musical experiences

- express initial reactions and personal responses to musical performances in a variety of ways (e.g., move like an animal of which the music reminds them)
- describe ways in which the elements of music are used for different purposes in the music they perform, listen to, and create (e.g., the [tempo](../glossary/tempo-9c4444a6.md) and [dynamics](../glossary/dynamics.md) of a lullaby, the beat and rhythm of a march, the sound quality of a trumpet in a fanfare)
- identify and give examples of their strengths and areas for growth as musical performers, creators, interpreters, and audience members (e.g., singing in unison, providing constructive feedback and suggestions for a classmate’s or guest performer’s performance)

<sub>The source curriculum adds 3 teacher prompts, examples, and instructional notes for these expectations.</sub>

**C3 Exploring Forms and Cultural Contexts.** demonstrate an understanding of a variety of musical genres and styles from the past and present, and their social and/or community contexts

- identify and describe musical experiences in their own lives (e.g., list the places and times within a day when they hear or perform music; describe various times when they sing, play, and move to music in school, at home, and in the community)
- identify a variety of musical pieces from different cultures through performing and/or listening to them (e.g., [folk songs](../glossary/folk-song.md), songs for celebrations, ceremonial music from Canadian and world sources)

<sub>The source curriculum adds 2 teacher prompts, examples, and instructional notes for these expectations.</sub>

### D. Visual Arts

*By the end of Grade 1, students will:*

**D1 Creating and Presenting.** apply the creative process to produce a variety of two- and three-dimensional art works, using elements, principles, and [techniques](../glossary/technique-397b78cc.md) of visual arts to communicate feelings, ideas, and understandings

- create two- and three-dimensional works of art that express feelings and ideas inspired by personal experiences (e.g., a tempera [painting](../glossary/painting.md) that communicates their feelings about a special occasion or event such as a fair or a parade; a [sculpture](../glossary/sculpture.md) of a favourite musical instrument made with found objects; a [watercolour](../glossary/watercolour-paint.md) painting of a favourite part of the schoolyard; an [assemblage](../glossary/assemblage.md) in which images and objects from home and school are used to represent special memories)
- demonstrate an understanding of [composition](../glossary/composition.md), using [principles of design](../glossary/principles-of-design.md) to create [narrative art](../glossary/narrative-art.md) works or art works on a theme or topic (e.g., a [drawing](../glossary/drawing.md) of an approaching storm that uses a variety of lines to create [contrast](../glossary/contrast-5fce85ca.md) \[dashed, jagged, curved, spiral\]; a cardboard or [papier mâché](../glossary/papier-mache.md) sculpture of a mythical animal in a dynamic [pose](../glossary/pose.md) that uses surface [materials](../glossary/materials.md) to show a contrast in [texture](../glossary/texture-3e8c5463.md) \[fuzzy yarn; coarse, prickly sawdust\])
- use [elements of design](../glossary/elements-of-design.md) in art works to communicate ideas, messages, and personal understandings (e.g., a [pattern](../glossary/pattern-a3bc501e.md) of broken, wavy, and zigzag lines to make the bark of a tree look rough in a drawing; size and arrangement of [organic shapes](../glossary/organic-shapes-or-forms.md) in a painting of flowers to create the impression that the various flowers are at different distances from the viewer)
- use a variety of materials, tools, and techniques to respond to [design](../glossary/design.md) challenges (e.g.,  

 drawing: use wax crayon or oil [pastel](../glossary/pastel.md) lines on coloured paper to express their responses to different kinds of music or rhythm
 [mixed media](../glossary/mixed-media-work.md): use torn paper and textured materials to create a [landscape](../glossary/landscape.md) [collage](../glossary/collage-36ceb0fc.md) of a playground that includes a [horizon line](../glossary/horizon-line.md)
 painting: create [paint resists](../glossary/paint-resist.md) that are made with wax crayon on paper, using [rubbing plates](../glossary/rubbing-plate.md) that have a variety of textures \[e.g., bumpy, wavy\] to create imaginary creatures inspired by the artistic [style](../glossary/style-78c0be0f.md) of Eric Carle 
 [printmaking](../glossary/printmaking.md): use cut sponge or cardboard and paint [stamping](../glossary/stamp.md) to make a pattern of [geometric](../glossary/geometric-shape.md) and organic shapes 
 sculpture: use glued or taped scrap wood to build a wood block sculpture of an imaginary geometric machine)

<sub>The source curriculum adds 4 teacher prompts, examples, and instructional notes for these expectations.</sub>

**D2 Reflecting, Responding, and Analysing.** apply the critical analysis process to communicate feelings, ideas, and understandings in response to a variety of art works and art experiences

- express their feelings and ideas about art works and art experiences (e.g., describe feelings evoked by the use of colours in the painting Inside the Sugar Shack by Miyuki Tanobe or The Starry Night by Vincent van Gogh; use drama to respond to a community art work viewed during a neighbourhood walk; describe the ways in which an artist’s representation of an event relates to their own experiences)
- explain how elements and principles of design are used to communicate meaning or understanding in their own and others’ art work (e.g., explain how repeated lines and shapes are used to depict the texture of snake, lizard, leopard, or dinosaur skin; classify images on a topic, and, focusing on a [dominant element](../glossary/dominant-element.md), use the images to explain that there are many different ways of approaching the same subject)
- demonstrate an awareness of signs and symbols encountered in their daily lives and in works of art (e.g., green is associated with nature and sometimes with envy or illness in the West; red is associated with stopping \[traffic lights\] in the West, luck in China, success in Cherokee culture, mourning in South Africa)
- identify and document their strengths, their interests, and areas for improvement as creators of art (e.g., discuss what they think is good about works in their art folder during conferences with their teacher; do a think-pair-share on their favourite part of one of their art works)

<sub>The source curriculum adds 4 teacher prompts, examples, and instructional notes for these expectations.</sub>

**D3 Exploring Forms and Cultural Contexts.** demonstrate an understanding of a variety of art forms, styles, and techniques from the past and present, and their social and/or community contexts

- identify and describe visual art forms that they see in their home, at school, in their community, and in visual arts experiences (e.g., [illustrations](../glossary/illustration.md) in picture books, designs of various toys, patterns on clothing or other textiles, classroom visits by artists, student displays at their school, visits to galleries)
- demonstrate an awareness of a variety of works of art from diverse communities, times, and places (e.g., iconic architecture they have seen either in pictures or in real life, such as the CN Tower, the Eiffel Tower, the Taj Mahal; comics from different countries; decorations or patterns on crafts or old artefacts; contemporary and ancient [clay](../glossary/hand-building-clay.md) sculptures; paintings of family or community events from different cultures or from previous eras)

<sub>The source curriculum adds 2 teacher prompts, examples, and instructional notes for these expectations.</sub>
