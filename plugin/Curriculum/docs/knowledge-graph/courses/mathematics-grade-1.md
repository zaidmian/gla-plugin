# Mathematics (Grade 1)

**Mathematics** is the Grade 1 Mathematics program in [Mathematics](../subjects/mathematics.md).

Here you will find the expectations and learning for the six areas of study for Grade 1 Mathematics.

## Before you take this course

This course has no formal prerequisites.

## What students learn

The learning is organized into 6 strands: Social-Emotional Learning (SEL) Skills in Mathematics and the Mathematical Processes, Number, Algebra, Data, Spatial Sense, and Financial Literacy.

### A. Social-Emotional Learning (SEL) Skills in Mathematics and the Mathematical Processes

*Throughout this grade, in order to promote a positive identity as a math learner, to foster well-being and the ability to learn, build resilience, and thrive, students will:*

**A1 Social-Emotional Learning (SEL) Skills and the Mathematical Processes.** apply, to the best of their ability, a variety of social-emotional learning skills to support their use of the mathematical processes and their learning in connection with the expectations in the other five strands of the mathematics curriculum


### B. Number

*By the end of Grade 1, students will:*

**B1 Number Sense.** demonstrate an understanding of numbers and make connections to the way numbers are used in everyday life

- read and [represent](../glossary/represent.md) [whole numbers](../glossary/whole-number.md) up to and including 50, and describe various ways they are used in everyday life
- [compose](../glossary/compose-61cac0c1.md) and [decompose](../glossary/decomposition-of-numbers.md) whole numbers up to and including 50, using a variety of [tools](../glossary/tools-9e67a3c2.md) and strategies, in various contexts
- compare and order whole numbers up to and including 50, in various contexts
- [estimate](../glossary/estimate.md) the number of objects in collections of up to 50, and verify their estimates by counting
- [count](../glossary/count.md) to 50 by 1s, 2s, 5s, and 10s, using a variety of tools and strategies
- use [drawings](../glossary/drawing-20030707.md) to represent and solve [fair-share problems](../glossary/fair-share-problem.md) that involve 2 and 4 sharers, respectively, and have remainders of 1 or 2
- recognize that one half and two fourths of the same whole are equal, in fair-sharing contexts
- use drawings to compare and order [unit fractions](../glossary/unit-fraction-32c07bc4.md) representing the individual portions that result when a whole is shared by different numbers of sharers, up to a maximum of 10

<sub>The source curriculum adds 32 teacher prompts, examples, and instructional notes for these expectations.</sub>

**B2 Operations.** use knowledge of numbers and operations to solve mathematical problems encountered in everyday life

- use the properties of addition and subtraction, and the [relationship](../glossary/relationship-f5b5e0bf.md) between addition and subtraction, to solve problems and check calculations
- recall and demonstrate addition facts for numbers up to 10, and related subtraction facts
- use [mental math strategies](../glossary/mental-math-strategies.md), including [estimation](../glossary/estimation.md), to add and subtract whole numbers that add up to no more than 20, and explain the strategies used
- use objects, diagrams, and [equations](../glossary/equation.md) to represent, describe, and solve situations involving addition and subtraction of whole numbers that add up to no more than 50
- represent and solve [equal-group problems](../glossary/equal-group-problem.md) where the total number of items is no more than 10, including problems in which each group is a half, using tools and drawings

<sub>The source curriculum adds 22 teacher prompts, examples, and instructional notes for these expectations.</sub>


*Transferable skills developed in this strand: Critical Thinking & Problem Solving, Communication.*

### C. Algebra

*By the end of Grade 1, students will:*

**C1 Patterns and Relationships.** identify, describe, extend, create, and make predictions about a variety of patterns, including those found in real-life contexts

- identify and describe the [regularities](../glossary/regularity.md) in a variety of [patterns](../glossary/pattern-6d386f87.md), including patterns found in real-life contexts
- create and [translate](../glossary/translate-6bbeec99.md) patterns using movements, sounds, objects, shapes, letters, and numbers
- determine [pattern rules](../glossary/pattern-rule.md) and use them to extend patterns, make and justify predictions, and identify missing [elements](../glossary/element.md) in patterns
- create and describe patterns to illustrate [relationships](../glossary/relationship-f5b5e0bf.md) among whole numbers up to 50

<sub>The source curriculum adds 20 teacher prompts, examples, and instructional notes for these expectations.</sub>

**C2 Equations and Inequalities.** demonstrate an understanding of variables, expressions, equalities, and inequalities, and apply this understanding in various contexts

- identify quantities that can change and quantities that always remain the same in real-life contexts
- determine whether given pairs of addition and subtraction [expressions](../glossary/expression.md) are [equivalent](../glossary/equivalence.md) or not
- identify and use equivalent relationships for whole numbers up to 50, in various contexts

<sub>The source curriculum adds 11 teacher prompts, examples, and instructional notes for these expectations.</sub>

**C3 Coding.** solve problems and create computational representations of mathematical situations using coding concepts and skills

- solve problems and create [computational representations](../glossary/computational-representations.md) of mathematical situations by writing and [executing code](../glossary/execute-coding.md), including [code](../glossary/code.md) that involves [sequential events](../glossary/sequential-events-coding.md)
- read and alter existing code, including code that involves sequential events, and describe how changes to the code affect the [outcomes](../glossary/outcome-coding.md)

<sub>The source curriculum adds 9 teacher prompts, examples, and instructional notes for these expectations.</sub>

**C4 Mathematical Modelling.** apply the process of mathematical modelling to represent, analyse, make predictions, and provide insight into real-life situations 
 
 This overall expectation has no specific expectations. [Mathematical modelling](../glossary/mathematical-modelling.md) is an iterative and interconnected process that is applied to various contexts, allowing students to bring in learning from other strands. Students’ demonstration of the process of mathematical modelling, as they apply concepts and skills learned in other strands, is assessed and evaluated. 
 Read more about the mathematical modelling process.


*Transferable skills developed in this strand: Critical Thinking & Problem Solving, Communication, Digital Literacy.*

### D. Data

*By the end of Grade 1, students will:*

**D1 Data Literacy.** manage, analyse, and use data to make convincing arguments and informed decisions, in various contexts drawn from real life

- sort sets of data about people or things according to one [attribute](../glossary/attribute.md), and describe rules used for sorting
- collect data through observations, experiments, and interviews to answer [questions of interest](../glossary/question-of-interest.md) that focus on a single piece of information; record the data using methods of their choice; and organize the data in [tally tables](../glossary/tally-table.md)
- display sets of data, using [one-to-one correspondence](../glossary/one-to-one-correspondence.md), in [concrete graphs](../glossary/concrete-graph.md) and [pictographs](../glossary/pictograph.md) with proper sources, titles, and labels
- order categories of data from greatest to least [frequency](../glossary/frequency.md) for various data sets displayed in tally tables, concrete graphs, and pictographs
- analyse different sets of data presented in various ways, including in tally tables, concrete graphs, and pictographs, by asking and answering questions about the data and drawing conclusions, then make convincing arguments and informed decisions

<sub>The source curriculum adds 17 teacher prompts, examples, and instructional notes for these expectations.</sub>

**D2 Probability.** describe the likelihood that events will happen, and use that information to make predictions

- use mathematical language, including the terms “impossible”, “possible”, and “certain”, to describe the [likelihood](../glossary/likelihood.md) of events happening, and use that likelihood to make predictions and informed decisions
- make and test predictions about the likelihood that the categories in a data set from one [population](../glossary/population.md) will have the same frequencies in data collected from a different population of the same size

<sub>The source curriculum adds 7 teacher prompts, examples, and instructional notes for these expectations.</sub>


*Transferable skills developed in this strand: Critical Thinking & Problem Solving, Digital Literacy, Innovation, Creativity & Entrepreneurship, Communication.*

### E. Spatial Sense

*By the end of Grade 1, students will:*

**E1 Geometric and Spatial Reasoning.** describe and represent shape, location, and movement by applying [geometric properties](../glossary/geometric-property.md) and spatial relationships in order to navigate the world around them

- [sort](../glossary/sort.md)[three-dimensional objects](../glossary/three-dimensional-object-e7630a4b.md) and [two-dimensional shapes](../glossary/two-dimensional-shape.md) according to one [attribute](../glossary/attribute.md) at a time, and identify the sorting rule being used
- construct three-dimensional objects, and identify two-dimensional shapes contained within structures and objects
- construct and describe two-dimensional shapes and three-dimensional objects that have matching halves
- describe the [relative locations](../glossary/relative-location-81eee27a.md) of objects or people, using [positional language](../glossary/positional-language.md)
- give and follow directions for moving from one location to another

<sub>The source curriculum adds 24 teacher prompts, examples, and instructional notes for these expectations.</sub>

**E2 Measurement.** compare, estimate, and determine measurements in various contexts

- identify measurable attributes of two-dimensional shapes and three-dimensional objects, including length, area, [mass](../glossary/mass.md), [capacity](../glossary/capacity.md), and [angle](../glossary/angle.md)
- compare several everyday objects and order them according to length, area, mass, and capacity
- read the date on a calendar, and use a calendar to identify days, weeks, months, holidays, and seasons

<sub>The source curriculum adds 11 teacher prompts, examples, and instructional notes for these expectations.</sub>


*Transferable skills developed in this strand: Critical Thinking & Problem Solving, Communication.*

### F. Financial Literacy

*By the end of Grade 1, students will:*

**F1 Money and Finances.** demonstrate an understanding of the value of Canadian currency

- identify the various Canadian coins up to 50¢ and coins and bills up to $50, and compare their values

<sub>The source curriculum adds 4 teacher prompts, examples, and instructional notes for these expectations.</sub>


*Transferable skills developed in this strand: Communication, Global Citizenship and Sustainability.*
