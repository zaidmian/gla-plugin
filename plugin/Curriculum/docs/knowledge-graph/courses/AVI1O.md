# Visual Arts (AVI1O)

**Visual Arts (AVI1O)** is a Grade 9 open course in [The Arts](../subjects/the-arts.md).

This course is exploratory in nature, offering an overview of visual arts as a foundation for further study. Students will become familiar with the elements and principles of design and the expressive qualities of various materials by using a range of media, processes, techniques, and styles. Students will use the creative and critical analysis processes and will interpret art within a personal, contemporary, and historical context.

## Before you take this course

This course has no formal prerequisites.

## Where this course leads

Completing this course opens the door to [Visual Arts (AVI3M)](AVI3M.md).

## What students learn

The learning is organized into 3 strands: Creating and Presenting, Reflecting, Responding, and Analysing, and Foundations.

### A. Creating and Presenting

*By the end of this course, students will:*

**A1 The Creative Process.** apply the creative process to create a variety of art works, individually and/or collaboratively

- use a variety of strategies, individually and/or collaboratively, to generate ideas and to develop plans for the creation of art works (e.g., use research, synectic charts, and/or a class brainstorming session to generate a variety of creative ideas; reflect on the suitability of the ideas and choose one to serve as the basis for their plan; use notes and/or [thumbnail sketches](../glossary/thumbnail-sketch-eb6b0339.md) to develop their plans; revise their plans on the basis of peer- and self-assessment)
- use exploration/experimentation, reflection, and revision when producing a variety of art works in each of the following areas: [drawing](../glossary/scumbling-drawing-6e2669d8.md), [sculpture](../glossary/sculpture-9769406e.md), painting, and printmaking (e.g., explore a variety of materials and/or techniques; reflect on the input of their peers; refine their art work on the basis of useful feedback)
- document their use of the creative process in a portfolio (e.g., include thumbnail sketches of ideas and/or plans, notes on or examples of the results of experiments with different media or techniques, and copies of their preliminary and final work to show evidence of revision and artistic growth), and refer to this portfolio to reflect on how effectively they have used the creative process

<sub>The source curriculum adds 3 teacher prompts, examples, and instructional notes for these expectations.</sub>

**A2 The Elements and Principles of Design.** apply elements and principles of design to create art works for the purpose of self-expression and to communicate ideas, information, and/or messages

- explore elements and principles of design, and apply them to create art works that express personal feelings and/or communicate emotions to an [audience](../glossary/audience.md) (e.g., explore the use of [colour](../glossary/colour-452b5d2f.md), [texture](../glossary/texture-c26be1cd.md), and/or [space](../glossary/space-e9773d9e.md) to express specific emotions)
- apply elements and principles of design to create art works that communicate ideas and information (e.g., an informational public service poster on a social issue such as bullying or protecting the environment)

<sub>The source curriculum adds 2 teacher prompts, examples, and instructional notes for these expectations.</sub>

**A3 Production and Presentation.** produce art works, using a [variety](../glossary/variety-8578410f.md) of media/materials and traditional and/or [emerging technologies](../glossary/emerging-technologies.md), tools, and techniques, and demonstrate an understanding of a variety of ways of presenting their works and the works of others

- explore and experiment with a variety of media/materials and traditional and/or emerging technologies, tools, and techniques, and apply them to produce art works (e.g., experiment with contemporary art-making methods and materials; incorporate found objects, digital images, and [mixed media](../glossary/mixed-media-work-7e690e93.md) into their art work; use alternative painting surfaces and implements)
- use appropriate practices to prepare their art works for presentation (e.g., complete all aspects of their art works; sign and date and/or number their works and prepare appropriate labels for them; mount, mat, and/or frame their works, as appropriate)
- demonstrate an understanding of some of the ways in which art works can be presented to reach a variety of audiences (e.g., peers in a classroom, a range of students and teachers in a school, a broader audience at a venue in the community)

*Transferable skills developed in this strand: Critical Thinking & Problem Solving, Communication, Innovation, Creativity & Entrepreneurship, Global Citizenship and Sustainability, Digital Literacy.*

### B. Reflecting, Responding, and Analysing

*By the end of this course, students will:*

**B1 The Critical Analysis Process.** demonstrate an understanding of the critical analysis process by examining, interpreting, evaluating, and reflecting on various art works

- identify and describe their initial reactions to a variety of art works (e.g., their emotional reaction to a work such as Paul Peel’s After the Bath; their first impression of a work’s mood, subject, intent)
- identify, on the basis of examination, elements and principles of design used in various art works, and describe their effects (e.g., the use of [value](../glossary/value-3812b599.md) to enhance contrast between [foreground](../glossary/foreground-57b2c171.md) and [background](../glossary/background-8112240f.md); the use of colour to contribute to mood, depth, and/or [unity](../glossary/unity-3adf2707.md))
- interpret a variety of historical and/or contemporary art works (e.g., prehistoric cave paintings, Egyptian tomb paintings, Claes Oldenburg’s Shoestring Potatoes Spilling from a Bag) to identify their [subject matter](../glossary/subject-matter-00083cbe.md) and purpose and the meanings they convey
- use a variety of strategies (e.g., peer- and self-assessment, formal and informal [critiques](../glossary/critique-9d2534ef.md), small-group and class discussions) to identify and reflect on the qualities of their own art works and the works of others, and evaluate the effectiveness of these works

<sub>The source curriculum adds 4 teacher prompts, examples, and instructional notes for these expectations.</sub>

**B2 Art, Society, and Values.** demonstrate an understanding of how art works reflect the society in which they were created, and of how they can affect personal values

- identify the functions of various types of art works (e.g., advertising, environmental [installations](../glossary/installation-0ad9af94.md), religious art works, satirical works) in past and present societies
- identify ways in which various art works reflect the society in which they were created (e.g., the cave paintings at Lascaux, the pyramids at Giza, the Parthenon, the Qin terracotta warriors and horses)
- identify ways in which creating and/or analysing art works has affected their personal identity and values (e.g., with reference to their self-perception, their level of [empathy](../glossary/empathy-empathize-e79a68a9.md), their awareness of stereotypes, their awareness of their emotions and their ability to express them)

<sub>The source curriculum adds 3 teacher prompts, examples, and instructional notes for these expectations.</sub>

**B3 Connections Beyond the Classroom.** demonstrate an understanding of the types of knowledge and skills developed in visual arts, and identify various opportunities related to visual arts

- identify types of knowledge and skills acquired in visual arts (e.g., knowledge related to visual literacy; creative problem-solving skills, visual communication skills), and describe how they could be applied in a variety of areas of personal and professional life
- identify a variety of secondary and postsecondary pathways and careers related to visual arts (e.g., postsecondary arts programs; careers in advertising, [animation](../glossary/animation-f8c07cf4.md), art therapy, fashion design, graphic design)
- identify, on the basis of exploration, a variety of personal opportunities in their community in cultural or other fields related to visual arts (e.g., opportunities to be involved with design and/or decoration for multicultural events, school plays or exhibits, and/or community festivals; opportunities to design posters for a variety of events; volunteer or job opportunities at a local organization where they could use and/or develop their skills in visual arts)

### C. Foundations

*By the end of this course, students will:*

**C1 Terminology.** demonstrate an understanding of, and use correct terminology when referring to, elements, principles, and other components related to visual arts

- use appropriate terminology when identifying and describing the use of elements and principles of design in their own art works and the works of others (e.g., when describing the use of colour, value, and proportion in Tom Thomson’s The Jack Pine, the use of [line](../glossary/line-7d02983a.md) and space in Sol LeWitt’s X with Columns, or the use of layers of [tone](../glossary/tone-bf0b1efd.md) and texture in Eva Hess’s Contingent)
- use appropriate vocabulary to describe techniques, materials, and tools when creating and presenting visual art works (e.g., brayers, conté, frottage, markers, painting techniques, pencil techniques, relief, stencil)
- identify the stages of the creative process and the critical analysis process using appropriate terminology (e.g., in small groups, draw labelled diagrams to illustrate the stages of the creative and critical  analysis processes)

**C2 Conventions and Techniques.** demonstrate an understanding of conventions and techniques used in the creation of visual art works

- demonstrate an understanding of some techniques that artists use to achieve specific effects (e.g., the use of [cross-hatching](../glossary/cross-hatching-fac0a199.md) to create a feeling of depth and [dimension](../glossary/dimension-c62877f4.md); the use, in [watercolour](../glossary/watercolour-paint-dd1bb453.md) painting, of a transparent wash of colour to create the effect of light coming through the [composition](../glossary/composition-c0dbde58.md))
- demonstrate an understanding of some of the conventions used in visual art works (e.g., the use of metaphor, similes, symbols, synectics to create a specific effect or to communicate an idea;  the use of conventions associated with narrative art)

<sub>The source curriculum adds 2 teacher prompts, examples, and instructional notes for these expectations.</sub>

**C3 Responsible Practices.** demonstrate an understanding of responsible practices related to visual arts

- identify some legal and ethical issues associated with visual arts (e.g., copyright and ownership issues), and demonstrate legal and ethical practices when creating, presenting, and/or promoting art works (e.g., seek permission before reproducing copyrighted material)
- demonstrate safe and conscientious practices associated with the use of materials, tools, and technologies in visual arts (e.g., identify hazardous materials and adopt appropriate precautions and/or protective measures when using them; demonstrate respect for property, including classroom facilities, tools, equipment, and  technological devices)
- demonstrate an understanding of how the production and presentation of art works can affect the environment, and apply environmentally responsible practices when creating and presenting art works (e.g., reduce, reuse, and recycle when possible; limit their use of environmentally hazardous substances or non-sustainable resources; dispose of materials in environmentally responsible ways)

*Transferable skills developed in this strand: Critical Thinking & Problem Solving, Communication, Self-Directed Learning, Global Citizenship and Sustainability.*
