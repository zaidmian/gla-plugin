# Mathematics (Grade 7)

**Mathematics** is the Grade 7 Mathematics program in [Mathematics](../subjects/mathematics.md).

Here you will find the expectations and learning for the six areas of study for Grade 7 Mathematics.

## Before you take this course

This course has no formal prerequisites.

## What students learn

The learning is organized into 6 strands: Social-Emotional Learning (SEL) Skills in Mathematics and the Mathematical Processes, Number, Algebra, Data, Spatial Sense, and Financial Literacy.

### A. Social-Emotional Learning (SEL) Skills in Mathematics and the Mathematical Processes

*Throughout this grade, in order to promote a positive identity as a math learner, to foster well-being and the ability to learn, build resilience, and thrive, students will:*

**A1 Social-Emotional Learning (SEL) Skills and the Mathematical Processes.** apply, to the best of their ability, a variety of social-emotional learning skills to support their use of the mathematical processes and their learning in connection with the expectations in the other five strands of the mathematics curriculum


### B. Number

*By the end of Grade 7, students will:*

**B1 Number Sense.** demonstrate an understanding of numbers and make connections to the way numbers are used in everyday life

- represent and compare [whole numbers](../glossary/whole-number.md) up to and including one billion, including in [expanded form](../glossary/expanded-form.md) using [powers of ten](../glossary/power-of-ten.md), and describe various ways they are used in everyday life
- identify and represent [perfect squares](../glossary/perfect-square.md), and determine their [square roots](../glossary/square-root-of-a-number.md), in various contexts
- read, represent, compare, and order [rational numbers](../glossary/rational-number.md), including positive and negative fractions and [decimal numbers](../glossary/decimal-number.md) to thousandths, in various contexts
- use [equivalent fractions](../glossary/equivalent-fractions.md) to simplify fractions, when appropriate, in various contexts
- generate fractions and decimal numbers between any two quantities
- [round](../glossary/round-e1d768f1.md) decimal numbers to the nearest tenth, hundredth, or whole number, as applicable, in various contexts
- convert between fractions, decimal numbers, and [percents](../glossary/percent.md), in various contexts

<sub>The source curriculum adds 32 teacher prompts, examples, and instructional notes for these expectations.</sub>

**B2 Operations.** use knowledge of numbers and operations to solve mathematical problems encountered in everyday life

- use the properties and [order of operations](../glossary/order-of-operations.md), and the relationships between operations, to solve problems involving whole numbers, decimal numbers, fractions, [ratios](../glossary/ratio.md), [rates](../glossary/rate.md), and percents, including those requiring multiple steps or multiple operations
- understand and recall commonly used percents, fractions, and decimal equivalents
- use [mental math strategies](../glossary/mental-math-strategies.md) to increase and decrease a whole number by 1%, 5%, 10%, 25%, 50%, and 100%, and explain the strategies used
- use objects, diagrams, and [equations](../glossary/equation.md) to represent, describe, and solve situations involving addition and subtraction of [integers](../glossary/integer.md)
- add and subtract fractions, including by creating equivalent fractions, in various contexts
- determine the [greatest common factor](../glossary/greatest-common-factor.md) for a variety of whole numbers up to 144 and the [lowest common multiple](../glossary/lowest-common-multiple.md) for two and three whole numbers
- [evaluate](../glossary/evaluate.md) and express repeated multiplication of whole numbers using [exponential notation](../glossary/exponential-notation.md), in various contexts
- multiply and divide fractions by fractions, using tools in various contexts
- multiply and divide decimal numbers by decimal numbers, in various contexts
- identify [proportional](../glossary/proportional.md) and non-proportional situations and apply [proportional reasoning](../glossary/proportional-reasoning.md) to solve problems

<sub>The source curriculum adds 40 teacher prompts, examples, and instructional notes for these expectations.</sub>


*Transferable skills developed in this strand: Critical Thinking & Problem Solving, Communication.*

### C. Algebra

*By the end of Grade 7, students will:*

**C1 Patterns and Relationships.** identify, describe, extend, create, and make predictions about a variety of patterns, including those found in real-life contexts

- identify and compare a variety of [repeating](../glossary/repeating-pattern-5a556eeb.md), [growing](../glossary/growing-pattern.md), and [shrinking patterns](../glossary/shrinking-pattern-1b765839.md), including patterns found in real-life contexts, and compare [linear](../glossary/linear-pattern.md) growing patterns on the basis of their [constant rates](../glossary/constant-rate.md) and initial values
- create and [translate](../glossary/translate-6bbeec99.md) repeating, growing, and shrinking patterns involving whole numbers and decimal numbers using various [representations](../glossary/representation.md), including [algebraic expressions](../glossary/algebraic-expression.md) and [equations](../glossary/equation.md) for linear growing patterns
- determine [pattern rules](../glossary/pattern-rule.md) and use them to extend patterns, make and justify predictions, and identify missing [elements](../glossary/element.md) in repeating, growing, and shrinking patterns involving whole numbers and decimal numbers, and use algebraic representations of the pattern rules to solve for unknown values in linear growing patterns
- create and describe patterns to illustrate [relationships](../glossary/relationship-f5b5e0bf.md) among integers

<sub>The source curriculum adds 18 teacher prompts, examples, and instructional notes for these expectations.</sub>

**C2 Equations and Inequalities.** demonstrate an understanding of variables, expressions, equalities, and inequalities, and apply this understanding in various contexts

- add and subtract [monomials](../glossary/monomial.md) with a degree of 1 that involve whole numbers, using tools
- [evaluate](../glossary/evaluate.md) algebraic expressions that involve whole numbers and decimal numbers
- solve equations that involve multiple [terms](../glossary/term-b0dc5ad8.md), whole numbers, and decimal numbers in various contexts, and verify solutions
- solve [inequalities](../glossary/inequality.md) that involve multiple terms and whole numbers, and verify and graph the solutions

<sub>The source curriculum adds 17 teacher prompts, examples, and instructional notes for these expectations.</sub>

**C3 Coding.** solve problems and create computational representations of mathematical situations using coding concepts and skills

- solve problems and create [computational representations](../glossary/computational-representations.md) of mathematical situations by writing and executing [efficient code](../glossary/efficient-code-coding.md), including code that involves events influenced by a [defined count](../glossary/defined-count-coding.md) and/or [subprogram](../glossary/subprogram-coding.md) and other [control structures](../glossary/control-structure-coding.md)
- read and alter existing code, including code that involves events influenced by a defined count and/or subprogram and other control structures, and describe how changes to the code affect the [outcomes](../glossary/outcome-coding.md) and the efficiency of the code

<sub>The source curriculum adds 8 teacher prompts, examples, and instructional notes for these expectations.</sub>

**C4 Mathematical Modelling.** apply the process of mathematical modelling to represent, analyse, make predictions, and provide insight into real-life situations 
 
 This overall expectation has no specific expectations. [Mathematical modelling](../glossary/mathematical-modelling.md) is an iterative and interconnected process that is applied to various contexts, allowing students to bring in learning from other strands. Students’ demonstration of the process of mathematical modelling, as they apply concepts and skills learned in other strands, is assessed and evaluated. 
 Read more about the mathematical modelling process.


*Transferable skills developed in this strand: Critical Thinking & Problem Solving, Communication, Digital Literacy.*

### D. Data

*By the end of Grade 7, students will:*

**D1 Data Literacy.** manage, analyse, and use data to make convincing arguments and informed decisions, in various contexts drawn from real life

- explain why percentages are used to represent the [distribution](../glossary/distribution.md) of a variable for a [population](../glossary/population.md) or sample in large sets of data, and provide examples
- collect [qualitative data](../glossary/qualitative-data.md) and [discrete](../glossary/discrete-data.md) and [continuous](../glossary/continuous-data.md) [quantitative data](../glossary/quantitative-data.md) to answer [questions of interest](../glossary/question-of-interest.md), and organize the sets of data as appropriate, including using percentages
- select from among a variety of graphs, including [circle graphs](../glossary/circle-graph.md), the type of graph best suited to represent various sets of data; display the data in the graphs with proper sources, titles, and labels, and appropriate scales; and justify their choice of graphs
- create an [infographic](../glossary/infographic.md) about a data set, representing the data in appropriate ways, including in tables and circle graphs, and incorporating any other relevant information that helps to tell a story about the data
- determine the impact of adding or removing data from a data set on a [measure of central tendency](../glossary/measures-of-central-tendency.md), and describe how these changes alter the shape and distribution of the data
- analyse different sets of data presented in various ways, including in circle graphs and in [misleading graphs](../glossary/misleading-graph.md), by asking and answering questions about the data, challenging preconceived notions, and drawing conclusions, then make convincing arguments and informed decisions

<sub>The source curriculum adds 23 teacher prompts, examples, and instructional notes for these expectations.</sub>

**D2 Probability.** describe the likelihood that events will happen, and use that information to make predictions

- describe the difference between [independent](../glossary/independent-events.md) and [dependent events](../glossary/dependent-events.md), and explain how their probabilities differ, providing examples
- determine and compare the [theoretical](../glossary/theoretical-probability.md) and [experimental probabilities](../glossary/experimental-probability.md) of two independent events happening and of two dependent events happening

<sub>The source curriculum adds 8 teacher prompts, examples, and instructional notes for these expectations.</sub>


*Transferable skills developed in this strand: Critical Thinking & Problem Solving, Digital Literacy, Innovation, Creativity & Entrepreneurship, Communication.*

### E. Spatial Sense

*By the end of Grade 7, students will:*

**E1 Geometric and Spatial Reasoning.** describe and represent shape, location, and movement by applying [geometric properties](../glossary/geometric-property.md) and spatial relationships in order to navigate the world around them

- describe and classify [cylinders](../glossary/cylinder.md), [pyramids](../glossary/pyramid-bd42afae.md), and [prisms](../glossary/prism-cc799aab.md) according to their geometric properties, including [plane](../glossary/plane-symmetry.md) and [rotational symmetry](../glossary/rotational-symmetry.md)
- draw top, front, and side views, as well as [perspective views](../glossary/perspective-view.md), of objects and physical spaces, using appropriate scales
- perform [dilations](../glossary/dilation.md) and describe the similarity between the image and the original shape
- describe and perform [translations](../glossary/translation-ab11c126.md), [reflections](../glossary/reflection.md), and [rotations](../glossary/rotation.md) on a [Cartesian plane](../glossary/cartesian-plane.md), and predict the results of these [transformations](../glossary/transformation-c2e94529.md)

<sub>The source curriculum adds 24 teacher prompts, examples, and instructional notes for these expectations.</sub>

**E2 Measurement.** compare, estimate, and determine measurements in various contexts

- describe the differences and similarities between [volume](../glossary/volume.md) and [capacity](../glossary/capacity.md), and apply the relationship between millilitres (mL) and cubic centimetres (cm3) to solve problems
- solve problems involving [perimeter](../glossary/perimeter.md), area, and volume that require converting from one [metric unit](../glossary/metric-unit.md) of measurement to another
- use the relationships between the [radius](../glossary/radius.md), [diameter](../glossary/diameter.md), and [circumference](../glossary/circumference.md) of a [circle](../glossary/circle.md) to explain the [formula](../glossary/formula.md) for finding the circumference and to solve related problems
- construct circles when given the radius, diameter, or circumference
- show the relationships between the radius, diameter, and area of a circle, and use these relationships to explain the formula for measuring the area of a circle and to solve related problems
- represent cylinders as [nets](../glossary/net.md) and determine their [surface area](../glossary/surface-area.md) by adding the areas of their parts
- show that the volume of a [prism](../glossary/prism-cc799aab.md) or [cylinder](../glossary/cylinder.md) can be determined by multiplying the area of its [base](../glossary/base-shapes.md) by its height, and apply this relationship to find the area of the base, volume, and height of prisms and cylinders when given two of the three measurements

<sub>The source curriculum adds 31 teacher prompts, examples, and instructional notes for these expectations.</sub>


*Transferable skills developed in this strand: Critical Thinking & Problem Solving, Communication.*

### F. Financial Literacy

*By the end of Grade 7, students will:*

**F1 Money and Finances.** demonstrate the knowledge and skills needed to make informed financial decisions

- identify and compare [exchange rates](../glossary/exchange-rate.md), and convert foreign [currencies](../glossary/currency.md) to Canadian dollars and vice versa
- identify and describe various reliable sources of information that can help with planning for and reaching a [financial goal](../glossary/financial-goal.md)
- create, track, and adjust sample[budgets](../glossary/budget.md) designed to meet longer-term financial goals for various scenarios
- identify various societal and personal factors that may influence [financial decision making](../glossary/financial-decision-making.md), and describe the effects that each might have
- explain how [interest rates](../glossary/interest-rate.md) can impact [savings](../glossary/saving.md), [investments](../glossary/investment.md), and the cost of [borrowing](../glossary/borrowing.md) to pay for [goods and services](../glossary/goods-and-services.md) over time
- compare interest rates and [fees](../glossary/fees.md) for different accounts and loans offered by various [financial institutions](../glossary/financial-institution.md), and determine the best option for different scenarios

<sub>The source curriculum adds 24 teacher prompts, examples, and instructional notes for these expectations.</sub>


*Transferable skills developed in this strand: Critical Thinking & Problem Solving, Global Citizenship and Sustainability, Innovation, Creativity & Entrepreneurship.*
