# Mathematics (MTH1W)

**Mathematics (MTH1W)** is a Grade 9 destreamed course in [Mathematics](../subjects/mathematics.md).

This course enables students to consolidate, and continue to develop, an understanding of mathematical concepts related to number sense and operations, algebra, measurement, geometry, data, probability, and financial literacy. Students will use mathematical processes, mathematical modelling, and coding to make sense of the mathematics they are learning and to apply their understanding to culturally responsive and relevant real-world situations. Students will continue to enhance their mathematical reasoning skills, including proportional reasoning, spatial reasoning, and algebraic reasoning, as they solve problems and communicate their thinking.

## Before you take this course

This course has no formal prerequisites.

## Where this course leads

Completing this course opens the door to [Mathematics for Work and Everyday Life (MEL3E)](MEL3E.md).

## What students learn

The learning is organized into 7 strands: Social-Emotional Learning (SEL) Skills in Mathematics, Mathematical Thinking and Making Connections, Number, Algebra, Data, Geometry and Measurement, and Financial Literacy.

### AA. Social-Emotional Learning (SEL) Skills in Mathematics

*Throughout this course, in the context of learning related to the other strands, students will:*

**AA1 Social-Emotional Learning Skills.** develop and explore a variety of social-emotional learning skills in a context that supports and reflects this learning in connection with the expectations across all other strands


### A. Mathematical Thinking and Making Connections

*Throughout this course, in connection with the learning in the other strands, students will:*

**A1 Mathematical Processes.** apply the mathematical processes to develop a conceptual understanding of, and procedural fluency with, the mathematics they are learning

**A2 Making Connections.** make connections between mathematics and various [knowledge systems](../glossary/knowledge-systems.md), their lived experiences, and various real-life applications of mathematics, including careers


*Transferable skills developed in this strand: Communication, Collaboration, Critical Thinking & Problem Solving, Self-Directed Learning, Global Citizenship and Sustainability.*

### B. Number

*By the end of this course, students will:*

**B1 Development of Numbers and Number Sets.** demonstrate an understanding of the development and use of numbers, and make connections between [sets](../glossary/set.md) of numbers

- research a number concept to tell a story about its development and use in a specific culture, and describe its relevance in a current context
- describe how various [subsets](../glossary/subset.md) of a [number system](../glossary/number-system.md) are defined, and describe similarities and differences between these subsets
- use patterns and number relationships to explain [density](../glossary/density.md), [infinity](../glossary/infinity.md), and [limit](../glossary/limit.md) as they relate to number sets

<sub>The source curriculum adds 24 teacher prompts, examples, and instructional notes for these expectations.</sub>

**B2 Powers.** represent numbers in various ways, [evaluate](../glossary/evaluate-0eb501b2.md) [powers](../glossary/power-b8727f21.md), and [simplify](../glossary/simplify.md) [expressions](../glossary/expression-e7a5d782.md) by using the relationships between powers and their [exponents](../glossary/exponent-8f459230.md)

- analyse, through the use of patterning, the relationship between the sign and size of an exponent and the value of a power, and use this relationship to express numbers in [scientific notation](../glossary/scientific-notation-b34376e8.md) and evaluate powers
- analyse, through the use of patterning, the relationships between the exponents of powers and the operations with powers, and use these relationships to simplify numeric and [algebraic expressions](../glossary/algebraic-expression-b0207d4b.md)

<sub>The source curriculum adds 14 teacher prompts, examples, and instructional notes for these expectations.</sub>

**B3 Number Sense and Operations.** apply an understanding of [rational numbers](../glossary/rational-number-c3745a19.md), [ratios](../glossary/ratio-19b55b11.md), [rates](../glossary/rate-0134e47c.md), [percentages](../glossary/percent-50baef66.md), and [proportions](../glossary/proportion-d6358dbc.md), in various mathematical contexts, and to solve problems

- apply an understanding of [integers](../glossary/integer-23ea5883.md) to describe location, direction, amount, and changes in any of these, in various contexts
- apply an understanding of [unit fractions](../glossary/unit-fraction.md) and their relationship to other fractional amounts, in various contexts, including the use of measuring tools
- apply an understanding of integers to explain the effects that positive and negative signs have on the values of ratios, rates, fractions, and decimals, in various contexts
- solve problems involving operations with positive and negative fractions and [mixed numbers](../glossary/mixed-number-03051376.md), including problems involving formulas, measurements, and linear relations, using technology when appropriate
- pose and solve problems involving rates, percentages, and proportions in various contexts, including contexts connected to real-life applications of data, measurement, geometry, linear relations, and financial literacy

<sub>The source curriculum adds 39 teacher prompts, examples, and instructional notes for these expectations.</sub>


*Transferable skills developed in this strand: Self-Directed Learning, Global Citizenship and Sustainability, Critical Thinking & Problem Solving, Communication, Digital Literacy.*

### C. Algebra

*By the end of this course, students will:*

**C1 Algebraic Expressions and Equations.** demonstrate an understanding of the development and use of algebraic concepts and of their connection to numbers, using various tools and representations

- research an algebraic concept to tell a story about its development and use in a specific culture, and describe its relevance in a current context
- create [algebraic expressions](../glossary/algebraic-expression-b0207d4b.md) to [generalize](../glossary/generalize.md) relationships expressed in words, numbers, and visual representations, in various contexts
- compare algebraic expressions using concrete, numerical, graphical, and algebraic methods to identify those that are equivalent, and justify their choices
- simplify algebraic expressions by applying properties of operations of numbers, using various representations and tools, in different contexts
- create and solve equations for various contexts, and verify their solutions

<sub>The source curriculum adds 33 teacher prompts, examples, and instructional notes for these expectations.</sub>

**C2 Coding.** apply [coding](../glossary/coding-0e7cdc9f.md) skills to represent mathematical concepts and relationships dynamically, and to solve problems, in algebra and across the other strands

- use coding to demonstrate an understanding of algebraic concepts including [variables](../glossary/variable.md), [parameters](../glossary/parameter.md), [equations](../glossary/equation-56a842b9.md), and [inequalities](../glossary/inequality-8cf97660.md)
- create code by decomposing situations into computational steps in order to represent mathematical concepts and relationships, and to solve problems
- read code to predict its outcome, and alter code to adjust [constraints](../glossary/constraint.md), parameters, and outcomes to represent a similar or new mathematical situation

<sub>The source curriculum adds 20 teacher prompts, examples, and instructional notes for these expectations.</sub>

**C3 Application of Relations.** represent and compare [linear](../glossary/linear-relation.md) and [non-linear relations](../glossary/non-linear-relation.md) that [model](../glossary/model-a5301a2e.md) real-life situations, and use these representations to make predictions

- compare the shapes of graphs of linear and non-linear relations to describe their [rates of change](../glossary/rate-of-change-56385527.md), to make connections to [growing](../glossary/growing-pattern-62a3ce4a.md) and [shrinking patterns](../glossary/shrinking-pattern.md), and to make predictions
- represent linear relations using concrete materials, tables of values, graphs, and equations, and make connections between the various representations to demonstrate an understanding of rates of change and [initial values](../glossary/initial-value.md)
- compare two linear relations of the form y = ax + b graphically and algebraically, and interpret the meaning of their point of intersection in terms of a given context

<sub>The source curriculum adds 18 teacher prompts, examples, and instructional notes for these expectations.</sub>

**C4 Characteristics of Relations.** demonstrate an understanding of the characteristics of various representations of linear and non-linear relations, using tools, including coding when appropriate

- compare characteristics of graphs, tables of values, and equations of linear and non-linear relations
- graph relations represented as algebraic equations of the forms x = k, y = k, x + y = k, x – y = k, ax + by = k, and xy = k, and their associated inequalities, where a, b, and k are [constants](../glossary/constant-83735b5b.md), to identify various characteristics and the points and/or regions defined by these equations and inequalities
- [translate](../glossary/translation.md), [reflect](../glossary/reflection-ec65e55e.md), and [rotate](../glossary/rotation-359f32ef.md) lines defined by y = ax, where a is a constant, and describe how each [transformation](../glossary/transformation-a5f18224.md) affects the graphs and equations of the defined lines
- determine the equations of lines from graphs, tables of values, and concrete representations of linear relations by making connections between rates of change and [slopes](../glossary/slope.md), and between initial values and [y-intercepts](../glossary/y-intercept.md), and use these equations to solve problems

<sub>The source curriculum adds 24 teacher prompts, examples, and instructional notes for these expectations.</sub>


*Transferable skills developed in this strand: Communication, Global Citizenship and Sustainability, Critical Thinking & Problem Solving, Digital Literacy, Self-Directed Learning.*

### D. Data

*By the end of this course, students will:*

**D1 Collection, Representation, and Analysis of Data.** describe the collection and use of data, and represent and analyse data involving one and two variables

- identify a current context involving a large amount of data, and describe potential implications and consequences of its collection, storage, representation, and use
- represent and [statistically analyse](../glossary/statistically-analyse.md) data from a real-life situation involving a single variable in various ways, including the use of [quartile values](../glossary/quartile-values.md) and [box plots](../glossary/box-plot.md)
- create a [scatter plot](../glossary/scatter-plot-ce2ebd2d.md) to represent the relationship between two variables, determine the [correlation](../glossary/correlation-579e2253.md) between these variables by testing different [regression](../glossary/regression.md) models using technology, and use a model to make predictions when appropriate

<sub>The source curriculum adds 19 teacher prompts, examples, and instructional notes for these expectations.</sub>

**D2 Mathematical Modelling.** apply the [process of mathematical modelling](../glossary/mathematical-modelling-process.md), using data and mathematical concepts from other strands, to represent, analyse, make predictions, and provide insight into real-life situations

- describe the value of [mathematical modelling](../glossary/mathematical-modelling-process.md) and how it is used in real life to inform decisions
- identify a question of interest requiring the collection and analysis of data, and identify the information needed to answer the question
- create a plan to collect the necessary data on the question of interest from an appropriate [source](../glossary/source-98d066ce.md), identify [assumptions](../glossary/assumption.md), identify what may vary and what may remain the same in the situation, and then carry out the plan
- determine ways to display and analyse the data in order to create a mathematical model to answer the original question of interest, taking into account the nature of the data, the context, and the assumptions made
- report how the model can be used to answer the question of interest, how well the model fits the context, potential limitations of the model, and what predictions can be made based on the model

<sub>The source curriculum adds 24 teacher prompts, examples, and instructional notes for these expectations.</sub>


*Transferable skills developed in this strand: Communication, Critical Thinking & Problem Solving, Global Citizenship and Sustainability, Innovation, Creativity & Entrepreneurship.*

### E. Geometry and Measurement

*By the end of this course, students will:*

**E1 Geometric and Measurement Relationships.** demonstrate an understanding of the development and use of geometric and measurement relationships, and apply these relationships to solve problems, including problems involving real-life situations

- research a geometric concept or a measurement system to tell a story about its development and use in a specific culture or community, and describe its relevance in connection to careers and to other disciplines
- create and analyse designs involving geometric relationships and circle and triangle properties, using various tools
- solve problems involving different units within a [measurement system](../glossary/measurement-system.md) and between measurement systems, including those from various cultures or communities, using various representations and technology, when appropriate
- show how changing one or more dimensions of a two-dimensional shape and a [three-dimensional object](../glossary/three-dimensional-object.md) affects perimeter/circumference, area, surface area, and volume, using technology when appropriate
- solve problems involving the side-length relationship for right triangles in real-life situations, including problems that involve [composite shapes](../glossary/composite-shape.md)
- solve problems using the relationships between the volume of [prisms](../glossary/prism-c176c9d0.md) and [pyramids](../glossary/pyramid-c83ac36e.md) and between the volume of [cylinders](../glossary/cylinder-9baca03c.md) and [cones](../glossary/cone-617c19a1.md), involving various units of measure

<sub>The source curriculum adds 53 teacher prompts, examples, and instructional notes for these expectations.</sub>


*Transferable skills developed in this strand: Communication, Global Citizenship and Sustainability, Critical Thinking & Problem Solving.*

### F. Financial Literacy

*By the end of this course, students will:*

**F1 Financial Decisions.** demonstrate the knowledge and skills needed to make informed financial decisions

- identify a past or current financial situation and explain how it can inform financial decisions, by applying an understanding of the context of the situation and related mathematical knowledge
- identify financial situations that involve [appreciation](../glossary/appreciation.md) and [depreciation](../glossary/depreciation.md), and use associated graphs to answer related questions
- compare the effects that different interest rates, lengths of borrowing time, ways in which interest is calculated, and amounts of down payments have on the overall costs associated with purchasing goods or services, using appropriate tools
- modify [budgets](../glossary/budget-afac1d3e.md) displayed in various ways to reflect specific changes in circumstances, and provide a rationale for the modifications

<sub>The source curriculum adds 23 teacher prompts, examples, and instructional notes for these expectations.</sub>


*Transferable skills developed in this strand: Self-Directed Learning, Global Citizenship and Sustainability, Critical Thinking & Problem Solving.*
