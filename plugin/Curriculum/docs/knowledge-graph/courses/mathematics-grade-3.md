# Mathematics (Grade 3)

**Mathematics** is the Grade 3 Mathematics program in [Mathematics](../subjects/mathematics.md).

Here you will find the expectations and learning for the six areas of study for Grade 3 Mathematics.

## Before you take this course

This course has no formal prerequisites.

## What students learn

The learning is organized into 6 strands: Social-Emotional Learning (SEL) Skills in Mathematics and the Mathematical Processes, Number, Algebra, Data, Spatial Sense, and Financial Literacy.

### A. Social-Emotional Learning (SEL) Skills in Mathematics and the Mathematical Processes

*Throughout this grade, in order to promote a positive identity as a math learner, to foster well-being and the ability to learn, build resilience, and thrive, students will:*

**A1 Social-Emotional Learning (SEL) Skills and the Mathematical Processes.** apply, to the best of their ability, a variety of social-emotional learning skills to support their use of the mathematical processes and their learning in connection with the expectations in the other five strands of the mathematics curriculum


### B. Number

*By the end of Grade 3, students will:*

**B1 Number Sense.** demonstrate an understanding of numbers and make connections to the way numbers are used in everyday life

- read, [represent](../glossary/represent.md), [compose](../glossary/compose-61cac0c1.md), and [decompose](../glossary/decomposition-of-numbers.md) [whole numbers](../glossary/whole-number.md) up to and including 1000, using a variety of [tools](../glossary/tools-9e67a3c2.md) and strategies, and describe various ways they are used in everyday life
- compare and order whole numbers up to and including 1000, in various contexts
- [round](../glossary/round-e1d768f1.md) whole numbers to the nearest ten or hundred, in various contexts
- [count](../glossary/count.md) to 1000, including by 50s, 100s, and 200s, using a variety of tools and strategies
- use [place value](../glossary/place-value.md) when describing and representing multi-digit numbers in a variety of ways, including with [base ten materials](../glossary/base-ten-materials.md)
- use [drawings](../glossary/drawing-20030707.md) to represent, solve, and compare the results of [fair-share problems](../glossary/fair-share-problem.md) that involve sharing up to 20 items among 2, 3, 4, 5, 6, 8, and 10 sharers, including problems that result in whole numbers, [mixed numbers](../glossary/mixed-number.md), and fractional amounts
- represent and solve [fair-share problems](../glossary/fair-share-problem.md) that focus on determining and using [equivalent fractions](../glossary/equivalent-fractions.md), including problems that involve halves, fourths, and eighths; thirds and sixths; and fifths and tenths

<sub>The source curriculum adds 34 teacher prompts, examples, and instructional notes for these expectations.</sub>

**B2 Operations.** use knowledge of numbers and operations to solve mathematical problems encountered in everyday life

- use the properties of operations, and the relationships between multiplication and division, to solve problems and check calculations
- recall and demonstrate multiplication facts of 2, 5, and 10, and related division facts
- use [mental math strategies](../glossary/mental-math-strategies.md), including [estimation](../glossary/estimation.md), to add and subtract whole numbers that add up to no more than 1000, and explain the strategies used
- demonstrate an understanding of [algorithms](../glossary/algorithm.md) for adding and subtracting whole numbers by making connections to and describing the way other tools and strategies are used to add and subtract
- represent and solve problems involving the addition and subtraction of whole numbers that add up to no more than 1000, using various tools and algorithms
- represent multiplication of numbers up to 10 × 10 and division up to 100 ÷ 10, using a variety of tools and drawings, including [arrays](../glossary/array.md)
- represent and solve problems involving multiplication and division, including problems that involve groups of one half, one fourth, and one third, using tools and drawings
- represent the connection between the [numerator](../glossary/numerator.md) of a fraction and the repeated addition of the [unit fraction](../glossary/unit-fraction-32c07bc4.md) with the same [denominator](../glossary/denominator.md) using various tools and drawings, and [standard fractional notation](../glossary/standard-fractional-notation.md)
- use the [ratios](../glossary/ratio.md) of 1 to 2, 1 to 5, and 1 to 10 to [scale](../glossary/scale-on-a-graph.md) up numbers and to solve problems

<sub>The source curriculum adds 43 teacher prompts, examples, and instructional notes for these expectations.</sub>


*Transferable skills developed in this strand: Critical Thinking & Problem Solving, Communication.*

### C. Algebra

*By the end of Grade 3, students will:*

**C1 Patterns and Relationships.** identify, describe, extend, create, and make predictions about a variety of patterns, including those found in real-life contexts

- identify and describe repeating [elements](../glossary/element.md) and operations in a variety of patterns, including patterns found in real-life contexts
- create and[translate](../glossary/translate-6bbeec99.md) patterns that have repeating elements, movements, or operations using various [representations](../glossary/representation.md), including shapes, numbers, and [tables of values](../glossary/table-of-values.md)
- determine [pattern rules](../glossary/pattern-rule.md) and use them to extend patterns, make and justify predictions, and identify missing elements in patterns that have repeating elements, movements, or operations
- create and describe patterns to illustrate [relationships](../glossary/relationship-f5b5e0bf.md) among whole numbers up to 1000

<sub>The source curriculum adds 19 teacher prompts, examples, and instructional notes for these expectations.</sub>

**C2 Equations and Inequalities.** demonstrate an understanding of variables, expressions, equalities, and inequalities, and apply this understanding in various contexts

- describe how [variables](../glossary/variable-1b812434.md) are used, and use them in various contexts as appropriate
- determine whether given sets of addition, subtraction, multiplication, and division [expressions](../glossary/expression.md) are [equivalent](../glossary/equivalence.md) or not
- identify and use equivalent relationships for whole numbers up to 1000, in various contexts

<sub>The source curriculum adds 12 teacher prompts, examples, and instructional notes for these expectations.</sub>

**C3 Coding.** solve problems and create computational representations of mathematical situations using coding concepts and skills

- solve problems and create [computational representations](../glossary/computational-representations.md) of mathematical situations by writing and [executing code](../glossary/execute-coding.md), including [code](../glossary/code.md) that involves [sequential](../glossary/sequential-events-coding.md), [concurrent](../glossary/concurrent-events-coding.md), and [repeating events](../glossary/repeating-event-coding.md)
- read and alter existing code, including code that involves sequential, concurrent, and repeating events, and describe how changes to the code affect the [outcomes](../glossary/outcome-coding.md)

<sub>The source curriculum adds 9 teacher prompts, examples, and instructional notes for these expectations.</sub>

**C4 Mathematical Modelling.** apply the process of mathematical modelling to represent, analyse, make predictions, and provide insight into real-life situations 
 
 This overall expectation has no specific expectations. [Mathematical modelling](../glossary/mathematical-modelling.md) is an iterative and interconnected process that is applied to various contexts, allowing students to bring in learning from other strands. Students’ demonstration of the process of mathematical modelling, as they apply concepts and skills learned in other strands, is assessed and evaluated. 
 Read more about the mathematical modelling process.


*Transferable skills developed in this strand: Critical Thinking & Problem Solving, Communication, Digital Literacy.*

### D. Data

*By the end of Grade 3, students will:*

**D1 Data Literacy.** manage, analyse, and use data to make convincing arguments and informed decisions, in various contexts drawn from real life

- sort sets of data about people or things according to two and three [attributes](../glossary/attribute.md), using tables and logic diagrams, including [Venn](../glossary/venn-diagram-ca7ba686.md), [Carroll](../glossary/carroll-diagram.md), and [tree diagrams](../glossary/tree-diagram.md), as appropriate
- collect data through observations, experiments, and interviews to answer [questions of interest](../glossary/question-of-interest.md) that focus on [qualitative](../glossary/qualitative-data.md) and [quantitative data](../glossary/quantitative-data.md), and organize the data using [frequency tables](../glossary/frequency-table.md)
- display sets of data, using [many-to-one correspondence](../glossary/many-to-one-correspondence.md), in [pictographs](../glossary/pictograph.md) and [bar graphs](../glossary/bar-graph.md) with proper sources, titles, and labels, and appropriate scales
- determine the [mean](../glossary/mean.md) and identify the [mode](../glossary/mode.md)(s), if any, for various data sets involving whole numbers, and explain what each of these measures indicates about the data
- analyse different sets of data presented in various ways, including in frequency tables and in graphs with different scales, by asking and answering questions about the data and drawing conclusions, then make convincing arguments and informed decisions

<sub>The source curriculum adds 23 teacher prompts, examples, and instructional notes for these expectations.</sub>

**D2 Probability.** describe the likelihood that events will happen, and use that information to make predictions

- use mathematical language, including the terms “impossible”, “unlikely”, “equally likely”, “likely”, and “certain”, to describe the [likelihood](../glossary/likelihood.md) of events happening, and use that likelihood to make predictions and informed decisions
- make and test predictions about the likelihood that the mean and the mode(s) of a data set will be the same for data collected from different [populations](../glossary/population.md)

<sub>The source curriculum adds 7 teacher prompts, examples, and instructional notes for these expectations.</sub>


*Transferable skills developed in this strand: Critical Thinking & Problem Solving, Digital Literacy, Innovation, Creativity & Entrepreneurship, Communication.*

### E. Spatial Sense

*By the end of Grade 3, students will:*

**E1 Geometric and Spatial Reasoning.** describe and represent shape, location, and movement by applying [geometric properties](../glossary/geometric-property.md) and spatial relationships in order to navigate the world around them

- [sort](../glossary/sort.md), construct, and identify [cubes](../glossary/cube.md), [prisms](../glossary/prism-cc799aab.md), [pyramids](../glossary/pyramid-bd42afae.md), [cylinders](../glossary/cylinder.md), and [cones](../glossary/cone.md) by comparing their faces, edges, vertices, and angles
- [compose](../glossary/compose-61cac0c1.md) and [decompose](../glossary/decompose.md) various structures, and identify the [two-dimensional shapes](../glossary/two-dimensional-shape.md) and [three-dimensional objects](../glossary/three-dimensional-object-e7630a4b.md) that these structures contain
- identify [congruent](../glossary/congruent.md) lengths, angles, and faces of three-dimensional objects by mentally and physically matching them, and determine if the objects are congruent
- give and follow multistep instructions involving movement from one location to another, including distances and half- and quarter-turns

<sub>The source curriculum adds 18 teacher prompts, examples, and instructional notes for these expectations.</sub>

**E2 Measurement.** compare, estimate, and determine measurements in various contexts

- use appropriate units of length to estimate, measure, and compare the [perimeters](../glossary/perimeter.md) of polygons and curved shapes, and construct polygons with a given perimeter
- explain the relationships between millimetres, centimetres, metres, and kilometres as metric units of length, and use [benchmarks](../glossary/benchmark.md) for these units to estimate lengths
- use [non-standard units](../glossary/non-standard-units.md) appropriately to estimate, measure, and compare capacity, and explain the effect that overfilling or underfilling, and gaps between units, have on accuracy
- compare, estimate, and measure the [mass](../glossary/mass.md) of various objects, using a [pan balance](../glossary/pan-balance.md) and non-standard units
- use various units of different sizes to measure the same [attribute](../glossary/attribute.md) of a given item, and demonstrate that even though using different-sized units produces a different count, the size of the attribute remains the same
- use [analog](../glossary/analog-clock.md) and [digital clocks](../glossary/digital-clock.md) and timers to tell time in hours, minutes, and seconds
- compare the areas of two-dimensional shapes by matching, covering, or decomposing and recomposing the shapes, and demonstrate that different shapes can have the same area
- use appropriate non-standard units to measure area, and explain the effect that gaps and overlaps have on accuracy
- use square centimetres (cm2) and square metres (m2) to estimate, measure, and compare the areas of various two-dimensional shapes, including those with curved sides

<sub>The source curriculum adds 43 teacher prompts, examples, and instructional notes for these expectations.</sub>


*Transferable skills developed in this strand: Critical Thinking & Problem Solving, Communication.*

### F. Financial Literacy

*By the end of Grade 3, students will:*

**F1 Money and Finances.** demonstrate an understanding of the value and use of Canadian currency

- estimate and calculate the change required for various simple [cash transactions](../glossary/cash-transaction.md) involving whole-dollar amounts and amounts of less than one dollar
