# Mathematics (Grade 4)

**Mathematics** is the Grade 4 Mathematics program in [Mathematics](../subjects/mathematics.md).

Here you will find the expectations and learning for the six areas of study for Grade 4 Mathematics.

## Before you take this course

This course has no formal prerequisites.

## What students learn

The learning is organized into 6 strands: Social-Emotional Learning (SEL) Skills in Mathematics and the Mathematical Processes, Number, Algebra, Data, Spatial Sense, and Financial Literacy.

### A. Social-Emotional Learning (SEL) Skills in Mathematics and the Mathematical Processes

*Throughout this grade, in order to promote a positive identity as a math learner, to foster well-being and the ability to learn, build resilience, and thrive, students will:*

**A1 Social-Emotional Learning (SEL) Skills and the Mathematical Processes.** apply, to the best of their ability, a variety of social-emotional learning skills to support their use of the mathematical processes and their learning in connection with the expectations in the other five strands of the mathematics curriculum


### B. Number

*By the end of Grade 4, students will:*

**B1 Number Sense.** demonstrate an understanding of numbers and make connections to the way numbers are used in everyday life

- read, [represent](../glossary/represent.md), [compose](../glossary/compose-61cac0c1.md), and [decompose](../glossary/decomposition-of-numbers.md) [whole numbers](../glossary/whole-number.md) up to and including 10 000, using appropriate [tools](../glossary/tools-9e67a3c2.md) and strategies, and describe various ways they are used in everyday life
- compare and order whole numbers up to and including 10 000, in various contexts
- [round](../glossary/round-e1d768f1.md) whole numbers to the nearest ten, hundred, or thousand, in various contexts
- represent [fractions](../glossary/fraction.md) from halves to tenths using [drawings](../glossary/drawing-20030707.md), tools, and [standard fractional notation](../glossary/standard-fractional-notation.md), and explain the meanings of the [denominator](../glossary/denominator.md) and the [numerator](../glossary/numerator.md)
- use drawings and [models](../glossary/model.md) to represent, compare, and order fractions representing the individual portions that result from two different [fair-share](../glossary/fair-share-problem.md) scenarios involving any combination of 2, 3, 4, 5, 6, 8, and 10 sharers
- [count](../glossary/count.md) to 10 by halves, thirds, fourths, fifths, sixths, eighths, and tenths, with and without the use of tools
- read, represent, compare, and order [decimal](../glossary/decimal-number.md) tenths, in various contexts
- [round](../glossary/round-e1d768f1.md) decimal numbers to the nearest whole number, in various contexts
- describe [relationships](../glossary/relationship-f5b5e0bf.md) and show equivalences among fractions and decimal tenths, in various contexts

<sub>The source curriculum adds 42 teacher prompts, examples, and instructional notes for these expectations.</sub>

**B2 Operations.** use knowledge of numbers and operations to solve mathematical problems encountered in everyday life

- use the properties of operations, and the relationships between addition, subtraction, multiplication, and division, to solve problems involving whole numbers, including those requiring more than one operation, and check calculations
- recall and demonstrate multiplication facts for 1 × 1 to 10 × 10, and related division facts
- use [mental math strategies](../glossary/mental-math-strategies.md) to multiply whole numbers by 10, 100, and 1000, divide whole numbers by 10, and add and subtract decimal tenths, and explain the strategies used
- represent and solve problems involving the addition and subtraction of whole numbers that add up to no more than 10 000 and of decimal tenths, using appropriate tools and strategies, including [algorithms](../glossary/algorithm.md)
- represent and solve problems involving the multiplication of two- or three-digit whole numbers by one-digit whole numbers and by 10, 100, and 1000, using appropriate tools, including [arrays](../glossary/array.md)
- represent and solve problems involving the division of two- or three-digit whole numbers by one-digit whole numbers, expressing any remainder as a fraction when appropriate, using appropriate tools, including arrays
- represent the relationship between the repeated addition of a [unit fraction](../glossary/unit-fraction-32c07bc4.md) and the multiplication of that unit fraction by a whole number, using tools, drawings, and standard fractional notation
- show simple multiplicative relationships involving whole-number [rates](../glossary/rate.md), using various tools and drawings

<sub>The source curriculum adds 37 teacher prompts, examples, and instructional notes for these expectations.</sub>


*Transferable skills developed in this strand: Critical Thinking & Problem Solving, Communication.*

### C. Algebra

*By the end of Grade 4, students will:*

**C1 Patterns and Relationships.** identify, describe, extend, create, and make predictions about a variety of patterns, including those found in real-life contexts

- identify and describe [repeating](../glossary/repeating-pattern-5a556eeb.md) and [growing patterns](../glossary/growing-pattern.md), including patterns found in real-life contexts
- create and [translate](../glossary/translate-6bbeec99.md) repeating and growing patterns using various [representations](../glossary/representation.md), including [tables of values](../glossary/table-of-values.md) and [graphs](../glossary/graph.md)
- determine [pattern rules](../glossary/pattern-rule.md) and use them to extend patterns, make and justify predictions, and identify missing [elements](../glossary/element.md) in repeating and growing patterns
- create and describe patterns to illustrate [relationships](../glossary/relationship-f5b5e0bf.md) among whole numbers and decimal tenths

<sub>The source curriculum adds 19 teacher prompts, examples, and instructional notes for these expectations.</sub>

**C2 Equations and Inequalities.** demonstrate an understanding of variables, expressions, equalities, and inequalities, and apply this understanding in various contexts

- identify and use symbols as [variables](../glossary/variable-1b812434.md) in [expressions](../glossary/expression.md) and [equations](../glossary/equation.md)
- solve equations that involve whole numbers up to 50 in various contexts, and verify solutions
- solve [inequalities](../glossary/inequality.md) that involve addition and subtraction of whole numbers up to 20, and verify and graph the solutions

<sub>The source curriculum adds 13 teacher prompts, examples, and instructional notes for these expectations.</sub>

**C3 Coding.** solve problems and create computational representations of mathematical situations using coding concepts and skills

- solve problems and create [computational representations](../glossary/computational-representations.md) of mathematical situations by writing and [executing code](../glossary/execute-coding.md), including [code](../glossary/code.md) that involves [sequential](../glossary/sequential-events-coding.md), [concurrent](../glossary/concurrent-events-coding.md), [repeating](../glossary/repeating-event-coding.md), and [nested events](../glossary/nested-events-coding.md)
- read and alter existing code, including code that involves sequential, concurrent, repeating, and nested events, and describe how changes to the code affect the [outcomes](../glossary/outcome-coding.md)

<sub>The source curriculum adds 9 teacher prompts, examples, and instructional notes for these expectations.</sub>

**C4 Mathematical Modelling.** apply the process of mathematical modelling to represent, analyse, make predictions, and provide insight into real-life situations 
 
 This overall expectation has no specific expectations. [Mathematical modelling](../glossary/mathematical-modelling.md) is an iterative and interconnected process that is applied to various contexts, allowing students to bring in learning from other strands. Students’ demonstration of the process of mathematical modelling, as they apply concepts and skills learned in other strands, is assessed and evaluated. 
 Read more about the mathematical modelling process.


*Transferable skills developed in this strand: Critical Thinking & Problem Solving, Communication, Digital Literacy.*

### D. Data

*By the end of Grade 4, students will:*

**D1 Data Literacy.** manage, analyse, and use data to make convincing arguments and informed decisions, in various contexts drawn from real life

- describe the difference between [qualitative](../glossary/qualitative-data.md) and [quantitative data](../glossary/quantitative-data.md), and describe situations where each would be used
- collect data from different [primary](../glossary/primary-data.md) and [secondary sources](../glossary/secondary-sources-75f902ad.md) to answer [questions of interest](../glossary/question-of-interest.md) that involve comparing two or more sets of data, and organize the data in [frequency tables](../glossary/frequency-table.md) and [stem-and-leaf plots](../glossary/stem-and-leaf-plot.md)
- select from among a variety of graphs, including [multiple-bar graphs](../glossary/multiple-bar-graph.md), the type of graph best suited to represent various sets of data; display the data in the graphs with proper sources, titles, and labels, and appropriate scales; and justify their choice of graphs
- create an [infographic](../glossary/infographic.md) about a data set, representing the data in appropriate ways, including in frequency tables, stem-and-leaf plots, and multiple-bar graphs, and incorporating any other relevant information that helps to tell a story about the data
- determine the [mean](../glossary/mean.md) and the [median](../glossary/median.md) and identify the [mode](../glossary/mode.md)(s), if any, for various data sets involving whole numbers, and explain what each of these measures indicates about the data
- analyse different sets of data presented in various ways, including in stem-and-leaf plots and multiple-bar graphs, by asking and answering questions about the data and drawing conclusions, then make convincing arguments and informed decisions

<sub>The source curriculum adds 26 teacher prompts, examples, and instructional notes for these expectations.</sub>

**D2 Probability.** describe the likelihood that events will happen, and use that information to make predictions

- use mathematical language, including the terms “impossible”, “unlikely”, “equally likely”, “likely”, and “certain”, to describe the [likelihood](../glossary/likelihood.md) of events happening, represent this likelihood on a [probability line](../glossary/probability-line.md), and use it to make predictions and informed decisions
- make and test predictions about the likelihood that the mean, median, and mode(s) of a data set will be the same for data collected from different [populations](../glossary/population.md)

<sub>The source curriculum adds 7 teacher prompts, examples, and instructional notes for these expectations.</sub>


*Transferable skills developed in this strand: Critical Thinking & Problem Solving, Digital Literacy, Innovation, Creativity & Entrepreneurship, Communication.*

### E. Spatial Sense

*By the end of Grade 4, students will:*

**E1 Geometric and Spatial Reasoning.** describe and represent shape, location, and movement by applying [geometric properties](../glossary/geometric-property.md) and spatial relationships in order to navigate the world around them

- identify geometric properties of [rectangles](../glossary/rectangle.md), including the number of [right angles](../glossary/right-angle.md), [parallel](../glossary/parallel.md)and [perpendicular](../glossary/perpendicular.md) sides, and [lines of symmetry](../glossary/line-of-symmetry.md)
- plot and read [coordinates](../glossary/coordinates.md) in the [first quadrant of a Cartesian plane](../glossary/first-quadrant-of-a-cartesian-plane.md), and describe the [translations](../glossary/translation-ab11c126.md) that move a point from one coordinate to another
- describe and perform translations and [reflections](../glossary/reflection.md) on a [grid](../glossary/grid.md), and predict the results of these transformations

<sub>The source curriculum adds 14 teacher prompts, examples, and instructional notes for these expectations.</sub>

**E2 Measurement.** compare, estimate, and determine measurements in various contexts

- explain the relationships between grams and kilograms as [metric units](../glossary/metric-unit.md) of [mass](../glossary/mass.md), and between litres and millilitres as metric units of [capacity](../glossary/capacity.md), and use [benchmarks](../glossary/benchmark.md) for these units to estimate mass and capacity
- use [metric prefixes](../glossary/metric-prefix.md) to describe the [relative size](../glossary/relative-size.md) of different metric units, and choose appropriate units and tools to measure length, mass, and capacity
- solve problems involving [elapsed time](../glossary/elapsed-time.md) by applying the relationships between different units of time
- identify [angles](../glossary/angle.md) and classify them as [right](../glossary/right-angle.md), [straight](../glossary/straight-angle.md), [acute](../glossary/acute-angle.md), or [obtuse](../glossary/obtuse-angle.md)
- use the [row and column structure](../glossary/row-and-column-structure.md) of an [array](../glossary/array.md) to measure the areas of rectangles and to show that the area of any rectangle can be found by multiplying its side lengths
- apply the [formula](../glossary/formula.md) for the area of a rectangle to find the unknown measurement when given two of the three

<sub>The source curriculum adds 30 teacher prompts, examples, and instructional notes for these expectations.</sub>


*Transferable skills developed in this strand: Critical Thinking & Problem Solving, Communication.*

### F. Financial Literacy

*By the end of Grade 4, students will:*

**F1 Money and Finances.** demonstrate the knowledge and skills needed to make informed financial decisions

- identify various [methods of payment](../glossary/method-of-payment.md) that can be used to purchase [goods and services](../glossary/goods-and-services.md)
- estimate and calculate the cost of [transactions](../glossary/transaction.md) involving multiple items priced in whole-dollar amounts, not including sales tax, and the amount of change needed when payment is made in cash, using mental math
- explain the concepts of [spending](../glossary/spending.md), [saving](../glossary/saving.md), [earning](../glossary/earning.md), [investing](../glossary/investing.md), and [donating](../glossary/donating.md), and identify key factors to consider when making basic decisions related to each
- explain the relationship between spending and saving, and describe how spending and saving behaviours may differ from one person to another
- describe some ways of determining whether something is reasonably priced and therefore a good purchase

<sub>The source curriculum adds 20 teacher prompts, examples, and instructional notes for these expectations.</sub>


*Transferable skills developed in this strand: Critical Thinking & Problem Solving, Communication, Global Citizenship and Sustainability.*
