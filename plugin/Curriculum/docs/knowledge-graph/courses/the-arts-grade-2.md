# The Arts (Grade 2)

**The Arts** is the Grade 2 The Arts program in [The Arts](../subjects/the-arts.md).

Here you will find an overview of student development and learning in the arts at the primary level, as well as the expectations and learning for the four areas of study for Grade 2.

## Before you take this course

This course has no formal prerequisites.

## What students learn

The learning is organized into 4 strands: Dance, Drama, Music, and Visual Arts.

### A. Dance

*By the end of Grade 2, students will:*

**A1 Creating and Presenting.** apply the creative process to the composition of simple dance phrases, using the [elements of dance](../glossary/elements-of-dance.md) to communicate feelings and ideas

- develop short movement phrases inspired by a variety of activities in their community (e.g., riding a bike; movements from sports, yoga, or playground games/activities) and incorporating different pathways (e.g., straight, curvy), directions (e.g., forward, back, sideways, diagonal), and [shapes](../glossary/shape.md) (e.g., big/small shapes, shapes created individually and with partners)
- use dance as a language to represent the main ideas in poems and stories, with a focus on [body](../glossary/body.md) and [space](../glossary/space.md) (e.g., use arm movements to suggest a cheering crowd; use a circle [pathway](../glossary/pathway.md) to suggest the relationship among several characters; use a smooth and delicate sequence of expanding movements to suggest a butterfly emerging from a cocoon)
- create distinct beginnings and endings for dance [phrases](../glossary/phrase.md) in a variety of ways (e.g., having a moment of silence at the beginning and end of a dance phrase; freezing at the end of a dance phrase; starting and ending in similar or [contrasting](../glossary/contrast.md) shapes; dimming the lights to signal the end of a dance phrase)
- use a variety of locomotor and [non-locomotor movements](../glossary/non-locomotor-movement.md) to depict creatures and objects in the world around them (e.g., depict a large animal with torso, arms, and legs that creeps along at a low level; change movements to interpret the motions of various animals represented by the different musical sections of Camille Saint-Saëns’s Carnival of the Animals)

<sub>The source curriculum adds 4 teacher prompts, examples, and instructional notes for these expectations.</sub>

**A2 Reflecting, Responding, and Analysing.** apply the critical analysis process to communicate their feelings, ideas, and understandings in response to a variety of [dance pieces](../glossary/dance-piece.md) and experiences

- describe the similarities between their own dance phrases and those of others (e.g., similarities in the shapes, pathways, levels, and locomotor or non-locomotor movements used in one another’s dance phrases)
- identify, using dance vocabulary, the elements of dance in their own dance phrases and those of others, and describe how each element is used to communicate meaning (e.g., describe how various aspects of body \[shapes, body parts, locomotor and non-locomotor movements\] and space \[levels, direction\] are used to depict crashing waves)
- identify and give examples of their strengths and areas for growth as dance creators and audience members (e.g., describe to a partner what they do well; identify specific movements, stops, and turns that were effective in their dance)

<sub>The source curriculum adds 3 teacher prompts, examples, and instructional notes for these expectations.</sub>

**A3 Exploring Forms and Cultural Contexts.** demonstrate an understanding of a variety of [dance forms](../glossary/dance-form.md) and [styles](../glossary/style.md) from the past and present, and their social and/or community contexts

- describe, with teacher guidance, a variety of dances from communities around the world that they have seen in the media, at live performances and social gatherings, or in the classroom (e.g., folk dances, ceremonial dances, dances of worship, theatrical dances, social dances)
- identify various reasons why people dance in daily life and various contexts in which they do so (e.g., to socialize \[Bangra\], to dance for the earth \[at powwows\], to celebrate \[Jewish wedding ritual\], for exercise \[hip hop\], to tell stories \[ballet\], to relate history \[West African dance\])

<sub>The source curriculum adds 2 teacher prompts, examples, and instructional notes for these expectations.</sub>


*Transferable skills developed in this strand: Communication, Critical Thinking & Problem Solving, Innovation, Creativity & Entrepreneurship, Self-Directed Learning, Global Citizenship and Sustainability.*

### B. Drama

*By the end of Grade 2, students will:*

**B1 Creating and Presenting.** apply the creative process to [dramatic play](../glossary/dramatic-play.md) and process drama, using the [elements](../glossary/elements-of-drama.md) and [conventions of drama](../glossary/conventions-of-drama.md) to communicate feelings, ideas, and stories

- engage in dramatic play and [role play](../glossary/role-playing-role-play.md), with a focus on exploring main ideas and central characters in stories from diverse communities, times, and places (e.g., retell and enact a story from different points of view; stop at a dramatic point in a story and adopt roles of the characters in the story; enact a [scene](../glossary/scene.md) between characters in a fairy-tale kingdom, animals in the tundra, or neighbours in a back alley)
- demonstrate an understanding of the element of role by communicating thoughts, feelings, and perspectives appropriate to the role being played (e.g., devise and share a group [mime](../glossary/mime.md) showing how characters respond to the tension in a situation of conflict, departure, or anticipation; use [voice](../glossary/voice.md) expressively to convey an [interpretation](../glossary/interpretation.md) of a character’s attitude)
- plan and shape the direction of a dramatic play or role play, building on their own and others’ ideas both in and [out of role](../glossary/out-of-role.md), with support (e.g., In role: respond to a scientist \[roleplayed by the teacher\] who says the class must give up their pet dinosaur because it poses a safety hazard; Out of role: use conventions such as discussion and/or [guided imagery](../glossary/guided-imagery.md) to establish the setting, context, and characters for a drama activity)
- communicate feelings and ideas to a familiar audience (e.g., classmates), using several simple visual or technological aids to support and enhance their drama work (e.g., act out a familiar story using props instead of words; dim lights to create a spooky [mood](../glossary/mood.md); use simple objects or props such as fur or feathers to indicate animal or bird characters in an Aboriginal story)

<sub>The source curriculum adds 4 teacher prompts, examples, and instructional notes for these expectations.</sub>

**B2 Reflecting, Responding, and Analysing.** apply the critical analysis process to communicate feelings, ideas, and understandings in response to a variety of [drama works](../glossary/dramatic-works.md) and experiences

- express thoughts, feelings, and ideas about drama experiences and [performances](../glossary/performance.md) in a variety of ways (e.g., use a journal response, a [think-pair-share](../glossary/think-pair-share.md) activity, visual art work, or a drama convention such as [role on the wall](../glossary/role-on-the-wall.md) to explore both the inner thoughts and feelings of the character and the perspectives of others who know the character)
- identify, using drama terminology, the elements and conventions of drama used in [shared drama experiences](../glossary/shared-drama-experience.md) and theatre and describe how they help communicate ideas and feelings and create interest (e.g., as a class create a checklist of the elements in a drama and what each element contributes and helps communicate; use a [tableau](../glossary/tableau.md) to share a moment of importance in the story)
- identify and give examples of their strengths, interests, and areas for improvement as drama participants and audience members (e.g., identify the goals they had in presenting a drama work and communicate how they achieved those goals; generate multiple ideas for improvement in a [mapping](../glossary/mapping.md) activity or experiential play experience)

<sub>The source curriculum adds 3 teacher prompts, examples, and instructional notes for these expectations.</sub>

**B3 Exploring Forms and Cultural Contexts.** demonstrate an understanding of a variety of drama and theatre forms and [styles](../glossary/style-26d4a23f.md) from the past and present, and their social and/or community contexts

- identify and describe a variety of drama and theatre forms they experience in their home, school, and community, and in the media (e.g., favourite television, film, computer programs; favourite play roles; playing with puppets to enact real-life scenarios; attending plays; listening to stories about family and community traditions)
- demonstrate an awareness of some drama and theatre traditions of communities around the world (e.g., describe experiences with festivals, pageants, circuses; explain the use of special objects in ceremonies or celebrations; give examples of the use of a narrator in plays or street theatre)

<sub>The source curriculum adds 2 teacher prompts, examples, and instructional notes for these expectations.</sub>


*Transferable skills developed in this strand: Innovation, Creativity & Entrepreneurship, Communication, Global Citizenship and Sustainability, Critical Thinking & Problem Solving, Self-Directed Learning.*

### C. Music

*By the end of Grade 2, students will:*

**C1 Creating and Performing.** apply the creative process to create and [perform](../glossary/perform.md) music for a variety of purposes, using the [elements](../glossary/elements-of-music.md) and techniques of music

- sing [unison](../glossary/unison.md) songs in tune and/or play simple [melodies](../glossary/melody.md) and [accompaniments](../glossary/accompaniment.md) for music from a wide variety of cultures, styles, and [historical periods](../glossary/historical-periods.md) (e.g., perform a simple three-note melodic [ostinato](../glossary/ostinato.md) to support a melody)
- apply the elements of music when singing, playing an instrument, and moving (e.g., [pitch](../glossary/pitch.md): move the body to show how individual pitches go up, go down, or stay the same, and how they connect to form a melody)
- create simple compositions for a specific purpose and a familiar audience (e.g., create accompaniments for songs, stories, or poems; create a simple song using the notes “mi”, “so”, and “la”, or the notes of a [pentatonic scale](../glossary/pentatonic-scale.md))
- use the tools and techniques of [musicianship](../glossary/musicianship.md) in musical performances (e.g., use controlled breathing and relaxed but straight posture when singing; show awareness of proper playing technique when playing instruments; match pitches within an accessible vocal range; clap back [rhythms](../glossary/rhythm.md) accurately while keeping a steady [beat](../glossary/beat.md))
- use [symbols](../glossary/symbols.md) to represent sounds and sounds to represent musical symbols (e.g., match short melody maps with the corresponding phrases in a song; use [rhythm syllables](../glossary/rhythm-syllables.md) such as “ta ti-ti” to represent [note](../glossary/note.md) values orally)

<sub>The source curriculum adds 5 teacher prompts, examples, and instructional notes for these expectations.</sub>

**C2 Reflecting, Responding, and Analysing.** apply the critical analysis process to communicate their feelings, ideas, and understandings in response to a variety of music and musical experiences

- express personal responses to musical performances in a variety of ways (e.g., use a teacher-directed listening log to record their thoughts, feelings, ideas; write or draw their response)
- describe ways in which the elements of music are used for different purposes in the music they perform, listen to, and create (e.g., duration: an increase in [tempo](../glossary/tempo-9c4444a6.md) to indicate excitement; [dynamics](../glossary/dynamics.md): a decrease in volume to create a feeling of relaxation in the music; [timbre](../glossary/timbre.md): the sound quality of a particular instrument to create a particular mood)
- identify and give examples of their strengths and areas for growth as musical performers, creators, interpreters, and audience members (e.g., share with a partner what they did well during the last performance, using musical vocabulary)

<sub>The source curriculum adds 3 teacher prompts, examples, and instructional notes for these expectations.</sub>

**C3 Exploring Forms and Cultural Contexts.** demonstrate an understanding of a variety of musical genres and styles from the past and present, and their social and/or community contexts

- identify reasons why people make music in their daily lives (e.g., people sing songs that have special meaning in their family; children can use music to promote environmental awareness at school), and describe contexts in which they make music (e.g., family gatherings, seasonal celebrations)
- identify, through performing and/or listening, a variety of musical forms or pieces from different communities, times, and places (e.g., “O Canada”, an Iroquoian lullaby, Indian classical music, Obwisana from Ghana)

<sub>The source curriculum adds 2 teacher prompts, examples, and instructional notes for these expectations.</sub>


*Transferable skills developed in this strand: Innovation, Creativity & Entrepreneurship, Critical Thinking & Problem Solving, Communication, Self-Directed Learning, Global Citizenship and Sustainability.*

### D. Visual Arts

*By the end of Grade 2, students will:*

**D1 Creating and Presenting.** apply the creative process to produce a variety of two- and three-[dimensional](../glossary/dimension.md) art works, using elements, principles, and [techniques](../glossary/technique-397b78cc.md) of visual arts to communicate feelings, ideas, and understandings

- create two- and three-dimensional works of art that express feelings and ideas inspired by activities in their community or observations of nature (e.g., a streetscape [collage](../glossary/collage-36ceb0fc.md) with children playing, made with paint, [pastel](../glossary/pastel.md), and various kinds of paper \[newspaper, magazines\]; small glue-line prints in which a variety of curvy and pointy [lines](../glossary/line.md) show [illusory texture](../glossary/illusory-texture.md) or represent a [pattern](../glossary/pattern-a3bc501e.md) they have seen on insects in the schoolyard or garden)
- demonstrate an understanding of [composition](../glossary/composition.md), using [principles of design](../glossary/principles-of-design.md) to create [narrative art](../glossary/narrative-art.md) works or art works on a theme or topic (e.g., use [repetition](../glossary/repetition-3ba3d390.md) of colour throughout an image that communicates a story; create a [painting](../glossary/painting.md) or series of [stamp](../glossary/stamp.md) prints, showing depth, [perspective](../glossary/perspective.md), and [contrast](../glossary/contrast-5fce85ca.md) of pattern by overlapping fish and vegetation of different sizes and [shapes](../glossary/shape-c23ea962.md))
- use [elements of design](../glossary/elements-of-design.md) in art works to communicate ideas, messages, and understandings (e.g., use [tints](../glossary/tint.md) of a colour to create light areas for [emphasis](../glossary/emphasis.md) in a collaborative mural of favourite places in the neighbourhood; use a simple action [pose](../glossary/pose.md) to modify [form](../glossary/form-f566a84f.md) in a [sculpture](../glossary/sculpture.md) of a pet or other animal made with modelling [clay](../glossary/hand-building-clay.md))
- use a variety of [materials](../glossary/materials.md), tools, and techniques to respond to [design](../glossary/design.md) challenges (e.g.,  

 [drawing](../glossary/drawing.md): make marker or coloured-pencil drawings of trees that are close and far away, using contrasts in size and placement on the paper to show depth of [space](../glossary/space-fd5bc538.md), and basing the drawings on observations of real trees and trees in a variety of art works \[e.g., works by Emily Carr or Tom Thomson\]
 [mixed media](../glossary/mixed-media-work.md): use acrylic paint over textured materials \[e.g., burlap, cardboard\] to make expressive [organic shapes](../glossary/organic-shapes-or-forms.md), using a combination of traditional techniques \[[blending](../glossary/blending.md), glazing, sgraffito, [scumbling](../glossary/scumbling-drawing.md), impasto\] and experimental techniques \[use of sponges, fingers, sticks, twigs, feathers, masking tape\]
 painting: make a tempera painting depicting friends playing playground games, using a [limited palette](../glossary/limited-palette.md) of colours
 [printmaking](../glossary/printmaking.md): make a [print](../glossary/print.md) of a [motif](../glossary/motif-879bcc68.md) for a storybook about dinosaurs, using polystyrene [plate](../glossary/printing-plate.md) stamps or modelling-clay [imprints](../glossary/imprint.md) of dinosaurs and plants
 sculpture: make insect shapes and habitat features, using wood, twigs, raffia, corn husks, and other natural materials, to explore science concepts)

<sub>The source curriculum adds 4 teacher prompts, examples, and instructional notes for these expectations.</sub>

**D2 Reflecting, Responding, and Analysing.** apply the critical analysis process to communicate feelings, ideas, and understandings in response to a variety of art works and art experiences

- express their feelings and ideas about works of art (e.g., explain why they prefer a work by one artist over another; explain to a partner how well an art work reflects their personal knowledge and prior experience)
- explain how elements and principles of design are used to communicate meaning or understanding in their own and others’ art work (e.g., use of different colours for achieving different effects, such as warm, sunny colours for a beach or cool colours for a wet forest; depiction of various [textures](../glossary/texture-3e8c5463.md), such as rough tree bark, smooth plastics, and ridged corduroy; [elaboration](../glossary/elaboration.md) and [variation](../glossary/variation.md) to create variety in otherwise symmetrical buildings)
- demonstrate an awareness of signs and symbols encountered in their daily lives and in works of art (e.g., symbols and shapes related to school, travel, and the arts; sports or institutional logos; symbols from art works or heritage crafts of family or community significance)
- identify and document their strengths, their interests, and areas for improvement as creators of art (e.g., identify what is interesting about a work they have produced; identify what they feel they have done well and what they would do differently next time to improve)

<sub>The source curriculum adds 4 teacher prompts, examples, and instructional notes for these expectations.</sub>

**D3 Exploring Forms and Cultural Contexts.** demonstrate an understanding of a variety of art forms, [styles](../glossary/style-78c0be0f.md), and techniques from the past and present, and their social and/or community contexts

- identify and describe a variety of visual art forms they see in their home, at school, in their community, and in visual arts experiences (e.g., design of everyday items; picture books; artists-in-education; community art works, such as public sculpture, architecture, and murals; Aboriginal designs in dancing regalia; art works in student art exhibitions and community art festivals)
- demonstrate an awareness of a variety of works of art and artistic traditions from diverse communities, times, and places (e.g., depictions of nature, of people doing things together, or of people at work; miniature paintings from India; Aboriginal textiles, [ceramics](../glossary/ceramics.md), and petroglyphs; contemporary Inuit drawings of life in the North by Annie Pootoogook)

<sub>The source curriculum adds 2 teacher prompts, examples, and instructional notes for these expectations.</sub>


*Transferable skills developed in this strand: Communication, Critical Thinking & Problem Solving, Innovation, Creativity & Entrepreneurship, Self-Directed Learning, Global Citizenship and Sustainability.*
