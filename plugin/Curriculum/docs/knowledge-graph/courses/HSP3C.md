# Introduction to Anthropology, Psychology, and Sociology (HSP3C)

**Introduction to Anthropology, Psychology, and Sociology (HSP3C)** is a Grade 11 college preparation course in [Social Sciences and Humanities](../subjects/social-sciences-and-humanities.md).

This course introduces students to theories, questions, and issues related to anthropology, psychology, and sociology. Students learn about approaches and research methods used by social scientists. Students will be given opportunities to apply theories from a variety of perspectives, to conduct social science research, and to become familiar with current issues within the three disciplines.

## Before you take this course

This course has no formal prerequisites.

## What students learn

The learning is organized into 4 strands: Research and Inquiry Skills, Anthropology, Psychology, and Sociology.

### A. Research and Inquiry Skills

*Throughout this course, students will:*

**A1 Exploring.** explore topics related to anthropology, [psychology](../glossary/psychology.md), and sociology, and formulate questions appropriate for each discipline to guide their research

- explore a variety of topics related to anthro­pology, [psychology](../glossary/psychology.md), and [sociology](../glossary/sociology.md) (e.g., addictions, gender socialization, [rites of passage](../glossary/rite-of-passage-cfce383e.md)) to identify topics for research and inquiry
- identify key concepts (e.g., through discussion, brainstorming, use of visual organizers) related to their selected topics
- formulate effective questions to guide their research and inquiry

<sub>The source curriculum adds 2 teacher prompts, examples, and instructional notes for these expectations.</sub>

**A2 Investigating.** create research plans, and locate and select information relevant to their chosen topics, using appropriate social science research and inquiry methods

- create appropriate research plans to investi­gate their selected topics (e.g., if conducting pri­mary research, outline purpose and method; identify sources of information), ensuring that their plans follow guidelines for ethical research
- locate and select information relevant to their investigations from a variety of primary sources (e.g., interviews, observations, surveys, questionnaires, original documents in print or other media – film, photographs) and/or secondary sources (e.g., textbooks, research reports, newspaper and magazine articles)
- based on preliminary research, for each investigation formulate a hypothesis, thesis statement, or research question, and use it to focus their research

<sub>The source curriculum adds 2 teacher prompts, examples, and instructional notes for these expectations.</sub>

**A3 Processing Information.** assess, record, analyse, and synthesize information gathered through research and inquiry

- assess various aspects of information gath­ered from primary and secondary sources (e.g., accuracy, relevance, reliability, inherent values and [bias](../glossary/bias-ade0ba52.md), voice)
- record and organize information and key ideas using a variety of formats (e.g., notes, graphic organizers, summaries, audio/digital records)
- analyse and interpret research information (e.g., compare information gathered from primary and secondary sources; determine whether similar information is found in different sources)
- demonstrate academic honesty by docu­menting the sources of all information generated through research
- synthesize findings and formulate conclusions (e.g., weigh and connect information to determine the answers to their research questions)

<sub>The source curriculum adds 1 teacher prompt, example, and instructional note for these expectations.</sub>

**A4 Communicating and Reflecting.** communicate the results of their research and inquiry clearly and effectively, and reflect on and evaluate their research, inquiry, and communication skills

- use an appropriate format (e.g., oral presenta­tion, brochure, flyer, poster, multimedia presentation, web page) to communicate the results of their research and inquiry effectively for a specific purpose and audience
- use terms relating to anthropology, psychol­ogy, and sociology correctly (e.g., [functionalism](../glossary/functionalism-structural-functionalism.md), [structuralism](../glossary/structuralism.md), feminism, [culture](../glossary/culture-9ec6e0d9.md), socialization, [ethnography](../glossary/ethnography.md), survey, archaeology, temperament, behaviour)
- clearly communicate the results of their inquiries (e.g., write clearly, organize ideas logically, use language conventions properly), and follow APA conventions for acknowledging sources (e.g., generate a reference list in APA style)
- demonstrate an understanding of the general research process by reflecting on and evaluating their own research, inquiry, and communication skills

<sub>The source curriculum adds 1 teacher prompt, example, and instructional note for these expectations.</sub>


*Transferable skills developed in this strand: Self-Directed Learning, Critical Thinking & Problem Solving, Communication, Digital Literacy.*

### B. Anthropology

*By the end of this course, students will:*

**B1 Theories, Perspectives, and Methodologies.** demonstrate an understanding of major theories, perspectives, and research methods in anthropology

- identify significant contributions of influential anthropologists (e.g., Noam Chomsky, Charles Darwin, Jane Goodall, the Leakeys, Margaret Mead, Edward Sapir, Marvin Harris, Richard Lee, Biruté Galdikas, Sherry Ortner)
- outline the key ideas of major anthropologi­cal schools of thought (e.g., functionalism, struc­turalism, [cultural materialism](../glossary/cultural-materialism.md), feminist anthropology), and explain how they can be used to under­ stand features of cultural systems
- describe significant issues in different areas of [anthropology](../glossary/anthropology.md) (e.g., linguistic, physical, cultural, and forensic anthropology; primatology)
- describe the main research methods used for conducting anthropological research (e.g., par­ticipant observation, oral history, semi-structured interview, ethnography, genealogy, archaeological excavation, [decolonizing research methods](../glossary/decolonizing-research-methods.md)), and explain critiques of the various methods

<sub>The source curriculum adds 3 teacher prompts, examples, and instructional notes for these expectations.</sub>

**B2 Explaining Human Behaviour and Culture.** use an anthropological perspective to explain how diverse factors influence and shape human behaviour and culture

- describe, from an anthropological perspective, how various factors (e.g., physical environment, globalization, pandemics) influence and shape human behaviour and culture (e.g., technology, language, social structures, law, politics, religion and ritual, art)
- identify the effects that diffusion, [assimilation](../glossary/assimilation.md), and [multiculturalism](../glossary/multiculturalism-e33f33b0.md) have on culture
- explain how studying cultural systems of different times and places helps anthropologists understand human behaviour and culture in the present

<sub>The source curriculum adds 3 teacher prompts, examples, and instructional notes for these expectations.</sub>

**B3 Socialization.** use a cultural anthropology perspective to explain patterns of human socialization

- explain how culture produces diverse forms of human behaviour (e.g., explain variations in rites of passage, language, family roles, the develop­ment of subcultures, or political practices from one culture to another)
- describe ways in which culture is an agent of socialization (e.g., in communicating and upholding such things as kinship rights and obligations, social customs, religious practices)

<sub>The source curriculum adds 2 teacher prompts, examples, and instructional notes for these expectations.</sub>


*Transferable skills developed in this strand: Global Citizenship and Sustainability, Critical Thinking & Problem Solving, Communication.*

### C. Psychology

*By the end of this course, students will:*

**C1 Theories, Perspectives, and Methodologies.** demonstrate an understanding of major theories, perspectives, and research methods in psychology

- identify significant contributions of influential psychologists (e.g., Erik Erikson, Sigmund Freud, Carl Jung, Abraham Maslow, Ivan Pavlov, Carl Rogers, B. F. Skinner, Thomas Bouchard, Mary Ainsworth, Carol Gilligan)
- outline the key ideas of major psychological theories (e.g., [psychodynamic theory](../glossary/psychodynamic-theory.md), [behaviourism](../glossary/behaviourism.md), [cognitive theory](../glossary/cognitive-theory.md), humanistic theory), and explain how they can be used to understand human behaviour
- compare significant psychological approaches to the study of human behaviour (e.g., clinical, experimental, [personality](../glossary/personality.md), abnormal, and develop­mental psychology)
- describe the main research methods used for conducting psychological research (e.g., experi­ments, surveys, observations), and explain critiques of the various methods

<sub>The source curriculum adds 3 teacher prompts, examples, and instructional notes for these expectations.</sub>

**C2 Human Mental Processes and Behaviour.** use a psychological perspective to describe how diverse factors influence and shape human mental processes and behaviour

- describe, from a psychological perspective, how various factors (e.g., heredity, environment, personality, identity, developmental stage, level of attachment) influence and shape an individual’s psychological development
- describe, from a psychological perspective, ways in which context and the influence of other individuals can affect people’s emotional and behavioural responses (e.g., at work, within a family, in sports groups/clubs, in crowds, in urban versus rural areas, among bystanders)
- describe how diverse psychological factors (e.g., motivation, perception, attitudes, mental health, temperament) influence individual behaviour

<sub>The source curriculum adds 3 teacher prompts, examples, and instructional notes for these expectations.</sub>

**C3 Socialization.** use a psychological perspective to explain patterns of socialization

- identify the role of socialization in the psychological development of the individual (e.g., effects of direct teaching of social skills, effects of social isolation)
- describe how diverse personality traits (e.g., introversion, openness to experience, perfectionism) shape human behaviour and interaction in a variety of environments
- describe the procedures of and ethical prob­lems associated with major psychological experi­ments in socialization (e.g., Zimbardo’s Stanford prison experiment, Asch’s conformity experiment, Milgram’s obedience-to-authority experiment, Elliot’s “Blue-Eyed/Brown-Eyed” experiment)

<sub>The source curriculum adds 3 teacher prompts, examples, and instructional notes for these expectations.</sub>

### D. Sociology

*By the end of this course, students will:*

**D1 Theories, Perspectives, and Methodologies.** demonstrate an understanding of major theories, perspectives, and research methods in sociology

- identify the significant contributions of influential sociologists (e.g., Emile Durkheim, Karl Marx, Edward Said, Max Weber, Dorothy Smith, Charles Wright-Mills, Gordon Allport, Reginald Bibby, George Dei, Ibn Khaldun)
- outline the key ideas of the major sociological schools of thought (e.g., [structural functionalism](../glossary/functionalism-structural-functionalism.md), [symbolic interactionism](../glossary/symbolic-interactionism-382b0708.md), conflict theory, feminist perspective), and explain how they can be used to understand social behaviour
- identify and describe the main research methods that are used for conducting sociological research (e.g., surveys, case studies, observations, secondary analysis of statistical results, content analysis, focus groups), and describe critiques of the various methods

<sub>The source curriculum adds 2 teacher prompts, examples, and instructional notes for these expectations.</sub>

**D2 Explaining Social Behaviour.** use a sociological perspective to explain how diverse factors influence and shape social behaviour

- describe, from a sociological perspective, how diverse factors (e.g., social [norms](../glossary/norm.md) and expectations, social structures, social sanctions, social distinctions, socio-economic status, geographic location, physical environment, media coverage) influence and shape individual and group behaviour (e.g., conformity, obedience, discrimination, “groupthink”, choice of occupation)
- explain the relationship between [prejudice](../glossary/prejudice.md) and individual and systemic [discrimination](../glossary/discrimination-95172453.md) (e.g., on the basis of gender, race, socio-economic status, sexual orientation, ability, religion, age, appearance), and describe their impacts on individuals and society
- describe, from a sociological perspective, ways in which diverse influences (e.g., culture, religion, economics, technology) shape social behaviour (e.g., dating and courtship, social networking, bullying, following trends and fads)

<sub>The source curriculum adds 3 teacher prompts, examples, and instructional notes for these expectations.</sub>

**D3 Socialization.** use a sociological perspective to explain patterns of socialization

- describe how structural changes take place in social [institutions](../glossary/institution.md) (e.g., family; religious insti­tutions; legal, political, educational, and health sys­tems; the military) in response to diverse influ­ences (e.g., demographic or economic change, the growth of environmentalism, the introduction of the Canadian Charter of Rights and Freedoms, social movements)
- describe ways in which social structures (e.g., economy, family, class, gender, race) affect individual and group behaviour
- compare the relative influence of primary [agents of socialization](../glossary/agents-of-socialization.md) (e.g., family, peers) and secondary agents of socialization (e.g., media, religion) on the [socialization](../glossary/socialization.md) of the individual

<sub>The source curriculum adds 3 teacher prompts, examples, and instructional notes for these expectations.</sub>


*Transferable skills developed in this strand: Critical Thinking & Problem Solving, Global Citizenship and Sustainability, Communication, Self-Directed Learning.*
