# The Arts (Grade 5)

**The Arts** is the Grade 5 The Arts program in [The Arts](../subjects/the-arts.md).

Here you will find an overview of student development and learning in the arts at the junior level, as well as the expectations and learning for the four areas of study for Grade 5.

## Before you take this course

This course has no formal prerequisites.

## What students learn

The learning is organized into 4 strands: Dance, Drama, Music, and Visual Arts.

### A. Dance

*By the end of Grade 5, students will:*

**A1 Creating and Presenting.** apply the creative process to the composition of movement sequences and short [dance pieces](../glossary/dance-piece.md), using the [elements of dance](../glossary/elements-of-dance.md) to communicate feelings and ideas

- translate into movement sequences a variety of images and ideas from other classroom subjects, including the arts (e.g., portray the character of a young, boisterous child from a drama by using a variety of [levels](../glossary/level.md), quick movements, and [indirect](../glossary/indirect-action.md) [pathways](../glossary/pathway.md) in dance; develop movement [phrases](../glossary/phrase.md) based on an image from a history textbook, a newspaper article, an Aboriginal story, or a painting in visual arts class)
- use dance as a language to explore, interpret, and communicate ideas derived from a variety of literature sources (e.g., newspaper articles about sports, entertainment, or current events; stories, poems, picture books)
- use movement in the [choreographic form](../glossary/choreographic-form.md) [call and response](../glossary/call-and-response.md) in a variety of ways when creating dance pieces (e.g., the teacher performs or calls a movement and the whole class responds; one student calls and the rest of the group responds; in partners, one student leads the movement and the other mirrors it)
- use the element of relationship in short dance pieces to communicate an idea (e.g., two dancers coming face to face to show either shared understanding or disagreement; a group of dancers holding hands to show unity)

<sub>The source curriculum adds 4 teacher prompts, examples, and instructional notes for these expectations.</sub>

**A2 Reflecting, Responding, and Analysing.** apply the critical analysis process to communicate their feelings, ideas, and understandings in response to a variety of dance pieces and experiences

- relate stories and characters in their own and others’ dance pieces to personal knowledge and experience (e.g., explain and demonstrate how dancers’ [postures](../glossary/posture.md) and mannerisms reflect things they have observed in everyday life; describe how the dance informed, moved, or changed their own perspective on an issue)
- identify the elements of dance used in their own and others’ dance pieces and explain how they help communicate a message (e.g., describe their use of a high level, direct path, and strong movements to portray authority)
- identify and give examples of their strengths and areas for growth as dance creators and audience members (e.g., identify two dance phrases that they believe were effective in their performance and explain their reasons for thinking so; assess whether they responded well to peer feedback about a performance and whether they implemented it)

<sub>The source curriculum adds 3 teacher prompts, examples, and instructional notes for these expectations.</sub>

**A3 Exploring Forms and Cultural Contexts.** demonstrate an understanding of a variety of [dance forms](../glossary/dance-form.md), traditions, and [styles](../glossary/style.md) from the past and present, and their sociocultural and historical contexts

- describe, with teacher guidance, dance forms and styles that reflect the beliefs and traditions of diverse communities, times, and places (e.g., choral dance was used to honour the god Dionysus, who was revered in ancient Greece; ballet developed to entertain the aristocracy in European courts; group and partner dances – such as the swing and the salsa – reflect various types of social interaction; dance has a symbolic celebratory role in African-American wedding rituals)
- identify and describe some of the ways in which dance influences popular culture (e.g., the influence of hip hop dance on people’s mannerisms and behaviour, or on fashion, magazines, and music videos)

<sub>The source curriculum adds 2 teacher prompts, examples, and instructional notes for these expectations.</sub>


*Transferable skills developed in this strand: Innovation, Creativity & Entrepreneurship, Critical Thinking & Problem Solving, Communication, Self-Directed Learning, Global Citizenship and Sustainability.*

### B. Drama

*By the end of Grade 5, students will:*

**B1 Creating and Presenting.** apply the creative process to [process drama](../glossary/process-drama.md) and the development of [drama works](../glossary/dramatic-works.md), using the [elements](../glossary/elements-of-drama.md) and [conventions of drama](../glossary/conventions-of-drama.md) to communicate feelings, ideas, and stories

- engage actively in [drama exploration](../glossary/dramatic-exploration.md) and [role play](../glossary/role-playing-role-play.md), with a focus on examining issues and themes in fiction and non-fiction sources from diverse communities, times, and places (e.g., interview story characters who represent opposing views on an issue; use role play to explore social issues related to topics such as the environment, immigration, bullying, treaties, the rights and responsibilities of the child)
- demonstrate an understanding of the element of role by selectively using some other elements of drama (e.g., time and place, relationship), to build belief in a role and establish its dramatic context (e.g., select and use supporting [artefacts](../glossary/artefacts.md) or simple props; arrange furniture to establish setting; work with others to select or create objects to build a convincing setting, such as a character’s room or the inside of a cave; use the drama convention of [thought tracking](../glossary/thought-tracking.md) to establish a relationship between two characters)
- plan and shape the direction of the [drama](../glossary/dramatic-play.md) or role play by collaborating with others to develop ideas, both in and [out of role](../glossary/out-of-role.md) (e.g., In role: improvise possible solutions to a dramatic conflict based on ideas from discussion and personal experience; Out of role: brainstorm in a group to generate ideas and make artistic choices)
- communicate thoughts, feelings, and ideas to a specific audience, using audio, visual, and/or technological aids to achieve specific dramatic effects (e.g., shine a spotlight on a performer who is making a key point; use a clash of cymbals to highlight a pivotal moment)

<sub>The source curriculum adds 4 teacher prompts, examples, and instructional notes for these expectations.</sub>

**B2 Reflecting, Responding, and Analysing.** apply the critical analysis process to communicate feelings, ideas, and understandings in response to a variety of drama works and experiences

- express personal responses and make connections to characters, themes, and issues presented in their own and others’ drama works (e.g., draw a picture or write poetry to show how they see a character at the beginning and end of the drama; use [journal writing](../glossary/journal-writing.md) to convey a feeling of connection to a character in a drama)
- explain, using drama terminology, how different elements are used to communicate and reinforce the intended message in their own and others’ drama works (e.g., explain how specific [scenes](../glossary/scene.md) and/or relationships create tension and build up to the climax of the drama)
- identify and give examples of their strengths, interests, and areas for improvement as drama creators, performers, and audience members (e.g., use journals, charts, rubrics, and peer- and self-assessment charts to keep track of successful contributions, unproductive ideas or efforts, and evolving preferences in drama; describe how they used established criteria to evaluate their own and others’ work; describe how they incorporated constructive feedback into their drama work; assess how well they differentiated between stereotypes and authentic characters when developing roles)

<sub>The source curriculum adds 3 teacher prompts, examples, and instructional notes for these expectations.</sub>

**B3 Exploring Forms and Cultural Contexts.** demonstrate an understanding of a variety of drama and theatre forms, traditions, and [styles](../glossary/style-26d4a23f.md) from the past and present, and their sociocultural and historical contexts

- describe forms of process drama, theatre, [storytelling](../glossary/storytelling.md), and visual representation from diverse communities around the world, and explain how they may reflect some beliefs and traditions of their communities (e.g., identify contexts in which the spoken word is a form of drama; describe historical and/or contemporary examples of forms from African, Asian, and/or Central or South American societies; identify examples of forms that reflect alternative viewpoints within communities)
- demonstrate an understanding of the broader world of drama and theatre by identifying and describing the roles and responsibilities of key theatre personnel (e.g., describe what a producer, director, actor, stage manager, set or costume designer, and/or lighting or sound technician does in a typical day and what each needs in order to complete his or her work)

<sub>The source curriculum adds 1 teacher prompt, example, and instructional note for these expectations.</sub>


*Transferable skills developed in this strand: Innovation, Creativity & Entrepreneurship, Communication, Global Citizenship and Sustainability, Critical Thinking & Problem Solving, Self-Directed Learning.*

### C. Music

*By the end of Grade 5, students will:*

**C1 Creating and Performing.** apply the creative process to create and [perform](../glossary/perform.md) music for a variety of purposes, using the [elements](../glossary/elements-of-music.md) and techniques of music

- sing and/or play, in tune, from musical [notation](../glossary/notation.md), [unison](../glossary/unison.md) and two-part music with [accompaniments](../glossary/accompaniment.md), from a wide variety of cultures, [styles](../glossary/style-43c6460b.md), and [historical periods](../glossary/historical-periods.md) (e.g., perform a [recorder](../glossary/recorder.md) duet that has a variety of [rhythmic](../glossary/rhythmic-pattern.md) and melodic patterns)
- apply the elements of music when singing and/or playing, composing, and arranging music to create a specific effect (e.g., [form](../glossary/form-19c48db8.md), [timbre](../glossary/timbre.md): create a [rondo](../glossary/rondo-f1cd2bb5.md) \[ABACADA form\] using a familiar song as the [repeating](../glossary/repeat.md) A [section](../glossary/section.md), and [compose](../glossary/compose.md) short rhythmic or melodic materials for the B, C, and D sections using pitched or [non-pitched percussion instruments](../glossary/non-pitched-percussion-instruments.md), [found sounds](../glossary/found-sounds.md), recorders, or [body percussion](../glossary/body-percussion.md))
- create musical compositions for specific purposes and audiences (e.g., compose an accompaniment for a story, poem, or drama presentation to address an environmental issue such as water conservation, recycling, or planting trees; create a piece that uses a rhythmic ostinato in $$\frac{4}{4}$$time and that includes both eighth and sixteenth notes; use body percussion, found sounds, voice, and non-pitched [percussion instruments](../glossary/percussion-instrument.md) to vary the timbres in their work)
- use the tools and techniques of [musicianship](../glossary/musicianship.md) in musical performances (e.g., play recorder using proper hand position and posture; sing and/or play pitches and rhythms accurately; observe markings for [dynamics](../glossary/dynamics.md) and [articulation](../glossary/articulation.md); interpret [accidentals](../glossary/accidental.md) and [key signatures](../glossary/key-signature.md) through playing and/or singing; sing and/or play songs in [major and minor keys](../glossary/major-and-minor-keys.md))
- demonstrate an understanding of [standard](../glossary/standard-notation.md) and other types of musical notation through performance and composition (e.g., notation of rhythms of skipping songs in $$\frac{6}{8}$$ [metre](../glossary/metre.md); dynamic markings, clefs, key signatures; notational software for scoring their own compositions; guitar [tablature](../glossary/tablature.md))

<sub>The source curriculum adds 5 teacher prompts, examples, and instructional notes for these expectations.</sub>

**C2 Reflecting, Responding, and Analysing.** apply the critical analysis process to communicate their feelings, ideas, and understandings in response to a variety of music and musical experiences

- express detailed personal responses to musical performances in a variety of ways (e.g., describe the sounds of a steel band, using musical terminology; analyse a [movement](../glossary/movement.md) from Vivaldi’s Four Seasons in a think-pair-share listening activity, and describe their feelings and personal impressions; compare the mood of a piece from today and a piece from the baroque period, using Venn diagrams)
- identify the elements of music in the music they perform, listen to, and create, and describe how they are used (e.g., timbre: describe how [brass instruments](../glossary/brass-instrument.md) are used in a marching band; [duration](../glossary/duration-9ff8b381.md): clap [dotted rhythm](../glossary/dotted-rhythm.md) patterns in a fanfare, describe how a slow [tempo](../glossary/tempo-9c4444a6.md) contributes to the mood of a funeral march, describe the use of [syncopation](../glossary/syncopation.md) in rhythms in Latin American music; form and [texture](../glossary/texture.md): graphically portray the layering of melodies in a [round](../glossary/round.md); dynamics: relate the soft or loud sounds in a ballad to the meaning of the text)
- identify and give examples of their strengths and areas for growth as musical performers, creators, interpreters, and audience members (e.g., balancing the volume of their own singing part in relation to the volume of another singing part; using [expressive controls](../glossary/expressive-controls.md) while playing recorder; providing peer feedback in preparation for a musical performance; writing a reflection on a live or recorded musical performance)

<sub>The source curriculum adds 3 teacher prompts, examples, and instructional notes for these expectations.</sub>

**C3 Exploring Forms and Cultural Contexts.** demonstrate an understanding of a variety of musical genres and styles from the past and present, and their sociocultural and historical contexts

- identify and describe some of the key influences of music within contemporary culture (e.g., describe the use of music in film and advertising; identify effects of musical trends on young people’s musical tastes; describe examples of fusion in different musical styles and genres)
- demonstrate an awareness of the use of music and musical instruments in various traditions, from early times to today (e.g., describe the use of the drum in various cultures, including Aboriginal cultures, and at various times around the world in ceremonial and celebratory music)

<sub>The source curriculum adds 2 teacher prompts, examples, and instructional notes for these expectations.</sub>

### D. Visual Arts

*By the end of Grade 5, students will:*

**D1 Creating and Presenting.** apply the creative process to produce a [variety](../glossary/variety.md) of two- and three-[dimensional](../glossary/dimension.md) art works, using elements, principles, and [techniques](../glossary/technique-397b78cc.md) of visual arts to communicate feelings, ideas, and understandings

- create two- and three-dimensional art works that express feelings and ideas inspired by their own and others’ points of view (e.g., a [painting](../glossary/painting.md) based on a [photo montage](../glossary/photo-montage.md) about children’s rights and responsibilities; a coloured [line](../glossary/line.md) [drawing](../glossary/drawing.md) of an underwater setting or the view from an airplane that addresses environmental awareness by showing the interconnectedness of ecosystems; a painting of someone in a particular situation in which empathy for him or her is created through [characterization](../glossary/characterization.md))
- demonstrate an understanding of [composition](../glossary/composition.md), using selected [principles of design](../glossary/principles-of-design.md) to create [narrative art](../glossary/narrative-art.md) works or art works on a theme or topic (e.g., create an abstract painting using different [proportions](../glossary/proportion.md) of [complementary colours](../glossary/complementary-colours.md); create a simple [sculpture](../glossary/sculpture.md) of a human form that depicts an emotional response and shows awareness of proportion and [negative space](../glossary/negative-space-shapes.md) \[in the [style](../glossary/style-78c0be0f.md) of Barbara Hepworth\]; create an impression of depth and [space](../glossary/space-fd5bc538.md) by [neutralizing colour](../glossary/neutral-colours.md) [intensity](../glossary/intensity.md) and brightness in a landscape painting \[atmospheric [perspective](../glossary/perspective.md)\])
- use [elements of design](../glossary/elements-of-design.md) in art works to communicate ideas, messages, and understandings (e.g., a series of three [relief prints](../glossary/relief-printing.md) that use a glueline relief print process to illustrate the beginning, middle, and end of a story; a [poster](../glossary/poster.md) that presents solutions to stereotyping, bias, or bullying, using [angle of view](../glossary/angle-of-view.md); a graffiti-style mural that addresses a community issue, using convex shapes that lead the eye with [implied lines](../glossary/implied-line.md))
- use a variety of [materials](../glossary/materials.md), tools, and techniques to determine solutions to [design](../glossary/design.md) challenges (e.g.,  

 drawing: coloured pencils to create a [caricature](../glossary/caricature.md) of a celebrity that exaggerates facial features and uses linear shading and [cast shadows](../glossary/cast-shadow.md)
 [mixed media](../glossary/mixed-media-work.md): a [composite image](../glossary/composite-image.md) that uses photographs, photocopies, [transfers](../glossary/transfer-acrylic.md), images, and selected [opaque](../glossary/opaque.md) and transparent materials to reflect their self-identity
 painting: tempera paint or [watercolour](../glossary/watercolour-paint.md) pencils using unusual [colours](../glossary/colour.md) or perspectives to suggest a fantasy world
 [printmaking](../glossary/printmaking.md): a [relief print](../glossary/relief-printing.md) transferred from a textured surface, made with glue lines, craft foam, cardboard, paper, or string glued to board, using shapes to create a graphic design that explores [pattern](../glossary/pattern-a3bc501e.md) in a [non-objective](../glossary/non-objective-art.md) [op art](../glossary/op-art.md) style
 sculpture: a human figure or an imaginary creature made from [clay](../glossary/hand-building-clay.md), using basic handbuilding methods such as making the piece with coils or slabs of clay or by pinching and pulling the clay)

<sub>The source curriculum adds 4 teacher prompts, examples, and instructional notes for these expectations.</sub>

**D2 Reflecting, Responding, and Analysing.** apply the critical analysis process to communicate feelings, ideas, and understandings in response to a variety of art works and art experiences

- interpret a variety of art works and identify the feelings, issues, themes, and social concerns that they convey (e.g., use an [image round-table](../glossary/image-round-table.md) technique to compare interpretations of emotions suggested by abstract forms or figures in art work; sort and classify a variety of art images, such as Nigerian, Egyptian, Mayan, and Chinese sculptures, to determine common subjects or themes)
- explain how the elements and principles of design are used in their own and others’ art work to communicate meaning or understanding (e.g., packaging designs \[cereal boxes, drink packaging\] that use complementary colours create an impression different from that created by packages that use other colour schemes; Alexander Calder’s mobiles and Piet Mondrian’s paintings use colour, [line](../glossary/line.md), and geometric shape to create an impression of movement; colour, line, and pattern are used to convey a story in the illuminated manuscript of the Ramayana)
- demonstrate an understanding of how to read and interpret signs, symbols, and style in art works (e.g., Carl Ray’s paintings use symbols in the Woodland style of Aboriginal art to tell a story; Picasso’s [cubist](../glossary/cubist-technique.md) [portraits](../glossary/portrait.md) use stylistic features from African masks; a tiger is used in Asian art to signify bravery)
- identify and explain their strengths, their interests, and areas for improvement as creators, interpreters, and viewers of art (e.g., use of appropriate terminology in talking about their own art work; discussion of others’ ideas with sensitivity and respect; provision of reasons for their artistic choices in a diary entry in their art journal or [sketchbook](../glossary/sketchbook.md))

<sub>The source curriculum adds 4 teacher prompts, examples, and instructional notes for these expectations.</sub>

**D3 Exploring Forms and Cultural Contexts.** demonstrate an understanding of a variety of art forms, styles, and techniques from the past and present, and their sociocultural and historical contexts

- describe how forms and styles of visual and media arts represent various messages and contexts in the past and present (e.g., sculptural monuments to honour people in the past such as war veterans; promotion of ideas or products on film, television, and the Internet in everyday life)
- demonstrate an awareness of ways in which visual arts reflect the beliefs and traditions of a variety of peoples and of people in different times and places (e.g., the use of [contemporary](../glossary/contemporary-art.md) Aboriginal art to support cultural revitalization; the use of images on ancient Greek vases to reflect narratives of daily life, legends, and war; the relationship between public art and its location; exhibitions of the art of local artists in local festivals; displays and exhibitions of art works in galleries and museums)

<sub>The source curriculum adds 2 teacher prompts, examples, and instructional notes for these expectations.</sub>


*Transferable skills developed in this strand: Communication, Critical Thinking & Problem Solving, Innovation, Creativity & Entrepreneurship, Self-Directed Learning, Global Citizenship and Sustainability.*
