# Music (AMU1O)

**Music (AMU1O)** is a Grade 9 open course in [The Arts](../subjects/the-arts.md).

This course emphasizes the creation and performance of music at a level consistent with previous experience and is aimed at developing technique, sensitivity, and imagination. Students will develop musical literacy skills by using the creative and critical analysis processes in composition, performance, and a range of reflective and analytical activities. Students will develop an understanding of the conventions and elements of music and of safe practices related to music, and will develop a variety of skills transferable to other areas of their life.

## Before you take this course

This course has no formal prerequisites.

## Where this course leads

Completing this course opens the door to [Music (AMU3M)](AMU3M.md).

## What students learn

The learning is organized into 3 strands: Creating and Performing, Reflecting, Responding, and Analysing, and Foundations.

### A. Creating and Performing

*By the end of this course, students will:*

**A1 The Creative Process.** apply the stages of the creative process when performing notated and/or improvised music and composing and/or arranging music

- apply the creative process when performing notated and/or improvised music (e.g., explore variations in and make artistic decisions about [tempo](../glossary/tempo-5bb649fd.md), [dynamics](../glossary/dynamics-91081f20.md), and phrasing and related aspects of [articulation](../glossary/articulation-8498f008.md); experiment with a variety of possible responses in a [call-and-response](../glossary/call-and-response-51e449b4.md) activity; perform music for a partner, and reflect on his or her feedback in order to refine aspects of their performance)
- apply the creative process when composing and/or arranging music (e.g., use the applicable steps of the creative process when arranging an existing [melody](../glossary/melody-1b0ad9db.md) for performance on their instrument or when creating a simple composition for available percussion instruments; explore sounds from the human-created or natural environment as possible inspiration for a musical composition; refine their composition/arrangement on the basis of peer- and self-assessment)

<sub>The source curriculum adds 2 teacher prompts, examples, and instructional notes for these expectations.</sub>

**A2 The Elements of Music.** apply elements of music when performing notated and improvised music and composing and/or arranging music

- apply the elements of music and related concepts appropriately when interpreting and performing notated music (e.g., accurately play or sing notated articulations such as slurs, [accents](../glossary/accent-d68d1c99.md), staccato; play or sing [repertoire](../glossary/repertoire.md) with accurate [pitch](../glossary/pitch-955384ad.md) and [intonation](../glossary/intonation-dd06fd70.md); play or sing maintaining a consistent tempo; accurately interpret dynamic intensities as indicated in notated musical [phrases](../glossary/phrase-26fb7172.md); play or sing with [tone colour](../glossary/tone-colour-0908f225.md) appropriate to the repertoire)
- manipulate the elements of music and related concepts appropriately when improvising melodies and rhythms (e.g., when practising rhythmic call-and-response patterns and scales; when improvising melodies and rhythms using [diatonic](../glossary/diatonic.md) and [pentatonic](../glossary/pentatonic-scale-db568eea.md) patterns; when improvising simple two-[bar](../glossary/bar-cc538a7e.md) call-and-response melodies and rhythms; when improvising simple four-bar diatonic melodies over accompaniments generated by accompaniment software and representing a variety of accompaniment styles)
- apply the elements of music and related concepts appropriately when composing and/or arranging simple pieces of music (e.g., when writing a four-bar rhythm pattern that could be used as the basis for a composition; when writing a simple diatonic melody over a prescribed harmonic progression; when creating a simple composition using melody and rhythm in [binary form](../glossary/binary-form-ab-form-dc16d8f2.md))

<sub>The source curriculum adds 3 teacher prompts, examples, and instructional notes for these expectations.</sub>

**A3 Techniques and Technologies.** use a variety of techniques and technological tools when performing music and composing and/or arranging music

- demonstrate technical skill when performing notated and/or improvised music (e.g., perform a sixteen-bar melody using proper bow or finger technique; correctly perform articulations when [sight-reading](../glossary/sight-reading-c51b16cc.md) a piece of music; play a wind instrument or sing with suitable breath control)
- apply compositional techniques when composing and/or arranging  simple pieces of music (e.g., [compose](../glossary/compose-a51f8cb7.md) a simple melody with an effective melodic shape; [arrange](../glossary/arrange-d9676e6a.md) selected melodies to reflect specific musical forms)
- use current [technology](../glossary/technology-ab555f70.md) when practising, performing, composing, and/or arranging music (e.g., use available technology to record their performance or composition for self-assessment or to record a performance for evaluation; use [notation](../glossary/notation-e3bb5e7d.md)[software](../glossary/notation-software-02cf59d1.md) when composing simple melodies; perform with accompaniment software)

### B. Reflecting, Responding, and Analysing

*By the end of this course, students will:*

**B1 The Critical Analysis Process.** use the critical analysis process when responding to, analysing, reflecting on, and interpreting music

- listen to selections that represent a variety of musical styles and [genres](../glossary/genres-804fbc06.md), and identify and reflect on their personal responses to them (e.g., their initial reaction to an in-class performance by their peers; their favourite contemporary commercial music groups and the aspects of this music that they enjoy; their favourite parts of their band repertoire and what they like about them; the influence of specific pieces, styles, or genres on their arrangements or performance)
- identify and describe the use of elements and other components of music in a variety of selections, including their performance repertoire (e.g., describe melodic characteristics, rhythmic components, signs and [symbols](../glossary/symbols-61f18609.md), dynamics, and timbres in the print version of a choral selection before hearing the selection; describe how elements are used for expressive purposes in an arrangement being performed by the class, and how changes in these elements might alter the expressive qualities of the performance; describe technical aspects of a professional performance)
- describe the difference between technical aspects and expressive aspects of music, with particular [emphasis](../glossary/focus-or-emphasis.md) on shaping musical phrases and choosing appropriate tempo, and assess the contribution of both aspects to the successful [interpretation](../glossary/interpretation-d2bf1748.md) of musical selections (e.g., demonstrate both technical and expressive characteristics when interpreting music in performance; address both technical and expressive characteristics when analysing  musical performances)
- conduct research to gather reliable information relating to specific music, musicians, and the musical opinions of others, and describe the impact this information has had on their own opinions or assessments (e.g., listen to, read about, and discuss with their peers a selection of music by a contemporary artist or group; assess the reliability of the judgements expressed in their sources; describe how a particular source has influenced their opinions and/or why a different source has not influenced them)

<sub>The source curriculum adds 4 teacher prompts, examples, and instructional notes for these expectations.</sub>

**B2 Music and Society.** demonstrate an understanding of how traditional, commercial, and [art music](../glossary/art-music.md) reflect the society in which they were created and how they have affected communities or cultures

- identify and describe ways in which traditional music reflects the society in which it was created and how it has affected communities or cultures (e.g., the Celtic influence on East Coast fiddle music as a reflection of historical immigration patterns to Canada, and how this music has contributed to the local community; the [focus](../glossary/focus-or-emphasis.md) of the music in ceremonies and celebrations of some of Canada’s Aboriginal peoples; how instruments and [scale](../glossary/scale-0e051cac.md) patterns used in traditional Chinese music contributed to the creation and preservation of a distinct musical style in China)
- identify and describe ways in which commercial music reflects the society in which it was created and how it has affected communities or cultures (e.g., Big Band and other popular music during World War II as a source of support for the troops; the inspiration for the Seattle Grunge scene in the 1990s and its cultural impact; the links between reggae and Rastafarianism)
- identify and describe ways in which art music reflects the society in which it was created and how it has affected that culture or community (e.g., the impact of religious practices on the art music of different cultures, including various Asian cultures; the impact of availability of materials on the musical instruments developed by various cultures; the influence of Mozart’s patrons on the composer’s operatic work, and the impact Mozart had on changing the way opera was perceived)

<sub>The source curriculum adds 3 teacher prompts, examples, and instructional notes for these expectations.</sub>

**B3 Skills and Personal Growth.** demonstrate an understanding of how performing, creating, and critically analysing music has affected their skills and personal development

- identify and describe how the study of music has contributed to their personal growth (including the development of their values), their ability to express themselves, their awareness of the [aural](../glossary/aural-oral-66e68f28.md) world around them (both human-created and natural), and their awareness of others (e.g., how expressing themselves through performing or creating music has affected their self-awareness; how musical study has affected  their  appreciation of the aesthetic value of the sounds of nature and their awareness of people from other communities or cultures)
- describe the development of their musical skills and knowledge, and identify the main areas they will focus on for improvement (e.g., use journals and reflective practice logs as means of self-assessment; listen to and reflect on their own work; reflect on the input of peers and/or the teacher; compare their performance to an exemplary performance to help them identify areas for improvement)
- identify and describe some of the interpersonal skills and work habits that contribute to the successful completion of individual and collaborative musical tasks (e.g., the importance of punctuality, active participation, and active-listening, team-building, and leadership skills when participating in [ensembles](../glossary/ensemble-7bbf9429.md); the importance of preparation and perseverance when working independently)

<sub>The source curriculum adds 3 teacher prompts, examples, and instructional notes for these expectations.</sub>

**B4 Connections Beyond the Classroom.** identify and describe various opportunities for continued engagement in music

- identify and describe a variety of work or career possibilities related to music study (e.g., explore job websites; interview a freelance musician and report on his or her typical work week; identify the positions and track the responsibilities of all the people involved in the production and marketing of a commercial recording, including those involved in touring and publicity)
- identify and describe possible opportunities for continued musical study both in and out of school (e.g., the secondary courses necessary to go on to music-related study at a college or university; music or arts camps/workshops, including those offered through the Ontario Educational Leadership Camp, that are available to Ontario students; ways to extend their music learning outside of the classroom, including private lessons, music clubs)
- identify opportunities for and explain the benefits of participating in co-curricular music activities and other arts activities in the school (e.g., bands and/or choirs; opportunities to coordinate music for drama or dance presentations; opportunities related to arts activities outside of music, such as decoration, design, or technical opportunities associated with school events)

### C. Foundations

*By the end of this course, students will:*

**C1 Theory and Terminology.** demonstrate an understanding of music theory with respect to concepts of [notation](../glossary/notation-e3bb5e7d.md) and the elements and other components of music, and use appropriate terminology relating to them

- demonstrate an understanding of the elements of music, particularly through practical application and aural recognition, and use appropriate terminology related to these elements (e.g., describe scales and scale patterns, and perform them with appropriate dynamic contrasts and articulation and varied rhythms; describe the main characteristics of a variety of styles of music with reference to their use of elements, and use accompaniment software programs to demonstrate them; identify the elements of music in a range of aural samples; use correct terminology to describe the elements of music in a selection they are preparing for performance; identify and describe various textures in music \[[monophonic](../glossary/monophony-monophonic-music.md), [homophonic](../glossary/homophony-homophonic-music-ec792c5f.md), [polyphonic](../glossary/polyphony-polyphonic-music.md)\])
- demonstrate an understanding of, and use proper terminology when referring to, fundamental concepts associated with notation (e.g., identify [notes](../glossary/note.md) of the [grand staff](../glossary/grand-staff-d9cababd.md); identify and describe the meaning of signs and symbols; notate scale patterns; identify and demonstrate their understanding of varied tempo and expressive markings in the music in their repertoire; describe the [cycle of fifths](../glossary/cycle-of-fifths.md); transpose a vocal line from another range into their voice range)
- reproduce or identify accurately, from notation and/or listening, simple melodic, rhythmic, and harmonic examples (e.g., accurately play or sing notated rhythm patterns at sight; identify simple sound layering and voicings in aural samples; identify given i[ntervals](../glossary/interval-a15f11c0.md) in a [major scale](../glossary/major-scale-d7117e01.md) from aural samples, and notate the intervals; identify major or minor tonality in examples and/or repertoire)

**C2 Characteristics and Development of Music.** demonstrate an understanding of the history of some musical forms and of characteristics of types of music from around the world

- demonstrate an understanding of the origins and development of some musical forms (e.g., identify composers within a particular genre and situate them on a timeline; describe the development of music associated with rituals and ceremonies in various cultures)
- identify and describe shared and unique characteristics of types of music from around the world, including Aboriginal music (e.g., the use of instruments in Aboriginal song; the ways [digital](../glossary/digital.md) sound is used in contemporary music; the use of [dissonance](../glossary/dissonance.md) in choral music from various cultures)

<sub>The source curriculum adds 2 teacher prompts, examples, and instructional notes for these expectations.</sub>

**C3 Conventions and Responsible Practices.** demonstrate an understanding of responsible practices and performance conventions relating to music

- identify and describe key physical and health considerations associated with practising, performing, and listening to music (e.g., the correct body posture for playing their musical instrument; the function of the major muscles and the skeleton in performance situations; the importance of keeping their instrument clean and well maintained; the risks from exposure to loud sounds and the precautions necessary to protect hearing when performing or listening to loud music)
- demonstrate an understanding of conventions related to music performance, with reference to both performers and audience members (e.g., demonstrate respect for others within the ensemble; respond to non-verbal communication from the conductor’s podium; explain why the audience might applaud in the middle of a piece performed by a jazz quartet but not by a classical orchestra)
- identify ethical and legal responsibilities associated with music (e.g., conduct a role play to illustrate responsibilities related to copyright law; brainstorm to develop a list of emerging forms of music distribution on the Internet and ethical and legal issues associated with them)
