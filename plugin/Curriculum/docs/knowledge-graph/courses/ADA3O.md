# Drama (ADA3O)

**Drama (ADA3O)** is a Grade 11 open course in [The Arts](../subjects/the-arts.md).

This course requires students to engage in dramatic processes and the presentation of dramatic works, and emphasizes the application of drama skills in other contexts and opportunities. Students will interpret and present works in a variety of dramatic forms, create and script original works, and critically analyse the processes involved in producing drama works. Students will develop a variety of skills related to collaboration and the presentation of drama works.

## Before you take this course

This course has no formal prerequisites.

## Where this course leads

Completing this course opens the door to [Drama (ADA4E)](ADA4E.md).

## What students learn

The learning is organized into 3 strands: Creating and Presenting, Reflecting, Responding, and Analysing, and Foundations.

### A. Creating and Presenting

*By the end of this course, students will:*

**A1 The Creative Process.** use the creative process and a variety of sources and forms, both individually and collaboratively, to design and develop drama works

- develop interpretations of contemporary and historical sources from diverse cultures to use as the basis for drama (e.g., use narratives, poetry, paintings, photographs, or Internet postings as the basis for a dramatic [monologue](../glossary/monologue.md) or [dialogue](../glossary/dialogue-6ea18026.md); use interviews with guest artists or stage performers as the basis for a scene about “[A day in the life of an actor”;](../glossary/a-day-in-the-life.md) try out different scenarios for dramatizing an event from the past)
- select and use appropriate dramatic forms to present themes or ideas about diverse cultures, contexts, and perspectives (e.g., use [choral speaking](../glossary/choral-speaking-chanting-f0773d90.md), spoken word, and/or mime to dramatize an [Aboriginal](../glossary/aboriginal-person-d00a2d62.md) or Caribbean folk tale; use tableaux with transitions to dramatize a traditional saying or proverb)
- use [role play](../glossary/role-playing-role-play-903d2c52.md) to explore the possibilities of different scenarios, situations, and characters (e.g., use [forum theatre](../glossary/forum-theatre-fec6262d.md)to develop scenes for an original presentation; use improvisation to create two different endings for a specific scene)

<sub>The source curriculum adds 3 teacher prompts, examples, and instructional notes for these expectations.</sub>

**A2 Elements and Conventions.** use the elements and[conventions of drama](../glossary/conventions-of-drama.md) effectively in creating individual and ensemble drama works, including works based on a variety of sources

- identify distinctive uses of the [elements of drama](../glossary/elements-of-drama-a43d2502.md) in texts and sources from a range of cultures and incorporate them in their own drama works (e.g., use trickster characters such as Nanabush, coyote, and [Anansi](../glossary/anansi-stories-tales.md) from Aboriginal and Caribbean folk tales; observe [unity of time](../glossary/unity-of-time.md) and place, as in the drama of ancient Greece)
- use a variety of drama conventions to clarify roles, relationships, and themes in individual and ensemble drama works (e.g., use [inner and outer circle](../glossary/inner-and-outer-circle-a0254806.md), [overheard conversations](../glossary/overheard-conversations.md), and forum theatre to highlight different characters’ views; use [caption making](../glossary/caption-making.md) or collective drawing to clarify focus)

<sub>The source curriculum adds 2 teacher prompts, examples, and instructional notes for these expectations.</sub>

**A3 Presentation Techniques and Technologies.** use a variety of presentation techniques and technological tools to enhance the impact of drama works and communicate for specific audiences and purposes

- use a variety of techniques and technologies to communicate ideas to different audiences and for different purposes (e.g., use available sound and light technologies to adapt scenes to appeal to different audiences – young people, seniors, special interest groups; use an opening sound cue to establish the mood and engage the audience in a children's theatre production)
- select and use appropriate role development techniques during rehearsal and performance (e.g., techniques such as warm-ups, voice and movement exercises, concentration exercises, improvisation, guided tour)
- select and use a variety of technological tools to enhance the expressiveness and impact of drama works (e.g., dim the lighting to signal the approach of danger; use sound effects to suggest a particular type of action or mood)

### B. Reflecting, Responding, and Analysing

*By the end of this course, students will:*

**B1 The Critical Analysis Process.** use the critical analysis process to reflect on and evaluate their own and others’ drama works and activities

- use the critical analysis process before and during drama projects to assign roles appropriately within the group and to monitor the group process (e.g., use [journal writing](../glossary/journal-writing-0694a20e.md), large-group discussions, one-to-one feedback, [think-pair-share](../glossary/think-pair-share-27f500a8.md), and student-developed criteria to record activities and provide feedback to all group members)
- analyse drama works to determine how well they achieve their intended purpose (e.g., express opinions in follow-up discussions about the communicative effectiveness of their own and others’ drama works and performances)
- identify [aesthetic](../glossary/aesthetic.md) and technical aspects of a variety of drama works, either completed or in progress, suggest reasons for their use, and assess their effectiveness (e.g., in a large-group discussion, debate different options for lighting; write a review of a performance that includes an evaluation of the positioning and movement of the actors)

<sub>The source curriculum adds 3 teacher prompts, examples, and instructional notes for these expectations.</sub>

**B2 Drama and Society.** demonstrate an understanding of how societies present and past use or have used drama, and of how creating and viewing drama can benefit individuals, groups, and communities

- identify different purposes for drama and the forms used to achieve these purposes in diverse communities and cultures from the past and present (e.g., to provide entertainment and escape – musical comedy; to comment on social absurdity or hypocrisy – comedy of manners; to draw attention to problems or promote attitudinal change – issue-based drama; to explore aspects of social status or class – commedia dell’arte)
- identify ways in which drama can promote self- and social awareness (e.g., creating and performing can promote self-awareness, self-confidence, and personal growth; role playing can promote social understanding and increase one’s capacity for empathy)
- identify ways in which drama works can promote social improvement and good citizenship (e.g., by exploring issues and raising questions about the way things are; by modelling positive solutions to problems)
- identify and interpret types of drama and specific drama works that portray distinct cultures and traditions (e.g., develop a hypothesis about the reasons for the ritual and symbolism of political or religious ceremonies or the use of masked actors in theatre traditions in different cultures)

<sub>The source curriculum adds 4 teacher prompts, examples, and instructional notes for these expectations.</sub>

**B3 Connections Beyond the Classroom.** identify knowledge and skills they have acquired through drama activities, and demonstrate an understanding of ways in which they can apply this learning in personal, social, and career contexts

- describe and assess the role of collaboration in their creative process (e.g., identify aspects of their group creative process that required collaborative as well as individual effort and suggest possible improvements; outline the steps they used to define and allocate roles, establish timelines, and monitor how individuals carried out their responsibilities)
- identify problem-solving techniques they have learned through drama activities, and explain how they can be applied in work and other social contexts (e.g., as a group, create a presentation to model for other students how they used respectful discussion and debate to identify and analyse problems, brainstorm possible solutions, choose an acceptable option, monitor progress, and modify plans if necessary)
- relate skills and knowledge they have developed through drama to opportunities for employment in the broader educational and arts sectors (e.g., create a scrapbook or portfolio that showcases their set design work; identify cooperative education placements that reflect their particular theatre interests; write a résumé to support an application for an audition; write a review of a community theatre production and post it on the Internet or submit it for publication to a community newspaper)

*Transferable skills developed in this strand: Critical Thinking & Problem Solving, Communication, Self-Directed Learning, Global Citizenship and Sustainability.*

### C. Foundations

*By the end of this course, students will:*

**C1 Concepts and Terminology.** demonstrate an understanding of the nature and functions of drama forms, elements, conventions, and techniques, including the correct terminology for the various components

- identify and describe the forms, elements, conventions, and techniques used in a variety of drama styles, and explain their function in their own and others’ drama works (e.g., explain the use of non-realistic, stylized movements in commedia dell’arte, exaggerated movements in melodrama, neutral and/or character masks in mime)
- use correct terminology for the forms, elements, conventions, and techniques they learn about through viewing and creating drama works (e.g., improvisational scene work, gesture, voice, in role, motivation, mime, [simulation](../glossary/simulation-52da8ae9.md))
- demonstrate an understanding of production and promotion roles, practices, and terminology in producing and presenting drama works (e.g., production roles: [stage manager](../glossary/stage-manager.md), make-up designer, wardrobe  manager; promotion and marketing tools and procedures: poster, program, brochure, direct-mail campaign, advertising campaign, press relations strategy)

<sub>The source curriculum adds 3 teacher prompts, examples, and instructional notes for these expectations.</sub>

**C2 Contexts and Influences.** demonstrate an understanding of the origins and development of drama and theatre arts and their influence on past and present societies

- identify and describe theatre traditions  from various cultures and historical periods (e.g., summarize the key characteristics and/or conventions of Thai shadow puppetry, Renaissance drama, commedia dell’arte)
- describe ways in which contemporary dramas show the influence of current media (e.g., media settings or personalities provide content and themes for drama; emerging media such as blogs or videos provide new vehicles for drama; media products such as reality television suggest new forms for drama)

<sub>The source curriculum adds 2 teacher prompts, examples, and instructional notes for these expectations.</sub>

**C3 Responsible Practices.** demonstrate an understanding of safe, ethical, and responsible personal and interpersonal practices in drama activities

- identify and follow safe and ethical practices in all drama activities (e.g., follow safety rules and procedures when performing backstage tasks and operating technical equipment; follow procedures for the environmentally responsible use of materials and energy; make character, scene, and script choices that reflect community standards; acknowledge source material when producing and/or presenting a partial or complete drama work)
- demonstrate an understanding of the tasks and responsibilities involved in producing drama works (e.g., show up on time; clarify responsibilities; cooperate to build trust; carry out duties consistently and fully)
- observe correct theatre and [audience etiquette](../glossary/audience-etiquette-2e173e54.md) in classroom drama work and formal performance contexts (e.g., as an audience member: avoid behaviour that could distract performers or other audience members; as a performer: take direction respectfully, respond appropriately to the director’s notes, cooperate with the stage manager)

*Transferable skills developed in this strand: Critical Thinking & Problem Solving, Self-Directed Learning, Communication, Global Citizenship and Sustainability, Innovation, Creativity & Entrepreneurship, Collaboration.*
