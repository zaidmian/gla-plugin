# Classical Languages (Ancient Greek/Latin) (LVGBD/LVLBD)

**Classical Languages (Ancient Greek/Latin) (LVGBD/LVLBD)** is an academic course in [Classical Studies and International Languages](../subjects/classical-studies-and-international-languages.md).

This course introduces students to the achievements of the classical world through the study of Latin or ancient Greek. Students will learn vocabulary and grammatical concepts essential for reading and translating adapted classical texts. English is the language of instruction, and students will develop their oral communication, reading, and writing skills in both English and the classical language. Through a variety of enrichment activities, students will explore aspects of life in the ancient world, including trade, commerce, education, arts, sports, ecology, daily life, and social practices, and will make connections across the curriculum between the classical world and the world around them.

## Before you take this course

This course has no formal prerequisites.

## Where this course leads

Completing this course opens the door to [Classical Languages (Ancient Greek/Latin) (LVGCU/LVLCU)](LVGCU-LVLCU.md).

## What students learn

The learning is organized into 4 strands: Oral Communication, Reading, Writing, and Intercultural Understanding.

### A. Oral Communication

*By the end of this course, students will:*

**A1 Listening to Understand.** determine meaning in a variety of [oral texts](../glossary/forms-of-oral-texts.md) in the classical language and oral texts in English about the classical world, using a range of [listening strategies](../glossary/listening-strategies.md)

- identify a range of listening comprehension strategies and use them before, during, and after listening to understand simple [adapted](../glossary/adapted-text.md) [oral texts](../glossary/forms-of-oral-texts.md) in the classical language and oral texts in English about the classical world (e.g., brainstorm about the topic to prepare for a listening activity; ask questions to clarify meaning during a classroom discussion; make jot notes while listening to the classical language and then develop a list of [derivatives](../glossary/derivative.md) to confirm their understanding; use personal experience and [prior knowledge](../glossary/prior-knowledge.md) to make connections to oral texts; use checklists to identify key ideas or implied messages while listening; restate classmates’ comments to monitor understanding)
- identify various elements of the classical language, including phonological and grammatical elements, and use the English terminology for those elements correctly (e.g., discriminate between classical and ecclesiastical Latin pronunciation; identify diphthongs while listening to a Latin text; identify [aspirated vowels](../glossary/aspirated-unaspirated.md) and consonants in spoken ancient Greek; identify and define parts of speech, [case](../glossary/case.md), number, and tense)
- demonstrate an understanding of the meaning of phrases, sentences, and simple [adapted](../glossary/adapted-text.md) [oral texts](../glossary/forms-of-oral-texts.md) in the classical language and of oral texts in English about the classical world (e.g., respond to greetings and simple classroom instructions in the classical language; identify English words derived from the classical language; [paraphrase](../glossary/paraphrase.md) material presented orally in English about aspects of ancient culture; answer questions posed in the classical language)
- (a) describe some strategies they found helpful before, during, and after listening; 
 (b) identify their areas of greater and lesser strength as listeners, and plan steps they can take to improve their [listening skills](../glossary/listening-and-speaking-skills.md) (e.g., describe what makes the [listening strategies](../glossary/listening-strategies.md) they use most often effective, and make a list of the most helpful ones for future reference; assess strategies suggested by the teacher and peers, and select some for use in future listening activities)

<sub>The source curriculum adds 4 teacher prompts, examples, and instructional notes for these expectations.</sub>

**A2 Speaking to Communicate.** communicate information and ideas orally in the classical language and in English, using a range of [speaking strategies](../glossary/speaking-strategies.md) and level-appropriate language suited to the purpose and audience (see the [Language Knowledge](../glossary/language-knowledge.md) chart for Latin or ancient Greek in Appendix A)

- identify a range of [speaking strategies](../glossary/speaking-strategies.md) and use them to express themselves clearly and coherently in the classical language and in English for various purposes and to a variety of audiences (e.g., repeat or [paraphrase](../glossary/paraphrase.md)ideas to assist peers’ understanding; plan and outline the main ideas and supporting details associated with the topic before giving an oral presentation; refer to a personal lexicon of words and phrases in the classical language that can be helpful in a variety of speaking contexts; identify subject matter to the audience at the outset of a presentation, and summarize essential points shared at the end; rehearse a presentation with a small group of peers to refine their delivery; self-correct errors while speaking)
- use simple vocabulary and grammatical constructions in a variety of oral language activities in the classical language (e.g., use simple greetings correctly; answer simple questions in the classical language; indicate the distinction between questions and statements by using correct [intonation](../glossary/intonation.md))
- use appropriate [diction](../glossary/diction.md) and correct grammar in a variety of oral language activities in English (e.g., deliver brief presentations on the cultural topics being studied; perform skits based on cultural material; during group discussions, respond appropriately to questions about [texts](../glossary/text-301894af.md) read in class; use correctly English words that are derived from the classical language)
- demonstrate knowledge of vocabulary and grammar by orally translating phrases, sentences, and simple [adapted texts](../glossary/adapted-text.md) from the classical language into English, with previous preparation and at sight
- (a) describe some strategies they found helpful before, during, and after speaking; 
 (b) identify their areas of greater and lesser strength as speakers, and plan steps they can take to improve their [speaking skill](../glossary/listening-and-speaking-skills.md)s (e.g., plan to use [non-verbal cues](../glossary/cues-non-verbal-and-visual.md) and [body language](../glossary/body-language.md) to support and clarify their spoken messages; assess strategies suggested by the teacher and peers, and select some for use in future speaking activities; monitor the progress of their speaking and determine steps for improving proficiency; identify the skills and strategies of effective speakers that they can apply to their own oral presentations)

### B. Reading

*By the end of this course, students will:*

**B1 Reading Comprehension.** determine meaning in a variety of texts about the classical world, in the classical language and in English, using a range of [reading strategies](../glossary/reading-strategies.md)

- identify a range of reading [comprehension strategies](../glossary/comprehension-strategies-bc1906c9.md) and use them before, during, and after reading to understand simple [adapted texts](../glossary/adapted-text.md) in the classical language and [texts](../glossary/text-301894af.md) in English about the classical world (e.g., skim text to identify unfamiliar words; use context to make inferences while reading; use visual cues to help interpret text; use a [graphic organizer](../glossary/graphic-organizer.md) such as a [T-chart](../glossary/t-chart.md) to compare the information presented in detailed stories with that in relief images; use their knowledge of the classical world to make connections between a text and its cultural context; use a graphic organizer such as a [Venn diagram](../glossary/venn-diagram.md) to identify similarities and differences between texts on the same topic by different authors)
- identify various elements of the classical language, including grammatical constructions and relationships (e.g., subject–verb agreement, noun–adjective agreement), modern conventions of the writing system (e.g., capitalization of proper nouns and adjectives, differences in punctuation between ancient Greek/Latin and English), and the numerical system (e.g., cardinal and ordinal Roman numerals), and demonstrate an understanding of their use
- demonstrate an understanding of a variety of simple [adapted texts](../glossary/adapted-text.md) in the classical language (e.g., dialogues, stories), using their [knowledge](../glossary/language-knowledge.md) of the classical language and information about the classical world (e.g., translate a short passage, drawing on their knowledge of gladiatorial combat; show understanding of the key ideas in a written dialogue about life in an ancient Greek or Roman household, applying their knowledge of vocabulary and social practices)
- use a range of vocabulary-acquisition strategies before, during, and after reading [texts](../glossary/text-301894af.md) in the classical language to determine the meaning of new words, and make connections to English words derived from the classical language (e.g., use contextual cues to deduce the meaning of new words; develop a list of words that they have encountered when reading [sight passages](../glossary/sight-passage.md) in the classical language; identify vocabulary relevant to the topic of the classical text being studied and keep a personal dictionary; make a list of English derivatives and use a [T-chart](../glossary/t-chart.md) to list their Latin or ancient Greek [roots](../glossary/root-b2a7106c.md))

<sub>The source curriculum adds 4 teacher prompts, examples, and instructional notes for these expectations.</sub>

**B2 Awareness of Cultural Context.** demonstrate an awareness of the cultural context of the classical language, using information from a variety of [media](../glossary/media.md) in both the classical language and English

- make accurate inferences about the classical world, using information from a variety of sources and [media](../glossary/media.md) (e.g., relief images; ancient coins; modern maps of the ancient Mediterranean; cultural histories in English; excerpts in English from classical authors such as Ovid, Homer, Aristophanes; ancient art forms such as statues, mosaics, [frescoes](../glossary/fresco.md), pottery)
- (a) describe some strategies they found helpful before, during, and after reading to understand [texts](../glossary/text-301894af.md) in both the classical language and English; 
 (b) identify their areas of greater and lesser strength as readers, and plan steps they can take to improve their reading skills (e.g., assess the effectiveness of the [reading strategies](../glossary/reading-strategies.md) they use most often, and plan to keep a journal of those they have found helpful for future reference; identify the types of information that dictionaries and grammar books provide to improve reading [comprehension](../glossary/comprehension.md), and describe their level of proficiency in applying this knowledge; assess strategies suggested by the teacher and peers, and select some to use in future reading)

### C. Writing

*By the end of this course, students will:*

**C1 Translating from the Classical Language into English and Composing in the Classical Language.** translate [texts](../glossary/text-301894af.md) from the classical language into English and write texts in the classical language, using appropriate vocabulary and [language structures](../glossary/language-structures.md) and correct grammar (see the [Language Knowledge](../glossary/language-knowledge.md) chart for Latin or ancient Greek in Appendix A)

- write accurate English translations, correctly applying knowledge of vocabulary and grammar, of simple [adapted texts](../glossary/adapted-text.md) in the classical language (e.g., complete a cloze passage; write a translation of a [sight passage](../glossary/sight-passage.md), referring to the running vocabulary list provided beneath it or to the glossary)
- compose short, simple phrases, clauses, and sentences in the classical language, using a few appropriate strategies and resources to ensure suitable word choice and correct word order and grammar (e.g., parse simple English phrases, clauses, and sentences to identify elements that govern choices of noun [case](../glossary/case.md) and/or verb tense in the classical language; identify phrases, clauses, and sentences in the classical language to use as models for their own compositions; consult [print and electronic resources](../glossary/print-and-electronic-re-sources.md), such as dictionaries and grammar books, to identify appropriate vocabulary and word endings; use the glossary provided in a textbook being used or word lists provided by the teacher)

<sub>The source curriculum adds 2 teacher prompts, examples, and instructional notes for these expectations.</sub>

**C2 Writing in English.** write a variety of English [texts](../glossary/text-301894af.md) related to the classical language, using appropriate vocabulary and [language structures](../glossary/language-structures.md) and correctly applying knowledge of spelling, grammar, and punctuation conventions

- write a variety of English [texts](../glossary/text-301894af.md) in response to passages in the classical language and to related cultural concepts (e.g., a letter from a citizen petitioning the emperor for assistance, journals, newspaper articles, research reports)
- write in English to demonstrate an understanding of simple [adapted texts](../glossary/adapted-text.md) in the classical language (e.g., write a [paraphrase](../glossary/paraphrase.md) or summary of a text being read in class; write brief answers to comprehension questions about a text being studied; complete a brief creative writing assignment about an aspect of the ancient world that they are studying or reading about in stories)
- correctly apply knowledge of vocabulary, [language structures](../glossary/language-structures.md), and conventions of spelling, grammar, and punctuation when writing in English

<sub>The source curriculum adds 2 teacher prompts, examples, and instructional notes for these expectations.</sub>

**C3 Using the Writing Process.** use the stages of the [writing process](../glossary/writing-process.md) – pre-writing, producing drafts, [revising](../glossary/revising.md), [editing](../glossary/editing.md), and publishing – to develop and organize content, clarify ideas and expression, correct errors, and present their work effectively

- generate, develop, focus, and organize ideas for writing in both English and the classical language, using some pre-writing strategies and resources, during [modelled](../glossary/modelled-activity.md), shared, [guided](../glossary/guided-activity.md), and independent writing activities (e.g., identify the topic, purpose, and audience for their writing; brainstorm to identify vocabulary related to their writing topic; create a [mind map](../glossary/mind-map.md) or web diagram to organize their ideas from [research](../glossary/research.md))
- plan, produce, and revise drafts using some strategies and resources and choosing forms and [stylistic elements](../glossary/stylistic-elements.md) suited to the purpose and audience (e.g., use sentence prompts when writing a description in the classical language; write a summary of an event, using information from two or more sources; use a model to structure a brief dialogue in English or the classical language; review and revise their drafts to improve the logical order of ideas, and add supporting details based on feedback from the teacher and peers)
- produce a polished final product, using some strategies and resources to improve correctness and [coherence](../glossary/coherence-69cf30fb.md) and to enhance the readability and visual appeal of their work (e.g., use a teacher-prepared checklist to verify that vocabulary is appropriate and spelling and punctuation are correct; use appropriate font size and type for titles, headings, and text, as well as appropriate line spacing and margins, to improve readability; incorporate diagrams and other graphics to highlight important information)
- (a) describe some strategies they found helpful before, during, and after writing; 
 (b) identify their areas of greater and lesser strength as writers, and plan steps they can take to improve their [writing skills](../glossary/writing-skills.md) in both the classical language and English (e.g., describe the strategy or strategies they found most helpful for organizing information and ideas; reflect on feedback from the teacher to identify the grammar conventions they find most challenging to apply correctly, and plan steps to improve their use of those language structures; identify strategies they plan to use to expand their vocabulary)

### D. Intercultural Understanding

*By the end of this course, students will:*

**D1 Understanding the Influence of Classical Languages.** demonstrate an understanding of the influence of classical languages on the languages of other cultures

- identify some words, phrases, and abbreviations that are derived from the classical language and found in English and other languages, and use them accurately (e.g., science/scientia; pecuniary/pecunia; Aboriginal/ab+origine; family/familia; a.m. as an abbreviation of ante meridiem; & as a ligature of et; etc. as an abbreviation of et cetera; eureka/ηὕρηκα)
- use knowledge of simple [language structures](../glossary/language-structures.md) that are common to the classical language, English, and other languages to enhance their speaking, reading, and [writing skills](../glossary/writing-skills.md) in both English and the classical language (e.g., subject-verb agreement, the use of [cases](../glossary/case.md) in sentences and in prepositional phrases)

**D2 Making Cultural Connections.** demonstrate an understanding of classical [culture](../glossary/culture-67ca9104.md) and its influence on other cultures

- demonstrate knowledge and understanding of some aspects of life in the classical world, using different strategies (e.g., describe the effect on Roman society of limiting higher education to the wealthy; explain the economic importance of slaves in Greek or Roman society; describe the roles of women, children, ancestors, and elders in Greek or Roman society; compare the diets of ordinary working people and wealthy people in the classical world using a [T-chart](../glossary/t-chart.md); write poems describing significant historical events, such as the eruption of Mt. Vesuvius; describe the importance of aqueducts to urban development in the Roman period; describe the importance of the latrines and sewer systems in ancient Rome; explain the connection between ecology and urban planning in Campania in the first century CE; describe the distribution of commercial and residential properties in Pompeii, Herculaneum, and Rome; assess the environmental impact of hunting in North Africa to supply animals for the gladiatorial games; describe the connection between the need for natural resources and the expansion of the Roman Empire; make a topographical map of the ancient Mediterranean; make drawings or replicas of classical [mosaics](../glossary/mosaic-836ea1a3.md), [frescoes](../glossary/fresco.md), or coins; use geographical modelling software to show how topography influenced the construction of roads and other infrastructure in the classical world)
- relate aspects of classical culture (e.g., religious practices, social customs, technology) to comparable aspects of other societies and cultures (e.g., prepare a chart listing social and monetary elements in the patron-client relationship and in private and imperial alimenta programs, and compare them to elements of social programs in effect today; create a [drama](../glossary/drama.md) about the roles of ancestors and elders in the ancient world and in First Nation communities; explain the origins of the modern calendar in the Roman calendar; create a Roman or Greek costume and note similarities to modern clothing in its reflection of status; re-enact a Roman school day and compare it to a day in a modern school; create a list of Roman technological innovations that were adopted by other societies; compare the impact that the water and waste-water system had on the lives of Romans with the impact that access to clean water has in modern societies)
- describe some ways in which their knowledge of the classical world relates to and/or enhances their understanding of other cultures, fields of study, and personal experiences (e.g., explain how knowledge of the classical language contributes to their understanding of terminology in fields such as medicine or law; explain how their understanding of the modern Olympic movement is enhanced by knowledge of the ideas and practices of the original Olympics)
