# World Religions and Belief Traditions in Daily Life (HRF3O)

**World Religions and Belief Traditions in Daily Life (HRF3O)** is a Grade 11 open course in [Social Sciences and Humanities](../subjects/social-sciences-and-humanities.md).

This course enables students to study world religions and belief traditions in local, Canadian, and global contexts. Students will explore aspects of the human quest for meaning and will examine world religions and belief traditions as exemplified in various sacred teachings and principles, rites, and passages. They will also study the interaction throughout history between society and various belief traditions, and will have opportunities to develop research and inquiry skills related to the study of world religions and belief traditions.

## Before you take this course

This course has no formal prerequisites.

## What students learn

The learning is organized into 6 strands: Research and Inquiry Skills, Commonalities, Concepts, and Contexts, Community Within Sacred and Secular Contexts, Actions, Sacred Stories and Writings, and Dimensions of the Sacred.

### A. Research and Inquiry Skills

*Throughout this course, students will:*

**A1 Exploring.** explore topics related to world religions and belief traditions, and formulate questions to guide their research

- explore a variety of topics related to world religions and belief traditions (e.g., rites of passage, religious observances) to identify topics for research and inquiry
- identify key concepts (e.g., through discussion, brainstorming, use of visual organizers) related to their selected topics
- formulate effective questions to guide their research and inquiry

<sub>The source curriculum adds 1 teacher prompt, example, and instructional note for these expectations.</sub>

**A2 Investigating.** create research plans, and locate and select information relevant to their chosen topics, using appropriate social science and humanities research and inquiry methods

- create appropriate research plans to investi­gate their selected topics (e.g., outline purpose and method; identify sources of information), ensuring that their plans follow guidelines for ethical research
- locate and select information relevant to their investigations from a variety of primary sources (e.g., interviews, observations, surveys, questionnaires, original documents in print or other media – film, photographs, songs, advertisements, [sacred](../glossary/sacred.md) texts) and/or secondary sources (e.g., book reviews, textbooks, websites, brochures, newspaper articles)
- based on preliminary research, for each investigation formulate a research question and identify potential subtopics to focus their research

<sub>The source curriculum adds 1 teacher prompt, example, and instructional note for these expectations.</sub>

**A3 Processing Information.** assess, record, analyse, and synthesize information gathered through research and inquiry

- assess various aspects of information gath­ered from primary and secondary sources (e.g., accuracy relevance, reliability, inherent values and [bias](../glossary/bias-ade0ba52.md), voice)
- record and organize information and key ideas using a variety of formats (e.g., notes, graphic organizers, summaries, audio/digital records)
- analyse and interpret research information (e.g., determine whether common themes arise in different sources)
- demonstrate academic honesty by docu­menting the sources of all information generated through research
- synthesize findings and formulate conclusions (e.g., weigh and connect information to determine the answer to their research questions)

<sub>The source curriculum adds 1 teacher prompt, example, and instructional note for these expectations.</sub>

**A4 Communicating and Reflecting.** communicate the results of their research and inquiry clearly and effectively, and reflect on and evaluate their research, inquiry, and communication skills

- use an appropriate format (e.g., oral presenta­tion, formal debate, poster, multimedia presentation, web page) to communicate the results of their research and inquiry effectively for a specific purpose and audience
- use terms relating to world religions and belief traditions correctly (e.g., belief, [creed](../glossary/creed.md), custom, deity, [divine](../glossary/divine.md), [faith](../glossary/faith.md), habit, [mysticism](../glossary/mysticism.md), [profane](../glossary/profane.md), sacred)
- clearly communicate the results of their inquiries (e.g., write clearly, organize ideas logically, use language conventions properly), and follow appropriate conventions for acknowledging sources (e.g., MLA, Chicago, or Turabian-style references and/or notes)
- demonstrate an understanding of the general research process by reflecting on and evaluating their own research, inquiry, and communication skills

*Transferable skills developed in this strand: Self-Directed Learning, Critical Thinking & Problem Solving, Digital Literacy, Communication.*

### B. Commonalities, Concepts, and Contexts

*By the end of this course, students will:*

**B1 Commonalities.** demonstrate an understanding of the commonalities among various world religions and belief traditions that coexist within a diverse society

- explain how common human needs (e.g., for a sense of community and identity, a moral code, a sense of purpose or meaning, [ritual](../glossary/ritual.md)) are reflected in religions and belief traditions across cultures and historical eras
- evaluate the importance of celebrations and commemorations in human experience (e.g., anniversaries, birthdays, historical markers)
- identify various world religions and belief traditions that coexist within a diverse society, and explain why it is important to learn about them
- explain how a focus on the differences between various religions rather than their commonalities can foster prejudice, [bias](../glossary/bias-ade0ba52.md), and [discrimination](../glossary/discrimination-95172453.md)

<sub>The source curriculum adds 4 teacher prompts, examples, and instructional notes for these expectations.</sub>

**B2 Terms and Concepts.** define and appropriately use terms and concepts related to world religions and belief traditions, examining their meaning in the context of various beliefs

- define and appropriately use terms and concepts related to the study of world religions and belief traditions (e.g., [agnosticism](../glossary/agnosticism.md), [atheism](../glossary/atheism.md), belief, [clergy](../glossary/clergy.md), conservative, [creed](../glossary/creed.md), [cult](../glossary/cult-d7fa7f9d.md), custom, [deity](../glossary/deity.md), [denomination](../glossary/denomination.md), [divine](../glossary/divine.md), ecumenism, [faith](../glossary/faith.md), [fundamentalism](../glossary/fundamentalism.md), habit, interfaith dialogue, [laity](../glossary/laity.md), liberal, [monotheism](../glossary/monotheism-7c518fa4.md), [mysticism](../glossary/mysticism.md), orthodoxy, [pantheism](../glossary/pantheism.md), [polytheism](../glossary/polytheism-b673120b.md), [profane,](../glossary/profane.md) reform, [ritual](../glossary/ritual.md), [sacred](../glossary/sacred.md), [secular](../glossary/secular.md), [supernatural](../glossary/supernatural.md), tradition)
- explain the perceived nature and role of the supernatural and the divine in various religions and belief traditions

<sub>The source curriculum adds 1 teacher prompt, example, and instructional note for these expectations.</sub>

**B3 Global and Local Contexts.** demonstrate an understanding of global and local contexts in which world religions and belief traditions function

- describe factors that contribute or have contributed to the global influence of various religions and belief traditions (e.g., emigration and immigration, forced migration through enslaved labour, proselytism, colonialism)
- analyse ways in which religions and belief traditions can influence the relationship between individuals and their society, with a focus on contemporary Canadian society
- explain the impact of including “creed” as a protected ground of discrimination under the Ontario Human Rights Code
- explain how individual religious beliefs can affect the ways in which people relate to others
- identify similarities between civil and religious observances and celebrations (e.g., the oath of citizenship, the national anthem, Remembrance Day)

<sub>The source curriculum adds 4 teacher prompts, examples, and instructional notes for these expectations.</sub>


*Transferable skills developed in this strand: Global Citizenship and Sustainability, Critical Thinking & Problem Solving, Communication.*

### C. Community Within Sacred and Secular Contexts

*By the end of this course, students will:*

**C1 Historical Contexts.** demonstrate an understanding of the historical contexts that have shaped particular religions and belief traditions

- identify and describe significant episodes from the history of various world religions and belief traditions
- identify major figures from the history of various world religions and belief traditions, and explain their origins, roles, and contributions
- explain the relationship between specific historical incidents and the practices or rituals associated with particular belief traditions (e.g., the exodus of Jews from Egypt and the eating of matzo at Passover)
- explain the relationship between religious movements (e.g., Reform Judaism) or events (e.g., the Second Vatican Council) and changes in religious practice over time

<sub>The source curriculum adds 3 teacher prompts, examples, and instructional notes for these expectations.</sub>

**C2 Community Beliefs and Practices.** demonstrate an understanding of various ways in which adherents of particular world religions and belief traditions relate to their own and other communities

- identify and explain differences in beliefs and belief-related practices between different branches within particular communities of believers, and describe unifying elements that connect them despite their differences
- describe various roles played by people in different belief traditions (e.g., senators in the Métis tradition; the laity in Orthodox Christianity; the cantor in Jewish Sabbath services; men and women, boys and girls in various traditions)
- demonstrate an understanding of how various world religions and belief traditions accommodate a range of commitment, from strict observance to liberal compromise
- describe difficulties that individuals may encounter in Canadian society when their reli­gious observance includes particular practices in regard to dress, diet, or behaviour (e.g., wear­ing hijab, refusing blood transfusions, following a vegetarian diet, offering tobacco)

<sub>The source curriculum adds 2 teacher prompts, examples, and instructional notes for these expectations.</sub>

**C3 Belief Traditions and Popular Culture.** demonstrate an understanding of ways in which various world religions and belief traditions are interpreted and adapted within popular culture

- describe ways in which popular culture uses traditional symbols, ideas, and other elements associated with various world religions and belief traditions
- explain the effects on faith communities as well as popular culture when specific faith symbols are appropriated by popular culture
- identify, explain, and evaluate examples of bias and prejudice in pop-culture representations of religions and belief traditions (e.g., depictions of Hinduism in The Simpsons; representations of Islam in the media)

### D. Actions

*By the end of this course, students will:*

**D1 Festivals, Celebrations, and Commemorations.** demonstrate an understanding of the role and significance of festivals, celebrations, and commemorations in various world religions and belief traditions

- explain the significance of major festivals and other commemorations in various world religions and belief traditions
- identify and describe the observances and practices associated with the major festivals, commemorations, and celebrations of various world religions and belief traditions (e.g., coloured water at Holi, dancing at powwows, lighting candles at the beginning of Shabbat, nativity scenes at Christmas)
- describe the role and significance of fasts and feasts in various belief traditions
- explain how practices and rituals employed in festivals and celebrations of various religions and belief traditions reflect the teachings of those traditions
- explain the origins of symbols associated with specific religious festivals, celebrations, and commemorations (e.g., Advent wreath, menorah, lotus, fireworks, sweetgrass)

<sub>The source curriculum adds 5 teacher prompts, examples, and instructional notes for these expectations.</sub>

**D2 Rites of Passage.** describe the role and significance of rites of passage in various world religions and belief traditions

- identify and explain the purpose of key rites of passage in various world religions and belief traditions (e.g., rituals associated with death, marriage, naming a child)
- describe the practices and rituals employed in specific rites of passage associated with vari­ous religions and belief traditions, and explain their significance
- demonstrate an understanding of symbols, art, and literature associated with rites of pas­sage in various religions and belief traditions

<sub>The source curriculum adds 3 teacher prompts, examples, and instructional notes for these expectations.</sub>

**D3 Daily Observances.** describe and explain the function and significance of the daily observances practised by followers of various world religions and belief traditions

- identify daily rituals of various world reli­gions and belief traditions (e.g., regular prayer, ritual bathing, meditation)
- describe the role and significance of daily observances in different world religions and belief traditions (e.g., Kusti prayers, Salah, Shema, sunrise ceremony, the Five Ks in Sikhism)
- explain how daily practices and rituals associated with various religions and belief traditions connect to and/or affect the lives of their adherents

<sub>The source curriculum adds 2 teacher prompts, examples, and instructional notes for these expectations.</sub>

### E. Sacred Stories and Writings

*By the end of this course, students will:*

**E1 Role of Sacred Writings, Oral Teachings, and Stories.** demonstrate an understanding of the role of sacred writings, oral teachings, and stories in various world religions and belief traditions

- compare and contrast sacred writings and oral teachings
- describe the importance of sacred writings and oral teachings in various world religions and belief traditions
- describe various sacred writings and oral teachings, providing specific examples from various world religions and belief traditions (e.g., the Bhagavad Gita, the Great Law of Peace of the Longhouse People, the Qur’an, the Jatakas, the Kojiki, the Mishnah, the New Testament)
- explain the intended message of specific passages from sacred writings or oral teachings from various religions and belief traditions (e.g., the Beatitudes, the Eightfold Path, the Seven Grandfather Teachings)

<sub>The source curriculum adds 2 teacher prompts, examples, and instructional notes for these expectations.</sub>

**E2 Influence of Sacred Writings, Oral Teachings, and Stories.** demonstrate an understanding of the influence of sacred writings, oral teachings, and stories on the development of belief and action.

- explain the influence of sacred writings and oral teachings in the origin and development of various world religions and/or belief traditions (e.g., the Code of Handsome Lake, the Qur’an, the New Testament, the Tripitaka)
- identify key precepts (e.g., the Golden Rule) derived from the sacred writings and oral teachings of various world religions and belief traditions and describe any commonalities
- explain the connections between the sacred stories and writings of various world religions and belief traditions and their festivals, celebra­tions, rites of passage, and daily observances (e.g., the Book of Esther and Purim, the Ramayana and Diwali, the Haudenosaunee creation story and the Green Corn Festival)
- explain the relationships between sacred stories and various symbols and works of art, music, or literature

### F. Dimensions of the Sacred

*By the end of this course, students will:*

**F1 Sacred Time.** demonstrate an understanding of how concepts of time influence the teachings and practices of various world religions and belief traditions

- describe ways in which common celebra­tions and commemorations associated with various religions and/or belief traditions are linked to concepts of time
- identify and explain the significance of historical events and natural cycles that are commemorated in various religions and belief traditions (e.g., planting and harvest time; the migrations of monarch butterflies, salmon, and birds; cycles of the moon and sun; the births and/or deaths of religious leaders)

<sub>The source curriculum adds 2 teacher prompts, examples, and instructional notes for these expectations.</sub>

**F2 Sacred Place.** demonstrate an understanding of how concepts of place influence the teachings and practices of various world religions and belief traditions

- describe the main features of the places of worship of various religions or belief traditions
- explain ways in which attitudes and actions of believers are shaped by convictions about the significance of place (e.g., places such as Turtle Island for the Haudenosaunee people, the Western Wall for Jews, the Ganges River for Hindus)
- identify the relationship between place and common celebrations and commemorations (e.g., cenotaphs on Remembrance Day)
- identify various sacred places that are destinations for pilgrims (e.g., Jerusalem, Mecca, Varanasi) and explain the difference between a [pilgrimage](../glossary/pilgrimage.md) (e.g., the hajj) and other types of [spiritual](../glossary/spiritual.md) journey (e.g., the vision quest)

<sub>The source curriculum adds 4 teacher prompts, examples, and instructional notes for these expectations.</sub>
