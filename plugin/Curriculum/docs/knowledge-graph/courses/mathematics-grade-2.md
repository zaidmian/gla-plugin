# Mathematics (Grade 2)

**Mathematics** is the Grade 2 Mathematics program in [Mathematics](../subjects/mathematics.md).

Here you will find the expectations and learning for the six areas of study for Grade 2 Mathematics.

## Before you take this course

This course has no formal prerequisites.

## What students learn

The learning is organized into 6 strands: Social-Emotional Learning (SEL) Skills in Mathematics and the Mathematical Processes, Number, Algebra, Data, Spatial Sense, and Financial Literacy.

### A. Social-Emotional Learning (SEL) Skills in Mathematics and the Mathematical Processes

*Throughout this grade, in order to promote a positive identity as a math learner, to foster well-being and the ability to learn, build resilience, and thrive, students will:*

**A1 Social-Emotional Learning (SEL) Skills and the Mathematical Processes.** apply, to the best of their ability, a variety of social-emotional learning skills to support their use of the mathematical processes and their learning in connection with the expectations in the other five strands of the mathematics curriculum


### B. Number

*By the end of Grade 2, students will:*

**B1 Number Sense.** demonstrate an understanding of numbers and make connections to the way numbers are used in everyday life

- read, [represent](../glossary/represent.md), [compose](../glossary/compose-61cac0c1.md), and [decompose](../glossary/decomposition-of-numbers.md) whole numbers up to and including 200, using a variety of [tools](../glossary/tools-9e67a3c2.md) and strategies, and describe various ways they are used in everyday life
- compare and order [whole numbers](../glossary/whole-number.md) up to and including 200, in various contexts
- [estimate](../glossary/estimate.md) the number of objects in collections of up to 200 and verify their estimates by counting
- [count](../glossary/count.md) to 200, including by 20s, 25s, and 50s, using a variety of tools and strategies
- describe what makes a number [even](../glossary/even-number.md) or [odd](../glossary/odd-number.md)
- use [drawings](../glossary/drawing-20030707.md) to represent, solve, and compare the results of [fair-share problems](../glossary/fair-share-problem.md) that involve sharing up to 10 items among 2, 3, 4, and 6 sharers, including problems that result in whole numbers, [mixed numbers](../glossary/mixed-number.md), and fractional amounts
- recognize that one third and two sixths of the same whole are equal, in fair-sharing contexts

<sub>The source curriculum adds 32 teacher prompts, examples, and instructional notes for these expectations.</sub>

**B2 Operations.** use knowledge of numbers and operations to solve mathematical problems encountered in everyday life

- use the properties of addition and subtraction, and the [relationships](../glossary/relationship-f5b5e0bf.md) between addition and multiplication and between subtraction and division, to solve problems and check calculations
- recall and demonstrate addition facts for numbers up to 20, and related subtraction facts
- use [mental math strategies](../glossary/mental-math-strategies.md), including [estimation](../glossary/estimation.md), to add and subtract whole numbers that add up to no more than 50, and explain the strategies used
- use objects, diagrams, and [equations](../glossary/equation.md) to represent, describe, and solve situations involving addition and subtraction of whole numbers that add up to no more than 100
- represent multiplication as repeated equal groups, including groups of one half and one fourth, and solve related problems, using various tools and drawings
- represent division of up to 12 items as the equal sharing of a quantity, and solve related problems, using various tools and drawings

<sub>The source curriculum adds 28 teacher prompts, examples, and instructional notes for these expectations.</sub>


*Transferable skills developed in this strand: Critical Thinking & Problem Solving, Communication.*

### C. Algebra

*By the end of Grade 2, students will:*

**C1 Patterns and Relationships.** identify, describe, extend, create, and make predictions about a variety of patterns, including those found in real-life contexts

- identify and describe a variety of patterns involving geometric designs, including [patterns](../glossary/pattern-6d386f87.md) found in real-life contexts
- create and [translate](../glossary/translate-6bbeec99.md) patterns using various [representations](../glossary/representation.md), including shapes and numbers
- determine [pattern rules](../glossary/pattern-rule.md) and use them to extend patterns, make and justify predictions, and identify missing [elements](../glossary/element.md) in patterns represented with shapes and numbers
- create and describe patterns to illustrate [relationships](../glossary/relationship-f5b5e0bf.md) among whole numbers up to 100

<sub>The source curriculum adds 21 teacher prompts, examples, and instructional notes for these expectations.</sub>

**C2 Equations and Inequalities.** demonstrate an understanding of variables, expressions, equalities, and inequalities, and apply this understanding in various contexts

- identify when symbols are being used as [variables](../glossary/variable-1b812434.md), and describe how they are being used
- determine what needs to be added to or subtracted from addition and subtraction [expressions](../glossary/expression.md) to make them [equivalent](../glossary/equivalence.md)
- identify and use equivalent relationships for whole numbers up to 100, in various contexts

<sub>The source curriculum adds 12 teacher prompts, examples, and instructional notes for these expectations.</sub>

**C3 Coding.** solve problems and create computational representations of mathematical situations using coding concepts and skills

- solve problems and create [computational representations](../glossary/computational-representations.md) of mathematical situations by writing and [executing code](../glossary/execute-coding.md), including [code](../glossary/code.md) that involves [sequential](../glossary/sequential-events-coding.md) and [concurrent events](../glossary/concurrent-events-coding.md)
- read and alter existing code, including code that involves sequential and concurrent events, and describe how changes to the code affect the [outcomes](../glossary/outcome-coding.md)

<sub>The source curriculum adds 8 teacher prompts, examples, and instructional notes for these expectations.</sub>

**C4 Mathematical Modelling.** apply the process of mathematical modelling to represent, analyse, make predictions, and provide insight into real-life situations 
 
 This overall expectation has no specific expectations. [Mathematical modelling](../glossary/mathematical-modelling.md) is an iterative and interconnected process that is applied to various contexts, allowing students to bring in learning from other strands. Students’ demonstration of the process of mathematical modelling, as they apply concepts and skills learned in other strands, is assessed and evaluated. 
 Read more about the mathematical modelling process.


*Transferable skills developed in this strand: Critical Thinking & Problem Solving, Communication, Digital Literacy.*

### D. Data

*By the end of Grade 2, students will:*

**D1 Data Literacy.** manage, analyse, and use data to make convincing arguments and informed decisions, in various contexts drawn from real life

- [sort](../glossary/sort.md) [sets of data](../glossary/set-of-data.md) about people or things according to two [attributes](../glossary/attribute.md), using tables and logic diagrams, including [Venn](../glossary/venn-diagram-ca7ba686.md) and [Carroll diagrams](../glossary/carroll-diagram.md)
- collect data through observations, experiments, and interviews to answer [questions of interest](../glossary/question-of-interest.md) that focus on two pieces of information, and organize the data in [two-way tally tables](../glossary/two-way-tally-table.md)
- display sets of data, using [one-to-one correspondence](../glossary/one-to-one-correspondence.md), in [concrete graphs](../glossary/concrete-graph.md), [pictographs](../glossary/pictograph.md), [line plots](../glossary/line-plot.md), and [bar graphs](../glossary/bar-graph.md) with proper sources, titles, and labels
- identify the [mode](../glossary/mode.md)(s), if any, for various data sets presented in concrete graphs, pictographs, line plots, bar graphs, and tables, and explain what this measure indicates about the data
- analyse different sets of data presented in various ways, including in logic diagrams, line plots, and bar graphs, by asking and answering questions about the data and drawing conclusions, then make convincing arguments and informed decisions

<sub>The source curriculum adds 21 teacher prompts, examples, and instructional notes for these expectations.</sub>

**D2 Probability.** describe the likelihood that events will happen, and use that information to make predictions

- use mathematical language, including the terms “impossible”, “possible”, and “certain”, to describe the [likelihood](../glossary/likelihood.md) of [complementary events](../glossary/complementary-events.md) happening, and use that likelihood to make predictions and informed decisions
- make and test predictions about the likelihood that the mode(s) of a data set from one [population](../glossary/population.md) will be the same for data collected from a different population

<sub>The source curriculum adds 8 teacher prompts, examples, and instructional notes for these expectations.</sub>


*Transferable skills developed in this strand: Critical Thinking & Problem Solving, Digital Literacy, Innovation, Creativity & Entrepreneurship, Communication.*

### E. Spatial Sense

*By the end of Grade 2, students will:*

**E1 Geometric and Spatial Reasoning.** describe and represent shape, location, and movement by applying [geometric properties](../glossary/geometric-property.md) and spatial relationships in order to navigate the world around them

- [sort](../glossary/sort.md) and identify [two-dimensional shapes](../glossary/two-dimensional-shape.md) by comparing number of sides, side lengths, [angles](../glossary/angle.md), and number of [lines of symmetry](../glossary/line-of-symmetry.md)
- [compose](../glossary/compose-61cac0c1.md) and [decompose](../glossary/decompose.md) two-dimensional shapes, and show that the area of a shape remains constant regardless of how its parts are rearranged
- identify congruent lengths and angles in two-dimensional shapes by mentally and physically matching them, and determine if the shapes are [congruent](../glossary/congruent.md)
- create and interpret simple maps of familiar places
- describe the [relative positions](../glossary/relative-position.md) of several objects and the movements needed to get from one object to another

<sub>The source curriculum adds 24 teacher prompts, examples, and instructional notes for these expectations.</sub>

**E2 Measurement.** compare, estimate, and determine measurements in various contexts

- choose and use [non-standard units](../glossary/non-standard-units.md) appropriately to measure lengths, and describe the [inverse relationship](../glossary/inverse-relationship.md) between the size of a [unit](../glossary/unit-95163b45.md) and the number of units needed
- explain the relationship between centimetres and metres as units of length, and use [benchmarks](../glossary/benchmark.md) for these units to estimate lengths
- measure and draw lengths in centimetres and metres, using a measuring tool, and recognize the impact of starting at points other than zero
- use units of time, including seconds, minutes, hours, and non-standard units, to describe the duration of various events

<sub>The source curriculum adds 19 teacher prompts, examples, and instructional notes for these expectations.</sub>


*Transferable skills developed in this strand: Critical Thinking & Problem Solving, Communication.*

### F. Financial Literacy

*By the end of Grade 2, students will:*

**F1 Money and Finances.** demonstrate an understanding of the value of Canadian currency

- identify different ways of representing the same amount of money up to Canadian 200¢ using various combinations of coins, and up to $200 using various combinations of $1 and $2 coins and $5, $10, $20, $50, and $100 bills

<sub>The source curriculum adds 5 teacher prompts, examples, and instructional notes for these expectations.</sub>


*Transferable skills developed in this strand: Communication, Global Citizenship and Sustainability.*
