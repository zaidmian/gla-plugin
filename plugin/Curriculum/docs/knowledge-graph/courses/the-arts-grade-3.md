# The Arts (Grade 3)

**The Arts** is the Grade 3 The Arts program in [The Arts](../subjects/the-arts.md).

Here you will find an overview of student development and learning in the arts at the primary level, as well as the expectations and learning for the four areas of study for Grade 3.

## Before you take this course

This course has no formal prerequisites.

## What students learn

The learning is organized into 4 strands: Dance, Drama, Music, and Visual Arts.

### A. Dance

*By the end of Grade 3, students will:*

**A1 Creating and Presenting.** apply the creative process to the composition of dance [phrases](../glossary/phrase.md), using the [elements of dance](../glossary/elements-of-dance.md) to communicate feelings and ideas

- imitate movements found in their natural environment in a variety of ways and incorporate them into a dance phrase (e.g., modify the movements of animals, snow falling to the ground, ice melting, plants growing; connect a series of insect-like movements together to make a phrase)
- use dance as a language to represent ideas from diverse literature sources, with a focus on time and [energy](../glossary/energy.md) (e.g., interpret stories, poems, and texts from other subject areas through dance; respond to a story about insects by depicting the [sustained](../glossary/sustained-movement.md) lifting and pulling actions of ants versus the sustained floating actions of butterflies)
- create dance phrases using a variety of [pattern forms](../glossary/pattern-form.md) (e.g., create dances with distinct, self-contained sections that share movement qualities using [AB form](../glossary/ab.md), [ABA form](../glossary/aba.md), or ABBA form; demonstrate a pattern physically by making “A” a soft and fluid section and “B” a fast and [percussive](../glossary/percussive-movement.md) section)
- demonstrate how dance elements can be used to create and expand the [movement vocabulary](../glossary/movement-vocabulary.md) within different sections of a larger [pattern](../glossary/pattern.md) (e.g., A: varying the use of [space](../glossary/space.md) while marching quickly; B: changing [levels](../glossary/level.md) while waving arms slowly; A: varying [locomotor](../glossary/locomotor-movement.md) and [non-locomotor](../glossary/non-locomotor-movement.md) percussive movements while marching quickly)

<sub>The source curriculum adds 4 teacher prompts, examples, and instructional notes for these expectations.</sub>

**A2 Reflecting, Responding, and Analysing.** apply the critical analysis process to communicate their feelings, ideas, and understandings in response to a variety of [dance pieces](../glossary/dance-piece.md) and experiences

- demonstrate an understanding of how the [elements of dance](../glossary/elements-of-dance.md) can be used in their own and others’ dance phrases to illustrate or explore learning in other subject areas (e.g., show and explain how the elements of [body](../glossary/body.md) and [relationship](../glossary/relationship.md) can be used to depict the science concept of magnetic attraction)
- identify, using dance vocabulary, the elements of dance used in their own and others’ dance phrases and explain their purpose (e.g., the use of body, space, time, and [energy](../glossary/energy.md) to create variety and interest; the use of levels, relationship, [pathways](../glossary/pathway.md), and shape to emphasize a mood; the use of [canon](../glossary/canon.md), direction, grouping [contrast](../glossary/contrast.md), and [repetition](../glossary/repetition.md) to explore pattern)
- identify and give examples of their strengths and areas for growth as dance creators and audience members (e.g., share with a partner what they did well; write in a journal about what they need to improve)

<sub>The source curriculum adds 3 teacher prompts, examples, and instructional notes for these expectations.</sub>

**A3 Exploring Forms and Cultural Contexts.** demonstrate an understanding of a variety of [dance forms](../glossary/dance-form.md) and [styles](../glossary/style.md) from the past and present, and their social and/or community contexts

- describe, with teacher guidance, a variety of dances from communities in Canada and around the world that they have seen in the media, at live performances and social gatherings, or in the classroom (e.g., dance numbers in animated movie musicals such as Happy Feet and Ice Age; First Nation dances at a powwow; folk dances of the early settlers; the farandole of France)
- identify and describe the role of dance in the community (e.g., performances as entertainment; community dances as a way of socializing; traditional dances as a way of maintaining cultural connectedness; dance classes for learning and communicating)

<sub>The source curriculum adds 2 teacher prompts, examples, and instructional notes for these expectations.</sub>

### B. Drama

*By the end of Grade 3, students will:*

**B1 Creating and Presenting.** apply the creative process to [dramatic play](../glossary/dramatic-play.md) and [process drama](../glossary/process-drama.md), using the [elements](../glossary/elements-of-drama.md) and [conventions of drama](../glossary/conventions-of-drama.md) to communicate feelings, ideas, and stories

- engage in dramatic play and [role play](../glossary/role-playing-role-play.md), with a focus on exploring themes, ideas, characters, and issues from imagination or in stories from diverse communities, times, and places (e.g., act out moments from “[a day in the life](../glossary/a-day-in-the-life.md)” of a main character from a story; improvise a short dialogue between two characters who are seeking a solution to a problem \[as in Aboriginal teacher/trickster stories\])
- demonstrate an understanding of how the element of time and place can support the development of role (e.g., present [tableaux](../glossary/tableau.md), with transitions and [thought tracking](../glossary/thought-tracking.md), that show differences between urban and rural settings and/or lifestyles to convey information about the characters)
- plan and shape the direction of a dramatic play or role play by building on their own and others’ ideas, both in and [out of role](../glossary/out-of-role.md) (e.g., In role: respond in role to extend the developing storyline in the drama \[as townsfolk, plead with the mayor to save their town\]; Out of role: in partners or small groups, combine their ideas to create a plan for how the characters will solve the problem in the drama)
- communicate feelings and ideas to a familiar audience (e.g., classmates) using audio, visual, and/or technological aids to support or enhance their drama work (e.g., use items found in the classroom to create a feeling or a [mood](../glossary/mood.md) suggested by the teacher; use sound effects or music to create an element of surprise or tension)

<sub>The source curriculum adds 4 teacher prompts, examples, and instructional notes for these expectations.</sub>

**B2 Reflecting, Responding, and Analysing.** apply the critical analysis process to communicate feelings, ideas, and understandings in response to a variety of [drama works](../glossary/dramatic-works.md) and experiences

- express thoughts, feelings, and ideas about a variety of drama experiences and [performances](../glossary/performance.md) (e.g., in a journal response, in a [think-pair-share](../glossary/think-pair-share.md) activity, in class discussion, by [writing in role](../glossary/writing-in-role.md), in a [four corners](../glossary/four-corners.md) activity, in a small group [improvisation](../glossary/improvisation-349b60de.md) or drawing)
- describe, using drama terminology, how elements and conventions of drama are used to shape their own and others’ work (e.g., describe how different characters’ actions help create suspense or tension; identify effective elements in a drama [presentation](../glossary/presentation.md); explain how setting highlights theme)
- identify and give examples of their strengths, interests, and areas for growth as drama participants and audience members (e.g., describe how their understanding of role play is developing; identify a role they would like to play, and explain why)

<sub>The source curriculum adds 3 teacher prompts, examples, and instructional notes for these expectations.</sub>

**B3 Exploring Forms and Cultural Contexts.** demonstrate an understanding of a variety of drama and theatre forms and [styles](../glossary/style-26d4a23f.md) from the past and present, and their social and/or community contexts.

- identify some distinct stylistic features of a few drama and theatre forms they experience in their home, school, and community, and in the media (e.g., puppet shows and mask plays use easily recognizable character types to tell a story; actors in live theatre productions use exaggerated [gestures](../glossary/gesture-8ae80d3b.md) and reactions designed to project beyond the footlights; street festivals use amplified live and/or recorded music, costumes, emcees, and amplified announcements to celebrate special events; clown acts use [mime](../glossary/mime.md) featuring clumsy gestures and comical accidents)
- demonstrate an awareness of ideas and emotions expressed in drama works from communities around the world (e.g., ideas about friendship or loyalty or power or perseverance in dramas based on fairy tales or myths from different countries; ethics and values found in Aboriginal plays)

<sub>The source curriculum adds 2 teacher prompts, examples, and instructional notes for these expectations.</sub>


*Transferable skills developed in this strand: Innovation, Creativity & Entrepreneurship, Global Citizenship and Sustainability, Communication, Critical Thinking & Problem Solving, Self-Directed Learning.*

### C. Music

*By the end of Grade 3, students will:*

**C1 Creating and Performing.** apply the creative process to create and [perform](../glossary/perform.md) music for a variety of purposes, using the [elements](../glossary/elements-of-music.md) and techniques of music

- sing, in tune, [unison](../glossary/unison.md) songs, [partner songs](../glossary/partner-song.md), and [rounds](../glossary/round.md), and/or play [accompaniments](../glossary/accompaniment.md) from a wide variety of cultures, [styles](../glossary/style-43c6460b.md), and [historical periods](../glossary/historical-periods.md) (e.g., sing or play an instrument accompanied by [body percussion](../glossary/body-percussion.md) or [found sounds](../glossary/found-sounds.md); sing or play a rhythmic or melodic [ostinato](../glossary/ostinato.md))
- apply the elements of music when singing, playing an instrument, and moving (e.g., [timbre](../glossary/timbre.md): sort sound sources by the way their sound is produced and make choices about which instruments will play in specific [sections](../glossary/section.md); form: change direction in a circle to show A and B sections of a song in [ABA form](../glossary/ternary-form-aba-form.md); [duration](../glossary/duration-9ff8b381.md): sing a song first very quickly then very slowly, and explain how the different tempi change their experience of the music)
- create compositions for a specific purpose and a familiar audience (e.g., create musical accompaniments for poems, stories, or dances they have created; create rhythmic ostinati based on significant words in a poem or words from a classroom topic or theme, then play them using instruments, body percussion, or [found sounds](../glossary/found-sounds.md); make changes to the rhythm and/or melody in a simple song that they know)
- use the tools and techniques of [musicianship](../glossary/musicianship.md) in musical performances (e.g., determine where breaths should be taken in a song; given the shape of a melody, suggest where a change in [dynamics](../glossary/dynamics.md) would be effective; use available [technology](../glossary/technology-96379b11.md) such as software, electronic instruments, or recording devices)
- demonstrate an understanding of standard and non-traditional musical [notation](../glossary/notation.md) (e.g., design melody maps based on the direction of the melody; demonstrate various ways of representing sounds using devised [symbols](../glossary/symbols.md); perform melodic patterns based on the notes “do”, “re”, “mi”, “so”, and “la” by using [solfège](../glossary/solfege.md) hand signs; create [soundscapes](../glossary/soundscape.md) illustrating dynamics and timbre)

<sub>The source curriculum adds 5 teacher prompts, examples, and instructional notes for these expectations.</sub>

**C2 Reflecting, Responding, and Analysing.** apply the critical analysis process to communicate their feelings, ideas, and understandings in response to a variety of music and musical experiences

- express personal responses to musical performances in a variety of ways (e.g., create a graphic or text response to a musical selection featuring a Latin American dance style)
- describe ways in which the elements of music are used in the music they perform, listen to, and create (e.g., use a Venn diagram to compare how the elements of two contrasting pieces create mood)
- identify and give examples of their strengths and areas for growth as musical performers, creators, interpreters, and audience members (e.g., singing in tune, breathing at the end of [phrases](../glossary/phrase-f64225dc.md), watching the conductor or teacher while rehearsing and performing)

<sub>The source curriculum adds 3 teacher prompts, examples, and instructional notes for these expectations.</sub>

**C3 Exploring Forms and Cultural Contexts.** demonstrate an understanding of a variety of musical genres and styles from the past and present, and their social and/or community contexts

- identify and describe ways in which music can be used in the community (e.g., to celebrate events, to bring people together, to dance to, to communicate, to entertain, to help people remember product names or telephone numbers in advertising, to help people remember concepts)
- identify, through performing and/or listening, a variety of musical forms or pieces from different communities, times, and places (e.g., songs, instrumental pieces, and dances in social activities or celebrations of early settlers and First Nation communities in Upper Canada)

<sub>The source curriculum adds 2 teacher prompts, examples, and instructional notes for these expectations.</sub>

### D. Visual Arts

*By the end of Grade 3, students will:*

**D1 Creating and Presenting.** apply the creative process to produce a variety of two- and three-[dimensional](../glossary/dimension.md) art works, using elements, principles, and [techniques](../glossary/technique-397b78cc.md) of visual arts to communicate feelings, ideas, and understandings

- create two- and three-dimensional works of art that express personal feelings and ideas inspired by the environment or that have the community as their subject (e.g., make a symmetrical [sculpture](../glossary/sculpture.md) of an insect or a flower, using natural [materials](../glossary/materials.md) such as wood, pebbles, dry seed pods, feathers; draw a picture depicting a solution to the problem of litter in their community; make a [painting](../glossary/painting.md) of nature, focusing on a feature of personal interest or meaning to themselves)
- demonstrate an understanding of [composition](../glossary/composition.md), using [principles of design](../glossary/principles-of-design.md) to create [narrative art](../glossary/narrative-art.md) works or art works on a theme or topic (e.g., use shapes of various sizes, in the [foreground](../glossary/foreground.md), [middle ground](../glossary/middle-ground.md), and [background](../glossary/background.md), to create an illusion of depth \[[perspective](../glossary/perspective.md)\] in a painting about a make-believe world; create a mural to express a response to a community celebration, using a variety of lines and shapes; using a scratchboard that has a layer of various [colours](../glossary/colour.md) covered by india [ink](../glossary/ink.md), make a high-[contrast](../glossary/contrast-5fce85ca.md) line [drawing](../glossary/drawing.md) about a story by scratching the black surface to reveal the colours beneath the surface)
- use [elements of design](../glossary/elements-of-design.md) in art works to communicate ideas, messages, and understandings (e.g., use asymmetrical cut-paper composite shapes to depict a Canadian [landscape](../glossary/landscape.md), with a clear foreground, [middle ground](../glossary/middle-ground.md), and background; use colour [values](../glossary/value.md) and shapes in a “What’s inside me?” painting in the X-ray [style](../glossary/style-78c0be0f.md) of Norval Morrisseau to create contrast between the inside and the outside of the figure)
- use a variety of materials, tools, and techniques to respond to [design](../glossary/design.md) challenges (e.g.,  

 drawing: use a variety of lines and shapes, drawn with [pencil](../glossary/drawing-pencil.md) and marker, to show movement in a [flipbook](../glossary/flipbook.md) about weather 
 [mixed media](../glossary/mixed-media-work.md): use wax crayons, oil [pastels](../glossary/pastel.md), [paint resist](../glossary/paint-resist.md), and materials of various [textures](../glossary/texture-3e8c5463.md) \[e.g. yarn, found objects\] to depict a tree or plant above ground, and use the technique of [elaboration](../glossary/elaboration.md) to depict what is hidden below ground
 painting: create a [watercolour](../glossary/watercolour-paint.md) or tempera painting of animals, using colour in a non-[representational](../glossary/representational-art.md) and expressive way
 [printmaking](../glossary/printmaking.md): paint [stencil](../glossary/stencil.md) prints in [warm](../glossary/warm-colours.md) and [cool colours](../glossary/cool-colours.md), creating a simplified pattern inspired by a favourite fruit
 sculpture: use modelling [clay](../glossary/hand-building-clay.md) to create organic [forms](../glossary/form-f566a84f.md) that are inspired by nature, such as shells, seed pods, and water-worn stones, and that show some kind of [metamorphosis](../glossary/metamorphosis.md) or [transformation](../glossary/transformation.md) into another form or figure)

<sub>The source curriculum adds 4 teacher prompts, examples, and instructional notes for these expectations.</sub>

**D2 Reflecting, Responding, and Analysing.** apply the critical analysis process to communicate feelings, ideas, and understandings in response to a variety of art works and art experiences

- express personal feelings and ideas about art experiences and images (e.g., create a [poster](../glossary/poster.md) for an exhibition, using words of different sizes and colours to show their excitement about the event; express thoughts and ideas about an art work while in role as the artist in a peer [artist interview](../glossary/artist-interview.md))
- explain how elements and principles of design are used to communicate meaning or understanding in their own and others’ art work (e.g., colour value in Emily Carr’s Indian Church; [organic shapes](../glossary/organic-shapes-or-forms.md) to make the monsters look less frightening and more like stuffed animals in Where the Wild Things Are by Maurice Sendak)
- demonstrate an awareness of the meaning of signs and symbols encountered in their daily lives and in works of art (e.g., fonts or logos that remind them of specific companies, messages, or moods; the meaning of animals such as the orca in Aboriginal clan symbols or the Inukshuk in Aboriginal art)
- identify and document their strengths, their interests, and areas for improvement as creators of art (e.g., keep an art journal to record what they think they have done well in their art works, or learned about in their art works, as they complete them; use the strategy of matching word and image to share their feelings about an art work or its creation)

<sub>The source curriculum adds 4 teacher prompts, examples, and instructional notes for these expectations.</sub>

**D3 Exploring Forms and Cultural Contexts.** demonstrate an understanding of a variety of art forms, styles, and techniques from the past and present, and their social and/or community contexts

- identify and describe a variety of visual art forms they see in their home, at school, in the community, and in visual arts experiences (e.g., [original](../glossary/original-art-work.md) paintings at a community gallery, sculptures in a local park, art [reproductions](../glossary/reproduction.md) in offices, murals or sculptural monuments in the community, mixed media art works at arts festivals)
- demonstrate an awareness of a variety of works of art and artistic traditions from diverse communities, times, and places (e.g., a picture book that tells a story about people and the time and place in which they work, play, and build their community; George Littlechild’s book This Land Is My Land; Daphne Odjig’s historical mural The Indian in Transition; Jacob Lawrence’s paintings of African-Americans working, playing, and interacting; classical Greek sculptures of sports figures, and contemporary sports sculptures, such as the fans in Michael Snow’s The Audience)

<sub>The source curriculum adds 2 teacher prompts, examples, and instructional notes for these expectations.</sub>
