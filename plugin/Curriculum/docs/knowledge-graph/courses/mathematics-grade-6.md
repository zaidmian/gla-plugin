# Mathematics (Grade 6)

**Mathematics** is the Grade 6 Mathematics program in [Mathematics](../subjects/mathematics.md).

Here you will find the expectations and learning for the six areas of study for Grade 6 Mathematics.

## Before you take this course

This course has no formal prerequisites.

## What students learn

The learning is organized into 6 strands: Social-Emotional Learning (SEL) Skills in Mathematics and the Mathematical Processes, Number, Algebra, Data, Spatial Sense, and Financial Literacy.

### A. Social-Emotional Learning (SEL) Skills in Mathematics and the Mathematical Processes

*Throughout this grade, in order to promote a positive identity as a math learner, to foster well-being and the ability to learn, build resilience, and thrive, students will:*

**A1 Social-Emotional Learning (SEL) Skills and the Mathematical Processes.** apply, to the best of their ability, a variety of social-emotional learning skills to support their use of the mathematical processes and their learning in connection with the expectations in the other five strands of the mathematics curriculum


### B. Number

*By the end of Grade 6, students will:*

**B1 Number Sense.** demonstrate an understanding of numbers and make connections to the way numbers are used in everyday life

- read and [represent](../glossary/represent.md) [whole numbers](../glossary/whole-number.md) up to and including one million, using appropriate [tools](../glossary/tools-9e67a3c2.md) and strategies, and describe various ways they are used in everyday life
- read and represent [integers](../glossary/integer.md), using a variety of tools and strategies, including horizontal and vertical number lines
- compare and order integers, [decimal numbers](../glossary/decimal-number.md), and [fractions](../glossary/fraction.md), separately and in combination, in various contexts
- read, represent, compare, and order decimal numbers up to thousandths, in various contexts
- [round](../glossary/round-e1d768f1.md) decimal numbers, both [terminating](../glossary/terminating-decimal.md) and [repeating](../glossary/repeating-decimal.md), to the nearest tenth, hundredth, or whole number, as applicable, in various contexts
- describe [relationships](../glossary/relationship-f5b5e0bf.md) and show equivalences among fractions and decimal numbers up to thousandths, using appropriate tools and drawings, in various contexts

<sub>The source curriculum adds 27 teacher prompts, examples, and instructional notes for these expectations.</sub>

**B2 Operations.** use knowledge of numbers and operations to solve mathematical problems encountered in everyday life

- use the properties of operations, and the relationships between operations, to solve problems involving whole numbers, decimal numbers, fractions, [ratios](../glossary/ratio.md), [rates](../glossary/rate.md), and whole number [percents](../glossary/percent.md), including those requiring multiple steps or multiple operations
- understand the [divisibility rules](../glossary/divisibility-rule.md) and use them to determine whether numbers are divisible by 2, 3, 4, 5, 6, 8, 9, and 10
- use [mental math strategies](../glossary/mental-math-strategies.md) to calculate percents of whole numbers, including 1%, 5%, 10%, 15%, 25%, and 50%, and explain the strategies used
- represent and solve problems involving the addition and subtraction of whole numbers and decimal numbers, using [estimation](../glossary/estimation.md) and [algorithms](../glossary/algorithm.md)
- add and subtract fractions with like and unlike [denominators](../glossary/denominator.md), using appropriate tools, in various contexts
- represent [composite numbers](../glossary/composite-number.md) as a product of their [prime factors](../glossary/prime-factor.md), including through the use of [factor trees](../glossary/factor-tree.md)
- represent and solve problems involving the multiplication of three-digit whole numbers by decimal tenths, using algorithms
- represent and solve problems involving the division of three-digit whole numbers by decimal tenths, using appropriate tools, strategies, and algorithms, and expressing remainders as appropriate
- multiply whole numbers by [proper fractions](../glossary/proper-fraction.md), using appropriate tools and strategies
- divide whole numbers by proper fractions, using appropriate tools and strategies
- represent and solve problems involving the division of decimal numbers up to thousandths by whole numbers up to 10, using appropriate tools and strategies
- solve problems involving ratios, including percents and rates, using appropriate tools and strategies

<sub>The source curriculum adds 44 teacher prompts, examples, and instructional notes for these expectations.</sub>


*Transferable skills developed in this strand: Critical Thinking & Problem Solving, Communication.*

### C. Algebra

*By the end of Grade 6, students will:*

**C1 Patterns and Relationships.** identify, describe, extend, create, and make predictions about a variety of patterns, including those found in real-life contexts

- identify and describe [repeating](../glossary/repeating-pattern-5a556eeb.md), [growing](../glossary/growing-pattern.md), and [shrinking patterns](../glossary/shrinking-pattern-1b765839.md), including patterns found in real-life contexts, and specify which growing patterns are [linear](../glossary/linear-pattern.md)
- create and [translate](../glossary/translate-6bbeec99.md) repeating, growing, and shrinking patterns using various representations, including [tables of values](../glossary/table-of-values.md), [graphs](../glossary/graph.md), and, for linear growing patterns, [algebraic expressions](../glossary/algebraic-expression.md) and [equations](../glossary/equation.md)
- determine [pattern rules](../glossary/pattern-rule.md) and use them to extend patterns, make and justify predictions, and identify missing [elements](../glossary/element.md) in repeating, growing, and shrinking patterns, and use algebraic representations of the pattern rules to solve for unknown values in linear growing patterns
- create and describe patterns to illustrate [relationships](../glossary/relationship-f5b5e0bf.md) among whole numbers and decimal numbers

<sub>The source curriculum adds 17 teacher prompts, examples, and instructional notes for these expectations.</sub>

**C2 Equations and Inequalities.** demonstrate an understanding of variables, expressions, equalities, and inequalities, and apply this understanding in various contexts

- add [monomials](../glossary/monomial.md) with a degree of 1 that involve whole numbers, using tools
- [evaluate](../glossary/evaluate.md) algebraic expressions that involve whole numbers and decimal tenths
- solve equations that involve multiple [terms](../glossary/term-b0dc5ad8.md) and whole numbers in various contexts, and verify solutions
- solve [inequalities](../glossary/inequality.md) that involve two operations and whole numbers up to 100 and verify and graph the solutions

<sub>The source curriculum adds 16 teacher prompts, examples, and instructional notes for these expectations.</sub>

**C3 Coding.** solve problems and create computational representations of mathematical situations using coding concepts and skills

- solve problems and create [computational representations](../glossary/computational-representations.md) of mathematical situations by writing and [executing](../glossary/execute-coding.md) [efficient code](../glossary/efficient-code-coding.md), including [code](../glossary/code.md) that involves [conditional statements](../glossary/conditional-statement-coding.md) and other [control structures](../glossary/control-structure-coding.md)
- read and alter existing code, including code that involves conditional statements and other control structures, and describe how changes to the code affect the [outcomes](../glossary/outcome-coding.md) and the efficiency of the code

<sub>The source curriculum adds 10 teacher prompts, examples, and instructional notes for these expectations.</sub>

**C4 Mathematical Modelling.** apply the process of mathematical modelling to represent, analyse, make predictions, and provide insight into real-life situations 
 
 This overall expectation has no specific expectations. [Mathematical modelling](../glossary/mathematical-modelling.md) is an iterative and interconnected process that is applied to various contexts, allowing students to bring in learning from other strands. Students’ demonstration of the process of mathematical modelling, as they apply concepts and skills learned in other strands, is assessed and evaluated. 
 Read more about the mathematical modelling process.


*Transferable skills developed in this strand: Critical Thinking & Problem Solving, Communication, Digital Literacy.*

### D. Data

*By the end of Grade 6, students will:*

**D1 Data Literacy.** manage, analyse, and use data to make convincing arguments and informed decisions, in various contexts drawn from real life

- describe the difference between [discrete](../glossary/discrete-data.md) and [continuous data](../glossary/continuous-data.md), and provide examples of each
- collect [qualitative data](../glossary/qualitative-data.md) and discrete and continuous[quantitative data](../glossary/quantitative-data.md) to answer [questions of interest](../glossary/question-of-interest.md) about a [population](../glossary/population.md), and organize the sets of data as appropriate, including using [intervals](../glossary/interval-fc21a685.md)
- select from among a variety of graphs, including [histograms](../glossary/histogram.md) and [broken-line graphs](../glossary/broken-line-graph.md), the type of graph best suited to represent various sets of data; display the data in the graphs with proper sources, titles, and labels, and appropriate scales; and justify their choice of graphs
- create an [infographic](../glossary/infographic.md) about a data set, representing the data in appropriate ways, including in tables, histograms, and broken-line graphs, and incorporating any other relevant information that helps to tell a story about the data
- determine the [range](../glossary/range.md) as a measure of spread and the [measures of central tendency](../glossary/measures-of-central-tendency.md) for various data sets, and use this information to compare two or more data sets
- analyse different sets of data presented in various ways, including in histograms and broken-line graphs and in [misleading graphs](../glossary/misleading-graph.md), by asking and answering questions about the data, challenging preconceived notions, and drawing conclusions, then make convincing arguments and informed decisions

<sub>The source curriculum adds 23 teacher prompts, examples, and instructional notes for these expectations.</sub>

**D2 Probability.** describe the likelihood that events will happen, and use that information to make predictions

- use fractions, decimals, and percents to express the [probability](../glossary/probability.md) of events happening, represent this probability on a [probability line](../glossary/probability-line.md), and use it to make predictions and informed decisions
- determine and compare the [theoretical](../glossary/theoretical-probability.md) and [experimental probabilities](../glossary/experimental-probability.md) of two [independent events](../glossary/independent-events.md) happening

<sub>The source curriculum adds 7 teacher prompts, examples, and instructional notes for these expectations.</sub>


*Transferable skills developed in this strand: Critical Thinking & Problem Solving, Digital Literacy, Innovation, Creativity & Entrepreneurship, Communication.*

### E. Spatial Sense

*By the end of Grade 6, students will:*

**E1 Geometric and Spatial Reasoning.** describe and represent shape, location, and movement by applying [geometric properties](../glossary/geometric-property.md) and spatial relationships in order to navigate the world around them

- create lists of the geometric properties of various types of [quadrilaterals](../glossary/quadrilateral.md), including the properties of the [diagonals](../glossary/diagonal.md), [rotational symmetry](../glossary/rotational-symmetry.md), and [line symmetry](../glossary/line-symmetry.md)
- construct [three-dimensional objects](../glossary/three-dimensional-object-e7630a4b.md) when given their top, front, and side views
- plot and read [coordinates](../glossary/coordinates.md)in all four [quadrants](../glossary/quadrant.md) of a [Cartesian plane](../glossary/cartesian-plane.md), and describe the [translations](../glossary/translation-ab11c126.md) that move a point from one coordinate to another
- describe and perform combinations of translations, [reflections](../glossary/reflection.md), and [rotations](../glossary/rotation.md) up to 360° on a [grid](../glossary/grid.md), and predict the results of these [transformations](../glossary/transformation-c2e94529.md)

<sub>The source curriculum adds 22 teacher prompts, examples, and instructional notes for these expectations.</sub>

**E2 Measurement.** compare, estimate, and determine measurements in various contexts

- measure length, area, [mass](../glossary/mass.md), and [capacity](../glossary/capacity.md) using the appropriate [metric units](../glossary/metric-unit.md), and solve problems that require converting smaller units to larger ones and vice versa
- use a [protractor](../glossary/protractor.md) to measure and construct [angles](../glossary/angle.md) up to 360°, and state the relationship between angles that are measured [clockwise](../glossary/clockwise-cw.md) and those that are measured [counterclockwise](../glossary/counterclockwise-ccw.md)
- use the properties of [supplementary angles](../glossary/supplementary-angles.md), [complementary angles](../glossary/complementary-angles.md), [opposite angles](../glossary/opposite-angles.md), and [interior](../glossary/interior-angle.md) and [exterior angles](../glossary/exterior-angle.md) to solve for unknown angle measures
- determine the areas of [trapezoids](../glossary/trapezoid.md), [rhombuses](../glossary/rhombus.md), [kites](../glossary/kite.md), and [composite polygons](../glossary/composite-polygon.md) by [decomposing](../glossary/decompose.md) them into shapes with known areas
- create and use [nets](../glossary/net.md) to demonstrate the relationship between the [faces](../glossary/face.md) of [prisms](../glossary/prism-cc799aab.md) and [pyramids](../glossary/pyramid-bd42afae.md) and their [surface areas](../glossary/surface-area.md)
- determine the surface areas of prisms and pyramids by calculating the areas of their [two-dimensional faces](../glossary/two-dimensional-shape.md) and adding them together

<sub>The source curriculum adds 39 teacher prompts, examples, and instructional notes for these expectations.</sub>


*Transferable skills developed in this strand: Critical Thinking & Problem Solving, Communication.*

### F. Financial Literacy

*By the end of Grade 6, students will:*

**F1 Money and Finances.** demonstrate the knowledge and skills needed to make informed financial decisions

- describe the advantages and disadvantages of various [methods of payment](../glossary/method-of-payment.md) that can be used to purchase [goods and services](../glossary/goods-and-services.md)
- identify different types of [financial goals](../glossary/financial-goal.md), including [earning](../glossary/earning.md) and [saving](../glossary/saving.md) goals, and outline some key steps in achieving them
- identify and describe various factors that may help or interfere with reaching financial goals
- explain the concept of [interest rates](../glossary/interest-rate.md), and identify types of interest rates and [fees](../glossary/fees.md) associated with different accounts and loans offered by various banks and other [financial institutions](../glossary/financial-institution.md)
- describe [trading](../glossary/trade.md), [lending](../glossary/lending.md), [borrowing](../glossary/borrowing.md), and [donating](../glossary/donating.md) as different ways to distribute financial and other resources among individuals and organizations

<sub>The source curriculum adds 16 teacher prompts, examples, and instructional notes for these expectations.</sub>


*Transferable skills developed in this strand: Critical Thinking & Problem Solving, Global Citizenship and Sustainability, Innovation, Creativity & Entrepreneurship.*
