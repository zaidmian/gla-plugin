# Clothing (HNL2O)

**Clothing (HNL2O)** is a Grade 10 open course in [Social Sciences and Humanities](../subjects/social-sciences-and-humanities.md).

This course introduces students to the world of clothing. Students will gain knowledge about clothing and demonstrate basic skills associated with various techniques and technologies used to create garments and accessories. Students will learn about the functions of clothing and accessories and what clothing communicates about the wearer. They will learn how to enhance their personal wardrobe by assessing garment quality, developing shopping strategies, and developing an understanding of the advantages and disadvantages of various retail formats. Students will develop research skills as they investigate topics related to clothing.

## Before you take this course

This course has no formal prerequisites.

## What students learn

The learning is organized into 4 strands: Research and Inquiry Skills, Influences on Clothing Choices, Making Knowledgeable Decisions, and Design and Clothing Construction Skills.

### A. Research and Inquiry Skills

*Throughout this course, students will:*

**A1 Exploring.** explore topics related to clothing, and formulate questions to guide their research

- explore a variety of topics related to clothing (e.g., fabrics, [fibres](../glossary/fibre.md), dress codes, wardrobe planning, evaluating clothing, consumer awareness) to identify topics for research and inquiry
- identify key concepts (e.g., through discussion, brainstorming, use of visual organizers) related to their selected topics
- formulate effective questions to guide their research and inquiry

<sub>The source curriculum adds 1 teacher prompt, example, and instructional note for these expectations.</sub>

**A2 Investigating.** create research plans, and locate and select information relevant to their chosen topics, using appropriate social science research and inquiry methods

- create appropriate research plans to investi­gate their chosen topic (e.g., outline purpose and method; identify sources of information), ensuring that their plans follow guidelines for ethical research
- locate and select information relevant to their investigations from a variety of primary sources (e.g., interviews, observations, surveys, questionnaires, fashion spreads in magazines) and/or secondary sources (e.g., textbooks, newspaper articles)
- based on preliminary research, for each investigation formulate a research question and use it to focus their research

**A3 Processing Information.** assess, record, analyse, and synthesize information gathered through research

- assess various aspects of information gath­ered from primary and secondary sources (e.g., accuracy, relevance, reliability, inherent values and [bias](../glossary/bias-ade0ba52.md), voice)
- record and organize information and key ideas using a variety of formats (e.g., notes, graphic organizers, audio/digital records)
- analyse and interpret research information (e.g., compare observations with images obtained from magazines; determine whether common images arise in media products from different eras)
- demonstrate academic honesty by docu­menting the sources of all information generated through research
- synthesize findings and formulate conclu­sions (e.g., weigh and connect information to determine the answer to their research question)

**A4 Communicating and Reflecting.** communicate the results of their research and inquiry clearly and effectively, and reflect on and evaluate their research, inquiry, and communication skills

- use an appropriate format (e.g., oral presenta­tion, brochure, poster, report, multimedia presentation, web page) to communicate the results of their research and inquiry effectively for a specific purpose and audience
- use terms related to clothing correctly (e.g., fibres, fabrics, natural, synthetic, knit, crochet, weave, [seam](../glossary/seam.md), hem, [raw edge](../glossary/raw-edge.md), wardrobe planning)
- clearly communicate the results of their inquiries (e.g., write clearly, organize ideas logically, use language conventions properly), and follow APA conventions for acknowledging sources (e.g., generate a reference list in APA style)
- demonstrate an understanding of the general research process by reflecting on and evaluating their own research, inquiry, and communication skills

### B. Influences on Clothing Choices

*By the end of this course, students will:*

**B1 The Functions and Social Impact of Clothing.** demonstrate an understanding of the functions clothing serves, the kinds of messages it conveys, and the impact it can have on social interactions

- describe the functions of clothing and accessories and the messages they convey (e.g., to protect against the weather; to convey status, power, or occupation; to adorn; to satisfy social conventions with respect to modesty or gender roles; to signal membership in a group)
- explain the impact that clothing can have on social interactions (e.g., how clothing’s ability to contribute to unity or signal authority can affect interactions; what people’s clothing can say about their values or personality, and how this might affect their interactions with others; how the ways we judge ourselves and others based on clothing can affect our interactions)

<sub>The source curriculum adds 2 teacher prompts, examples, and instructional notes for these expectations.</sub>

**B2 Wardrobe Planning and Clothing Selection.** demonstrate an understanding of various factors affecting personal wardrobe planning and clothes selection

- explain ways in which individuals’ personal clothing decisions can be affected by change (e.g., fads and trends; changes in styles; development of new materials; changes in one’s age, status, or social role)
- describe various criteria used in wardrobe planning (e.g., price, style, storage space, availability, what peers are wearing, family rules or traditions, cultural traditions, physical needs), and explain how these criteria affect their personal clothing and accessory selections
- describe the steps and processes involved in personal wardrobe planning (e.g., using a decision-making model, evaluating current wardrobe, identifying needs and wants, determining personal resources such as money for clothing and skills to alter or repair clothing, planning for special occasions)
- describe dress codes for a variety of situations and groups (e.g., with reference to situations at school, special occasions, a school dance, a funeral, various workplaces, a wedding, family functions; with reference to groups such as athletic teams, police officers, members of the military) and the effects such codes can have on personal wardrobe planning

### C. Making Knowledgeable Decisions

*By the end of this course, students will:*

**C1 Evaluating Clothing.** demonstrate an understanding of how to evaluate the quality, value, and suitability of clothing and accessories

- explain the criteria for evaluating the quality, value, and suitability of clothing and accessories (e.g., straight and secure stitching on seams, which allows garments to hang, wear, and wash well; durable fabrics for garments and accessories that will be worn and/or washed frequently)
- assess the quality of selected garments and accessories (e.g., work clothes, outerwear, sports garments, shoes, garments for special occasions)

<sub>The source curriculum adds 2 teacher prompts, examples, and instructional notes for these expectations.</sub>

**C2 Retail Approaches.** demonstrate an understanding of the effects that retail formats and promotions can have on individuals’ decisions about clothing purchases

- identify and describe various retail formats for clothing and accessories (e.g., department stores, chain stores, boutiques, specialty shops, second-hand stores, online shopping sites, outlet stores, shopping channels on television)
- explain how various retail formats can affect the ways individuals shop for and purchase clothing and accessories (e.g., with reference to shopping with peers, accessing unique products, comparison shopping, impulse buying)
- describe different types of retail promotions (e.g., in-store displays, billboards, magazine adver­tising, clearance sales, online surveys), and explain the effect of these promotions on their decisions about clothing purchases

<sub>The source curriculum adds 2 teacher prompts, examples, and instructional notes for these expectations.</sub>

**C3 Fibres and Fabric Care.** demonstrate an understanding of natural and synthetic fibres and [fabrics](../glossary/fabric.md), and of how their characteristics affect clothing choices and care

- identify common natural and synthetic fibres (e.g., cotton, silk, linen, wool, hemp, rayon, polyester, nylon, spandex) and describe their characteristics
- explain how knowledge of natural and [synthetic fibres](../glossary/synthetic-fibre.md) and fabrics, including how they are produced, can affect clothing choices (e.g., the selection of breathable lightweight cottons and rayons for summer clothes; wool or polar fleece for winter outerwear; silk or cashmere for softness next to the skin; organic cotton or hemp to limit environmental damage arising from the production of traditionally farmed cotton or petroleum-based polyester; down or synthetic fibre for insulation in outerwear)
- describe ways, including environmentally responsible ways, to care for garments to maintain their appearance and extend their life (e.g., with respect to following clothing care labels; laundering, ironing, removing stains from, and storing garments made from different kinds of fabrics; using environmentally responsible detergents and stain-removal practices)

### D. Design and Clothing Construction Skills

*By the end of this course, students will:*

**D1 Elements of Design.** describe the [elements of design](../glossary/elements-of-design-37840bcc.md) related to clothing and accessories, and explain how an awareness of these elements can affect personal clothing choices

- identify and describe the elements of design (e.g., line, shape, colour, texture) as they apply to clothing and accessories
- explain how the elements of design can enhance or detract from a person’s appearance and comfort (e.g., vertical lines make the wearer appear taller, soft fabrics enhance comfort), and consequently which elements should be con­sidered when selecting clothing and accessories

<sub>The source curriculum adds 2 teacher prompts, examples, and instructional notes for these expectations.</sub>

**D2 Tools and Technologies.** identify a variety of tools and technologies associated with the creation of clothing and accessories, and use tools and technologies safely and correctly when creating and/or repairing clothing and accessories

- identify various tools and technologies used when creating and/or repairing clothing and accessories (e.g., measuring tape, [seam ripper](../glossary/seam-ripper.md), scissors, iron, pressing ham, sewing machine, [serger](../glossary/serger.md), loom, knitting needles, crochet hook)
- demonstrate the safe use of tools and technologies when creating and/or repairing clothing and accessories (e.g., safe practices when using sharp objects such as pins, needles, or scissors, or chemicals such as dye or glue)
- use tools and technologies correctly when creating personal clothing or accessories (e.g., tools/technologies associated with cutting, pressing, pinning, sewing, knitting, crocheting)

<sub>The source curriculum adds 2 teacher prompts, examples, and instructional notes for these expectations.</sub>

**D3 Procedures, Skills, and Techniques.** demonstrate the ability to use appropriate procedures, skills, and techniques to create and repair clothing or accessories

- demonstrate the ability to follow precon­struction procedures (e.g., measuring, interpreting pattern envelopes and patterns, laying out patterns, preparing fabric and other materials, understanding knitting or crocheting instructions) when creating clothing or accessories
- demonstrate the ability to repair and/or alter personal clothing or accessories using basic sewing skills (e.g., repair a seam, seam finish a raw edge, alter a hem, apply an appliqué, replace a button, create an elastic casing)
- demonstrate the ability to create clothing or accessories using basic construction techniques (e.g., machine and/or hand sewing, knitting, crocheting, beading)
- demonstrate the ability to create new clothing or accessories from recycled materials or gar­ments (e.g., turn drink boxes into a bag; candy wrappers into a necklace; neckties into a skirt; a necktie into a handle for a bag; jeans into a skirt or a bag; tea towels into a dress; fabric samples into a tote, cosmetic bag, laptop case, or music player pouch)

<sub>The source curriculum adds 2 teacher prompts, examples, and instructional notes for these expectations.</sub>
