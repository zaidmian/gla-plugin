# Classical Languages (Ancient Greek/Latin) (LVGDU/LVLDU)

**Classical Languages (Ancient Greek/Latin) (LVGDU/LVLDU)** is an university preparation course in [Classical Studies and International Languages](../subjects/classical-studies-and-international-languages.md).

This course provides students with opportunities to further develop their knowledge of the achievements and rich cultural legacy of the classical world through the study of Latin or ancient Greek. Students will increase their vocabulary and refine their use of grammatical concepts by reading and translating a broad selection of adapted and original classical texts, including prose and poetry. English is the language of instruction, and students will further refine their ability to use oral communication, reading, and writing skills in both English and the classical language. Students will apply research and critical thinking skills to investigate diverse aspects of classical culture, and make increasingly insightful connections between the classical world and other societies.

## Before you take this course

Students should first complete [Classical Languages (Ancient Greek/Latin) (LVGCU/LVLCU)](LVGCU-LVLCU.md).

## What students learn

The learning is organized into 4 strands: Oral Communication, Reading, Writing, and Intercultural Understanding.

### A. Oral Communication

*By the end of this course, students will:*

**A1 Listening to Understand.** determine meaning in a variety of [oral texts](../glossary/forms-of-oral-texts.md) in the classical language and oral texts in English about the classical world, using a range of [listening strategies](../glossary/listening-strategies.md)

- identify a range of listening comprehension strategies and use them before, during, and after listening to understand [adapted](../glossary/adapted-text.md) and unadapted [oral texts](../glossary/forms-of-oral-texts.md) in the classical language, including complex [texts](../glossary/text-301894af.md), and oral texts in English about the classical world (e.g., listen selectively to focus on key ideas and information; use [prior knowledge](../glossary/prior-knowledge.md) of vocabulary to predict meaning; use grammatical clues, such as noun-adjective agreement between words separated by other words in a poem, to determine the meaning of lines of poetry; make jot notes during a class discussion and review after listening to reinforce their [comprehension](../glossary/comprehension.md); prepare questions while listening to a guest speaker’s presentation; confirm and clarify comprehension during listening by referring to pre-listening activities; replay audio clips to confirm their understanding of the meaning)
- identify various elements of the classical language, including phonological and grammatical elements, and use the English terminology for those elements correctly (e.g., differentiate between prose and poetry presented orally; distinguish between active and passive [voice](../glossary/voice-6f2d6ce6.md) in sentences read aloud; identify subordinate clauses in [oral texts](../glossary/forms-of-oral-texts.md); identify the [ablative absolute](../glossary/ablative-absolute.md) in Latin or the [genitive absolute](../glossary/genitive-absolute.md) in ancient Greek in complex contexts; identify the [subjunctive](../glossary/subjunctive.md) mood while listening to passages in the classical language; classify metre, such as dactylic hexameter, phalaecean, or elegiac couplet, in poetry read aloud; define [intonation](../glossary/intonation.md), pitch, consonance, and assonance)
- demonstrate an understanding of the meaning of phrases, sentences, and [adapted](../glossary/adapted-text.md) and unadapted [oral texts](../glossary/forms-of-oral-texts.md) in the classical language, including complex [texts](../glossary/text-301894af.md), and of oral texts in English about the classical world (e.g., define the terms patronus and cliens; identify and describe political bias in the poems of Martial, citing his use of diction; identify the main message of a poem by Catullus read aloud; describe ways in which the requirements of metre affect the way a line of a poem is written, its sound, and its meaning; after viewing a documentary about an archaeological excavation, describe an aspect of the [material culture](../glossary/material-culture.md) of the classical world; after listening to a translated account of daily life in the classical world, compare an aspect of ancient society to its counterpart in contemporary society)
- (a) describe a range of strategies they found helpful before, during, and after listening; 
 (b) identify their areas of greater and lesser strength as listeners, and plan steps they can take to improve their [listening skills](../glossary/listening-and-speaking-skills.md) (e.g., plan to self-monitor progress by using a checklist to compare their performance to an earlier one or to a personal goal; plan to listen for specific information in an [oral text](../glossary/forms-of-oral-texts.md) or presentation; use a personal journal to reflect on areas for improvement and ways to listen to prose and poetic texts more effectively; assess strategies suggested by the teacher and peers, and identify those that are most effective for listening to lectures)

<sub>The source curriculum adds 3 teacher prompts, examples, and instructional notes for these expectations.</sub>

**A2 Speaking to Communicate.** communicate information and ideas orally in the classical language and in English, using a range of [speaking strategies](../glossary/speaking-strategies.md) and level-appropriate language suited to the purpose and audience (see the [Language Knowledge](../glossary/language-knowledge.md) chart for Latin or ancient Greek in Appendix A)

- identify a range of [speaking strategies](../glossary/speaking-strategies.md) and use them to express themselves clearly and coherently in the classical language and in English for various purposes and to a variety of audiences (e.g., use visual aids when presenting information about [material culture](../glossary/material-culture.md) being studied; formulate questions to guide participation in a class discussion)
- use level-appropriate vocabulary and grammatical constructions in a variety of oral language activities in the classical language, and speak with [fluency](../glossary/fluency.md) and expression using [standard pronunciation](../glossary/standard-pronunciation.md) (e.g., compose phrases and short sentences and deliver them orally; read a poem or an excerpt from a poem aloud with attention to metre, [intonation](../glossary/intonation.md), and modulation)
- use appropriate diction and correct grammar in a variety of oral language activities in English (e.g., critique a poem using correct terminology; defend their interpretation of a classical speech or poem, citing evidence from the [text](../glossary/text-301894af.md); dramatize the events depicted in a short classical text, using appropriate language; deliver a reading of a poem translated from the classical language or a poem with a classical theme, using appropriate [intonation](../glossary/intonation.md) and modulation; incorporate into an oral presentation English words that are derived from the classical language; deliver a clear presentation, applying knowledge of logic gained from studying the grammar of the classical language)
- demonstrate knowledge of vocabulary and grammar by orally translating [adapted](../glossary/adapted-text.md) and unadapted texts, including complex [texts](../glossary/text-301894af.md), from the classical language into English, with previous preparation and at sight
- (a) describe a range of strategies they found helpful before, during, and after speaking; 
 (b) identify their areas of greater and lesser strength as speakers, and plan steps they can take to improve their [speaking skills](../glossary/listening-and-speaking-skills.md) (e.g., express preferences about [speaking strategies](../glossary/speaking-strategies.md); identify which aspects of feedback received from a peer were most helpful in improving a presentation; plan to use [body language](../glossary/body-language.md) and [tone](../glossary/tone-de007b95.md) of voice to help communicate the meaning of a poem)

### B. Reading

*By the end of this course, students will:*

**B1 Reading Comprehension.** determine meaning in a variety of texts about the classical world, in the classical language and in English, using a range of [reading strategies](../glossary/reading-strategies.md)

- identify a range of reading [comprehension strategies](../glossary/comprehension-strategies-bc1906c9.md) and use them before, during, and after reading to understand [adapted](../glossary/adapted-text.md) and unadapted texts, including complex [texts](../glossary/text-301894af.md), in the classical language and texts in English about the classical world (e.g., scan text prior to reading to make inferences about its intent; skim text to identify unfamiliar words and grammatical elements; skim text for specific cultural information; use titles, headings, footnotes, commentary, and/or marginal notes to extract information; reread to find information that was overlooked on first reading; use cultural references to shed light on the meaning of a classical text; make connections between a classical text and the modern world; use [apparatus critici](../glossary/apparatus-criticus.md) and commentaries to help interpret a text)
- identify various elements of the classical language, including grammatical constructions and relationships (e.g., the passive [voice](../glossary/voice-6f2d6ce6.md), subordinate clauses requiring the [subjunctive](../glossary/subjunctive.md)/[optative](../glossary/optative.md) mood, the [ablative absolute](../glossary/ablative-absolute.md) in Latin or the [genitive absolute](../glossary/genitive-absolute.md) in ancient Greek used with different participles, poetic word order), stylistic devices (e.g., anacoluthon, asyndeton, ellipsis, prolepsis, chiasmus, litotes, [hyperbole](../glossary/hyperbole.md), juxtaposition, the omission of words previously used within a passage), and modern conventions of the writing system (e.g., differences in the use of punctuation in prose and in poetry in modern printing), and demonstrate an understanding of their use
- demonstrate an understanding of a variety of [adapted](../glossary/adapted-text.md) and unadapted [texts](../glossary/text-301894af.md), including complex texts, in the classical language (e.g., prose selections of Tacitus, [epigrams](../glossary/epigram.md) of Martial, [lyric poems](../glossary/lyric-poetry.md) of Catullus, excerpts from Plato), using their [knowledge](../glossary/language-knowledge.md) of the classical language and information about the classical world (e.g., translate a poem or an epigram of Catullus, drawing on their knowledge of Roman social conventions; interpret brief passages from Plato’s Apology, drawing on their knowledge of Athens in the late fifth and early fourth centuries BCE)
- use a range of vocabulary-acquisition strategies before, during, and after reading texts in the classical language to determine the meaning of new words, and make connections to English words derived from the classical language (e.g., identify possible meanings of new words, using contextual cues, and consult a Latin–English/English–Latin or an ancient Greek–English/English–ancient Greek dictionary to confirm or correct their deductions; use their knowledge of English [derivatives](../glossary/derivative.md) to deduce the meaning of new words; identify and research the meaning of [colloquialisms](../glossary/colloquialism.md) and [idiomatic expressions](../glossary/idiomatic-expression.md) to create a personal dictionary; find familiar words used in new contexts and explain their meaning in relation to those contexts; use a [graphic organizer](../glossary/graphic-organizer.md) of their choice to record English derivatives and their roots in the classical language)

<sub>The source curriculum adds 4 teacher prompts, examples, and instructional notes for these expectations.</sub>

**B2 Awareness of Cultural Context.** demonstrate an awareness of the cultural context of the classical language, using information from a variety of [media](../glossary/media.md) in both the classical language and English

- make accurate inferences about the classical world, using information from a variety of sources and [media](../glossary/media.md) (e.g., inscriptions in the classical language; short excerpts from Cicero, Ovid, Homer, Herodotus; archaeological research papers; political histories in English; English translations of epic poems such as The Aeneid, The Iliad, The Odyssey; ancient art forms such as friezes, monuments; cultural resource materials such as art books, digital archives; resources about garden archaeology and the sacred grove)
- (a) describe a range of strategies they found helpful before, during, and after reading to understand [texts](../glossary/text-301894af.md) in both the classical language and English; 
 (b) identify their areas of greater and lesser strength as readers, and plan steps they can take to improve their reading skills (e.g., plan to record the steps they take as they translate texts and to reflect on the efficiency of their reading process; set targets to increase their use of helpful strategies, such as participation in circle discussions for interpreting poetry; keep a reading log to determine their reading trends, and set goals to expand the variety of texts they read)

### C. Writing

*By the end of this course, students will:*

**C1 Translating from the Classical Language into English and Composing in the Classical Language.** translate [texts](../glossary/text-301894af.md) from the classical language into English and write texts in the classical language, using appropriate vocabulary and [language structures](../glossary/language-structures.md) and correct grammar (see the [Language Knowledge](../glossary/language-knowledge.md) chart for Latin or ancient Greek in Appendix A)

- write accurate English translations, correctly applying knowledge of vocabulary and grammar, of [adapted](../glossary/adapted-text.md) and unadapted texts in the classical language (e.g., write a translation of a poem; translate a [sight passage](../glossary/sight-passage.md) of prose or poetry; translate complex sentences, making use of grammar notes and charts and explanations of grammatical constructions in [print and electronic resources](../glossary/print-and-electronic-re-sources.md); translate a complex passage, making use of the [apparatus criticus](../glossary/apparatus-criticus.md) provided with the passage, which shows variant readings)
- compose phrases, clauses, and sentences of varying lengths and complexity in the classical language, using a range of strategies and resources to ensure suitable word choice and correct word order and grammar (e.g., identify appropriate word order, verify the correct form for verb tenses in the active and passive [voice](../glossary/voice-6f2d6ce6.md), and select the correct [cases](../glossary/case.md) for nouns and adjectives, using [print and electronic resources](../glossary/print-and-electronic-re-sources.md); correctly select either the [ablative absolute](../glossary/ablative-absolute.md) \[Latin\]/[genitive absolute](../glossary/genitive-absolute.md) \[Greek\] or a temporal clause when composing sentences)

<sub>The source curriculum adds 2 teacher prompts, examples, and instructional notes for these expectations.</sub>

**C2 Writing in English.** write a variety of English [texts](../glossary/text-301894af.md) related to the classical language, using appropriate vocabulary and [language structures](../glossary/language-structures.md) and correctly applying knowledge of spelling, grammar, and punctuation conventions

- write a variety of English [texts](../glossary/text-301894af.md) in response to passages in the classical language and to related cultural concepts (e.g., a journal entry about the propaganda contained in Vergil’s Aeneid; a letter to Martial about one of his [epigrams](../glossary/epigram.md); a letter to Thucydides about his account of his actions at the fall of Amphipolis; a research report about the influence of a particular aspect of Roman culture on other cultures)
- write in English to demonstrate an understanding of unadapted [texts](../glossary/text-301894af.md) of varying levels of complexity in the classical language (e.g., write a [paraphrase](../glossary/paraphrase.md) or summary of a text being read in class; write detailed and well-developed answers to comprehension questions about texts being studied; write a report identifying ideas that are implicit but not directly stated in a text being studied and explaining their reasoning)
- correctly apply knowledge of vocabulary, [language structures](../glossary/language-structures.md), and conventions of spelling, grammar, and punctuation when writing in English

<sub>The source curriculum adds 1 teacher prompt, example, and instructional note for these expectations.</sub>

**C3 Using the Writing Process.** use the stages of the [writing process](../glossary/writing-process.md) – pre-writing, producing drafts, [revising](../glossary/revising.md), [editing](../glossary/editing.md), and publishing – to develop and organize content, clarify ideas and expression, correct errors, and present their work effectively

- generate, develop, focus, and organize ideas for writing in both English and the classical language, using a range of appropriate pre-writing strategies and resources, during [modelled](../glossary/modelled-activity.md), [shared](../glossary/shared-activity.md), [guided](../glossary/guided-activity.md), and independent writing activities (e.g., activate [prior knowledge](../glossary/prior-knowledge.md) about the classical language and culture through peer and group interactions; use rapid writing to capture ideas about classical culture presented in class; consult a historical map to prepare for writing directions for travel from one ancient Mediterranean city or settlement to another; record ideas by grouping them under headings; prioritize ideas for [research](../glossary/research.md) using a ranking system; use a point-form outline to organize content)
- plan, produce, and revise drafts, using a variety of strategies and resources and choosing forms and [stylistic elements](../glossary/stylistic-elements.md) suited to the purpose and audience (e.g., write a persuasive speech, using a passage from a classical text as a model; review their drafts to ensure that the ideas are presented in a logical order and with clear transitions; annotate their drafts using sticky notes and highlighters to record ideas about revision; create a list of questions to help guide revision; refer to feedback from the teacher and peers to identify sections of their draft that need clarifying details and/or more varied sentence structure and vocabulary)
- produce a polished final product, using a variety of strategies and resources to improve correctness and [coherence](../glossary/coherence-69cf30fb.md) and to enhance the readability and visual appeal of their work (e.g., verify that vocabulary is appropriate and that spelling and punctuation are correct, using [print and electronic resources](../glossary/print-and-electronic-re-sources.md); review the organization of their text to ensure that it meets the requirements of the form; use design elements such as graphics, fonts, and an appealing layout to highlight key information and enhance the readability of their work; include quotations to support ideas presented, and cite their sources using a consistent and approved citation method; apply a self-assessment method to check their final draft against the criteria for success)
- (a) describe a range of strategies they found helpful before, during, and after writing; 
 (b) identify their areas of greater and lesser strength as writers, and plan steps they can take to improve their [writing skills](../glossary/writing-skills.md) in both the classical language and English (e.g., identify strategies, such as the use of [graphic organizers](../glossary/graphic-organizer.md), that help them develop and structure ideas generated during brainstorming; describe the types of revisions to their writing that peer and teacher feedback helped them identify as necessary, and select self-correcting techniques to address those language areas in future drafts; plan to keep a log of pre-writing and writing activities to guide later revisions; plan to apply a strategy for conducting research and to monitor its effectiveness throughout the production of their text)

### D. Intercultural Understanding

*By the end of this course, students will:*

**D1 Understanding the Influence of Classical Languages.** demonstrate an understanding of the influence of classical languages on the languages of other cultures

- identify a wide variety of words, phrases, and abbreviations that are derived from the classical language and found in English and other languages, and use them accurately in appropriate contexts (e.g., define and explain the classical origin of: literary terms such as [metaphor](../glossary/metaphor.md), [simile](../glossary/simile.md), [hyperbole](../glossary/hyperbole.md); philosophical terms such as [epistemology](../glossary/epistemology.md), syllogism; educational terms such as [rhetoric](../glossary/rhetoric.md), docent; terms used in [drama](../glossary/drama.md) such as scene, [chorus](../glossary/chorus-4e8c06cd.md), orchestra; entertainment terms such as circus, amphitheatre)
- use knowledge of a wide variety of [language structures](../glossary/language-structures.md) that are common to the classical language, English, and other languages to enhance their speaking, reading, and [writing skills](../glossary/writing-skills.md) in both English and the classical language (e.g., the use of subordinate clauses in specific contexts; the formation and use of participial phrases)

**D2 Making Cultural Connections.** demonstrate an understanding of classical [culture](../glossary/culture-67ca9104.md) and its influence on other cultures

- demonstrate knowledge and understanding of diverse aspects of life in the classical world, using a variety of strategies (e.g., enter the dates of important Roman religious festivals in a digital calendar, using the original Roman dating system; create an interpretive dance that re-enacts Apollo’s pursuit of Daphne or Pan’s of Syrinx; outline classical ecological practices associated with aqueducts and other water systems, using drawings or a model; evaluate the consequences of warfare for the ecology of the ancient Mediterranean; create a map showing the influence of the Persian Wars on urban planning in ancient Greece; describe attitudes to and effects of multiculturalism in the Roman world; summarize the biographies of some women in antiquity who played significant roles in the events of their times; evaluate the effects of social divisions in the classical world; describe the origins, purpose, and achievements of the Delian League by writing a letter to Pericles complaining about his diversion of members’ dues to finance his Athenian building program; summarize in a research essay the usefulness of Trajan’s Column as a source of information about the impact of Roman conquest on the indigenous Dacians)
- relate aspects of classical culture (e.g., art, architecture, philosophy, social customs, political ideas, religious practices, technology, law, literature) to comparable aspects of other societies and cultures (e.g., create a poster identifying similarities and differences between classical law codes and modern law codes; outline in a research [essay](../glossary/essay.md) the influence of the [Vestal Virgins](../glossary/vestal-virgin.md) on the development of the Roman Catholic convent; lead a seminar analysing Roman urban planning policies during the Empire and evaluating their effectiveness in light of modern environmental standards; outline in a research essay the influence of Greek political philosophy on contemporary Western political ideas and institutions; deliver a presentation illustrating the influence of classical art on the art of later societies; identify examples of modern architecture that show the influence of classical architecture; compare and contrast Cormac McCarthy’s The Road and Vergil’s Aeneid; identify ways in which Homer’s Iliad and Odyssey and Vergil’s Aeneid influenced Dante’s description of the Inferno in his Divine Comedy)
- describe a variety of ways in which their knowledge of the classical world relates to and/or enhances their understanding of other cultures, fields of study, and personal experiences (e.g., explain how [knowledge](../glossary/language-knowledge.md) of the classical language enriches their use of the English language and other languages; explain how knowledge of the growth and decline of the Athenian and Roman empires contributes to their understanding of modern economic, military, or social history; describe how knowledge of classical history and art contributes to their understanding and appreciation of museum or gallery exhibits; use their knowledge of the myth of Pyramus and Thisbe to evaluate the impact of Ovid’s account on Shakespeare’s interpretation in A Midsummer Night’s Dream)
