# Mathematics (Grade 5)

**Mathematics** is the Grade 5 Mathematics program in [Mathematics](../subjects/mathematics.md).

Here you will find the expectations and learning for the six areas of study for Grade 5 Mathematics.

## Before you take this course

This course has no formal prerequisites.

## What students learn

The learning is organized into 6 strands: Social-Emotional Learning (SEL) Skills in Mathematics and the Mathematical Processes, Number, Algebra, Data, Spatial Sense, and Financial Literacy.

### A. Social-Emotional Learning (SEL) Skills in Mathematics and the Mathematical Processes

*Throughout this grade, in order to promote a positive identity as a math learner, to foster well-being and the ability to learn, build resilience, and thrive, students will:*

**A1 Social-Emotional Learning (SEL) Skills and the Mathematical Processes.** apply, to the best of their ability, a variety of social-emotional learning skills to support their use of the mathematical processes and their learning in connection with the expectations in the other five strands of the mathematics curriculum


### B. Number

*By the end of Grade 5, students will:*

**B1 Number Sense.** demonstrate an understanding of numbers and make connections to the way numbers are used in everyday life

- read, [represent](../glossary/represent.md), [compose](../glossary/composition-of-numbers.md), and [decompose](../glossary/decomposition-of-numbers.md) [whole numbers](../glossary/whole-number.md) up to and including 100 000, using appropriate [tools](../glossary/tools-9e67a3c2.md) and strategies, and describe various ways they are used in everyday life
- compare and order whole numbers up to and including 100 000, in various contexts
- represent [equivalent fractions](../glossary/equivalent-fractions.md) from halves to twelfths, including [improper fractions](../glossary/improper-fraction.md) and [mixed numbers](../glossary/mixed-number.md), using appropriate tools, in various contexts
- compare and order fractions from halves to twelfths, including improper fractions and mixed numbers, in various contexts
- read, represent, compare, and order [decimal numbers](../glossary/decimal-number.md) up to hundredths, in various contexts
- [round](../glossary/round-e1d768f1.md) decimal numbers to the nearest tenth, in various contexts
- describe [relationships](../glossary/relationship-f5b5e0bf.md) and show equivalences among fractions, decimal numbers up to hundredths, and whole number [percents](../glossary/percent.md), using appropriate tools and drawings, in various contexts

<sub>The source curriculum adds 30 teacher prompts, examples, and instructional notes for these expectations.</sub>

**B2 Operations.** use knowledge of numbers and operations to solve mathematical problems encountered in everyday life

- use the properties of operations, and the relationships between operations, to solve problems involving whole numbers and decimal numbers, including those requiring more than one operation, and check calculations
- recall and demonstrate multiplication facts from 0 × 0 to 12 × 12, and related division facts
- use [mental math strategies](../glossary/mental-math-strategies.md) to multiply whole numbers by 0.1 and 0.01 and [estimate](../glossary/estimate.md) sums and differences of decimal numbers up to hundredths, and explain the strategies used
- represent and solve problems involving the addition and subtraction of whole numbers that add up to no more than 100 000, and of decimal numbers up to hundredths, using appropriate tools, strategies, and algorithms
- add and subtract fractions with like [denominators](../glossary/denominator.md), in various contexts
- represent and solve problems involving the multiplication of two-digit whole numbers by two-digit whole numbers using the [area model](../glossary/area-model.md) and using [algorithms](../glossary/algorithm.md), and make connections between the two methods
- represent and solve problems involving the division of three-digit whole numbers by two-digit whole numbers using the area model and using algorithms, and make connections between the two methods, while expressing any remainder appropriately
- multiply and divide one-digit whole numbers by [unit fractions](../glossary/unit-fraction-32c07bc4.md), using appropriate tools and drawings
- represent and create equivalent [ratios](../glossary/ratio.md) and [rates](../glossary/rate.md), using a variety of tools and models, in various contexts

<sub>The source curriculum adds 36 teacher prompts, examples, and instructional notes for these expectations.</sub>


*Transferable skills developed in this strand: Critical Thinking & Problem Solving, Communication.*

### C. Algebra

*By the end of Grade 5, students will:*

**C1 Patterns and Relationships.** identify, describe, extend, create, and make predictions about a variety of patterns, including those found in real-life contexts

- identify and describe [repeating](../glossary/repeating-pattern-5a556eeb.md), [growing](../glossary/growing-pattern.md), and [shrinking patterns](../glossary/shrinking-pattern-1b765839.md), including patterns found in real-life contexts
- create and [translate](../glossary/translate-6bbeec99.md) growing and shrinking patterns using various [representations](../glossary/representation.md), including [tables of values](../glossary/table-of-values.md) and [graphs](../glossary/graph.md)
- determine [pattern rules](../glossary/pattern-rule.md) and use them to extend patterns, make and justify predictions, and identify missing [elements](../glossary/element.md) in repeating, growing, and shrinking patterns
- create and describe patterns to illustrate [relationships](../glossary/relationship-f5b5e0bf.md) among whole numbers and decimal tenths and hundredths

<sub>The source curriculum adds 20 teacher prompts, examples, and instructional notes for these expectations.</sub>

**C2 Equations and Inequalities.** demonstrate an understanding of variables, expressions, equalities, and inequalities, and apply this understanding in various contexts

- translate among words, [algebraic expressions](../glossary/algebraic-expression.md), and visual representations that describe [equivalent](../glossary/equivalence.md) relationships
- [evaluate](../glossary/evaluate.md) algebraic expressions that involve whole numbers
- solve [equations](../glossary/equation.md) that involve whole numbers up to 100 in various contexts, and verify solutions
- solve [inequalities](../glossary/inequality.md) that involve one operation and whole numbers up to 50, and verify and graph the solutions

<sub>The source curriculum adds 19 teacher prompts, examples, and instructional notes for these expectations.</sub>

**C3 Coding.** solve problems and create computational representations of mathematical situations using coding concepts and skills

- solve problems and create [computational representations](../glossary/computational-representations.md) of mathematical situations by writing and [executing code](../glossary/execute-coding.md), including [code](../glossary/code.md) that involves [conditional statements](../glossary/conditional-statement-coding.md) and other [control structures](../glossary/control-structure-coding.md)
- read and alter existing code, including code that involves conditional statements and other control structures, and describe how changes to the code affect the [outcomes](../glossary/outcome-coding.md)

<sub>The source curriculum adds 7 teacher prompts, examples, and instructional notes for these expectations.</sub>

**C4 Mathematical Modelling.** apply the process of mathematical modelling to represent, analyse, make predictions, and provide insight into real-life situations 
 
 This overall expectation has no specific expectations. [Mathematical modelling](../glossary/mathematical-modelling.md) is an iterative and interconnected process that is applied to various contexts, allowing students to bring in learning from other strands. Students’ demonstration of the process of mathematical modelling, as they apply concepts and skills learned in other strands, is assessed and evaluated. 
 Read more about the mathematical modelling process.


*Transferable skills developed in this strand: Critical Thinking & Problem Solving, Communication, Digital Literacy.*

### D. Data

*By the end of Grade 5, students will:*

**D1 Data Literacy.** manage, analyse, and use data to make convincing arguments and informed decisions, in various contexts drawn from real life

- explain the importance of various sampling techniques for collecting a sample of data that is representative of a [population](../glossary/population.md)
- collect data, using appropriate sampling techniques as needed, to answer [questions of interest](../glossary/question-of-interest.md) about a population, and organize the data in [relative-frequency tables](../glossary/relative-frequency-table.md)
- select from among a variety of graphs, including [stacked-bar graphs](../glossary/stacked-bar-graph.md), the type of graph best suited to represent various sets of data; display the data in the graphs with proper sources, titles, and labels, and appropriate scales; and justify their choice of graphs
- create an[infographic](../glossary/infographic.md) about a data set, representing the data in appropriate ways, including in relative-frequency tables and stacked-bar graphs, and incorporating any other relevant information that helps to tell a story about the data
- determine the [mean](../glossary/mean.md) and the [median](../glossary/median.md) and identify the [mode](../glossary/mode.md)(s), if any, for various data sets involving whole numbers and decimal numbers, and explain what each of these measures indicates about the data
- analyse different sets of data presented in various ways, including in stacked-bar graphs and in [misleading graphs](../glossary/misleading-graph.md), by asking and answering questions about the data, challenging preconceived notions, and drawing conclusions, then make convincing arguments and informed decisions

<sub>The source curriculum adds 27 teacher prompts, examples, and instructional notes for these expectations.</sub>

**D2 Probability.** describe the likelihood that events will happen, and use that information to make predictions

- use fractions to express the [probability](../glossary/probability.md) of events happening, represent this probability on a [probability line](../glossary/probability-line.md), and use it to make predictions and informed decisions
- determine and compare the [theoretical](../glossary/theoretical-probability.md) and [experimental probabilities](../glossary/experimental-probability.md) of an event happening

<sub>The source curriculum adds 6 teacher prompts, examples, and instructional notes for these expectations.</sub>


*Transferable skills developed in this strand: Critical Thinking & Problem Solving, Digital Literacy, Innovation, Creativity & Entrepreneurship, Communication.*

### E. Spatial Sense

*By the end of Grade 5, students will:*

**E1 Geometric and Spatial Reasoning.** describe and represent shape, location, and movement by applying [geometric properties](../glossary/geometric-property.md) and spatial relationships in order to navigate the world around them

- identify geometric properties of [triangles](../glossary/triangle.md), and construct different types of triangles when given side or [angle](../glossary/angle.md) measurements
- identify and construct [congruent](../glossary/congruent.md) triangles, [rectangles](../glossary/rectangle.md), and [parallelograms](../glossary/parallelogram.md)
- draw top, front, and side views of objects, and match drawings with objects
- plot and read [coordinates](../glossary/coordinates.md) in the [first quadrant of a Cartesian plane](../glossary/first-quadrant-of-a-cartesian-plane.md) using various scales, and describe the [translations](../glossary/translation-ab11c126.md) that move a point from one coordinate to another
- describe and perform translations, [reflections](../glossary/reflection.md), and [rotations](../glossary/rotation.md) up to 180° on a [grid](../glossary/grid.md), and predict the results of these [transformations](../glossary/transformation-c2e94529.md)

<sub>The source curriculum adds 26 teacher prompts, examples, and instructional notes for these expectations.</sub>

**E2 Measurement.** compare, estimate, and determine measurements in various contexts

- use appropriate [metric units](../glossary/metric-unit.md) to estimate and measure length, area, [mass](../glossary/mass.md), and [capacity](../glossary/capacity.md)
- solve problems that involve converting larger metric units into smaller ones, and describe the base ten relationships among metric units
- compare [angles](../glossary/angle.md) and determine their [relative size](../glossary/relative-size.md) by matching them and by measuring them using appropriate [non-standard units](../glossary/non-standard-units.md)
- explain how [protractors](../glossary/protractor.md) work, use them to measure and construct angles up to 180°, and use [benchmark](../glossary/benchmark.md) angles to estimate the size of other angles
- use the area relationships among rectangles, parallelograms, and triangles to develop the [formulas](../glossary/formula.md) for the area of a parallelogram and the area of a triangle, and solve related problems
- show that [two-dimensional shapes](../glossary/two-dimensional-shape.md) with the same area can have different [perimeters](../glossary/perimeter.md), and solve related problems

<sub>The source curriculum adds 37 teacher prompts, examples, and instructional notes for these expectations.</sub>


*Transferable skills developed in this strand: Critical Thinking & Problem Solving, Communication.*

### F. Financial Literacy

*By the end of Grade 5, students will:*

**F1 Money and Finances.** demonstrate the knowledge and skills needed to make informed financial decisions

- describe several ways money can be transferred among individuals, organizations, and businesses
- estimate and calculate the cost of [transactions](../glossary/transaction.md)involving multiple items priced in dollars and cents, including sales tax, using various strategies
- design sample basic [budgets](../glossary/budget.md) to manage [finances](../glossary/finances.md) for various [earning](../glossary/earning.md) and [spending](../glossary/spending.md) scenarios
- explain the concepts of [credit](../glossary/credit.md) and [debt](../glossary/debt.md), and describe how [financial decisions](../glossary/financial-decision-making.md) may be impacted by each
- calculate [unit rates](../glossary/unit-rate.md) for various [goods and services](../glossary/goods-and-services.md), and identify which rates offer the best value
- describe the types of [taxes](../glossary/tax.md) that are collected by the different levels of government in Canada, and explain how [tax revenue](../glossary/tax-revenue.md) is used to provide services in the community

<sub>The source curriculum adds 21 teacher prompts, examples, and instructional notes for these expectations.</sub>


*Transferable skills developed in this strand: Critical Thinking & Problem Solving, Global Citizenship and Sustainability, Innovation, Creativity & Entrepreneurship.*
